1 · Diagnosis — beginner nodes always shown; toggle difficulty above to add more

Working through classification, workup sequencing, and the microcytic / normocytic / macrocytic split.

Decision point 1 of 5
Diagnosis · Node 1Beginner

Framing a new anemia

A 34-year-old woman presents with fatigue. Hb 9.8 g/dL, MCV 68 fL, RDW 18%. No overt GI bleeding reported. Ferritin pending.

Learner selects
Order iron studies and take a focused bleeding/diet history
Attending
Correct: a microcytic, high-RDW anemia in a woman of reproductive age should prompt iron studies plus a focused bleeding/diet history — not one isolated test.
Diagnosis · Node 2Beginner

Reading the ferritin with inflammation present

Ferritin returns at 45 ng/mL. CRP is mildly elevated at 12 mg/L. History reveals heavy menstrual bleeding for 6 months.

Learner selects
Treat as iron deficiency despite the "normal" ferritin
Attending
Right call — with inflammation, the ferritin threshold for excluding iron deficiency rises (~70–100 ng/mL). A ferritin of 45 with a clear bleeding source is consistent with IDA despite being nominally "normal."
Diagnosis · Node 3Intermediate

Macrocytic anemia — B12 vs folate

62-year-old man, Hb 8.9 g/dL, MCV 112 fL. Mild peripheral paresthesias noted on exam. B12 and folate levels both pending, expected in 48 hours.

Learner selects
Start empiric B12 replacement now; hold folate until B12 deficiency is excluded
Attending
Correct — with neurologic signs present, start empiric B12 (don't wait), and specifically avoid folate alone until B12 deficiency is excluded: folate can normalize the blood count while neurologic damage silently progresses.
Diagnosis · Node 4Intermediate

Suspected hemolysis

28-year-old man, Hb 8.2 g/dL, elevated LDH, low haptoglobin, indirect hyperbilirubinemia, reticulocytosis.

Learner selects
Obtain a direct antiglobulin test (Coombs) now
Attending
Correct — a direct antiglobulin (Coombs) test should be obtained early alongside LDH, haptoglobin, and bilirubin, since it is what separates immune from non-immune hemolysis and directs the entire downstream workup.
Diagnosis · Node 5Advanced

Unexplained IDA in a postmenopausal woman

67-year-old woman, incidental IDA on routine labs. No menstrual bleeding (postmenopausal), no overt GI symptoms, stool occult blood not yet checked.

Learner selects
Pursue bidirectional (upper and lower) GI endoscopy
Attending
Correct — unexplained IDA in this population carries meaningfully elevated pretest probability of occult GI malignancy; bidirectional endoscopy finds a source in roughly one-third to half of such adults, with yield rising with age.

2 · Therapy / Intervention

Choosing between oral and IV iron, transfusion thresholds, ESAs, and disease-specific therapy.

Decision point 1 of 4
Therapy · Node 1Beginner

First-line therapy for straightforward IDA

Diagnosis: iron deficiency anemia secondary to menorrhagia. No GI symptoms, tolerates oral intake.

Learner selects
Start oral iron (alternate-day dosing) and refer for gynecologic evaluation
Attending
Correct — oral iron (alternate-day dosing improves absorption and tolerability) plus gynecologic referral for the menorrhagia addresses both the deficiency and its cause.
Therapy · Node 2Intermediate

Anemia of CKD — how far to push the hemoglobin?

CKD stage 4 patient, Hb 9.1 g/dL, symptomatic fatigue. Considering an erythropoiesis-stimulating agent (ESA).

Learner selects
Start ESA with a conservative target (~10–11.5 g/dL)
Attending
Correct — ESAs raise hemoglobin but do not improve mortality or cardiovascular outcomes in CKD, and conservative targets are recommended to avoid stroke/thromboembolic risk seen at higher targets.
Therapy · Node 3Intermediate

Transfusion threshold in a stable ICU patient

Hemodynamically stable ICU patient, Hb 7.8 g/dL, no active bleeding, no cardiac ischemia.

Learner selects
Hold transfusion; use a restrictive threshold (transfuse if Hb <7 g/dL)
Attending
Correct — a restrictive strategy is non-inferior to liberal strategies in most critically ill/stable cardiac patients, with less exposure and no mortality increase.
Therapy · Node 4Advanced

Iron-deficient HFrEF, not anemic

Patient with HFrEF (EF 30%), Hb 12.8 g/dL (not anemic by standard cutoffs), ferritin 90 ng/mL with transferrin saturation 15%, meeting criteria for iron deficiency.

Learner selects
Start IV iron (e.g. ferric carboxymaltose) despite the near-normal hemoglobin
Attending
Correct — IV ferric carboxymaltose improves functional status and reduces HF hospitalizations in iron-deficient HFrEF regardless of anemia status, though mortality benefit remains unproven.

3 · Prognosis

What anemia signals about outcomes, independent of correcting the number itself.

Decision point 1 of 2
Prognosis · Node 1Beginner

Mild anemia in an elderly patient, cause unclear

82-year-old with mild normocytic anemia (Hb 10.9 g/dL). Renal function, iron studies, B12/folate, and inflammatory markers are all unremarkable.

Learner selects
Note the association with increased mortality/frailty and monitor closely
Attending
Correct — unexplained anemia in older adults is independently associated with increased mortality and frailty, even at mild severity, once nutritional, renal, and inflammatory causes are excluded.
Prognosis · Node 2Advanced

Anemia at a new cancer diagnosis

Newly diagnosed solid-tumor patient found to be anemic at staging workup.

Learner selects
Recognize it as a marker of worse overall survival, largely reflecting disease burden
Attending
Correct — anemia at diagnosis is associated with worse overall survival across most solid tumors, though this partly reflects disease burden rather than pure causality.

4 · Screening & Harm

When to look for anemia proactively, and iatrogenic or drug-related contributors.

Decision point 1 of 2
Screening · Node 1Beginner

Screening in pregnancy

Antenatal clinic setting up a screening protocol for anemia across a general obstetric population.

Learner selects
Screen universally, at least once per trimester
Attending
Correct — universal screening is standard given high prevalence and low cost/harm, and is favored over risk-based approaches, which miss asymptomatic cases.
Screening · Node 2Intermediate

Chronic PPI use and iron/B12 deficiency

Patient on long-term PPI therapy for reflux presents with new mild anemia.

Learner selects
Consider PPI use as a plausible, reversible contributor and check iron/B12 status
Attending
Correct — long-term PPI use modestly increases risk of both iron and B12 deficiency anemia, due to reduced acid-dependent absorption, with risk rising with duration.

5 · Clinical Algorithm — Approach to Anemia

A morphology-first pathway from initial CBC/indices to a working diagnosis. For orientation only — always confirm against current guidelines and full clinical context.

Anemia confirmed on CBC (Hb below age/sex reference range)
Check MCV — microcytic, normocytic, or macrocytic?
Microcytic (MCV <80)
Ferritin low or RDW high?
Iron deficiency anemia → find the bleeding/absorption source
RDW normal + ferritin normal?
Consider thalassemia trait → Hb electrophoresis
Normocytic (MCV 80–100)
Reticulocyte count elevated?
Yes → hemolysis or blood loss → Coombs, LDH, haptoglobin, bilirubin
No → check renal function, inflammatory markers → CKD / ACD / early marrow process
Macrocytic (MCV >100)
B12 / folate low, or marrow/liver/thyroid cause?
Megaloblastic (B12/folate deficiency) → correct B12 before/with folate
Non-megaloblastic → consider liver disease, hypothyroidism, myelodysplasia, alcohol
Unexplained or discordant picture at any branch → peripheral smear review ± bone marrow biopsy
