## 🔭 GUIDED DISCOVERY — Full Framework

**Core Concept**
- Guided Discovery as distinct from Socratic teaching — learner as problem-solver, educator as architect of experience
- Scaffolded environment where the answer isn't given but the parameters make the correct conclusion reachable
- Struggle as the point (productive failure / desirable difficulty)
- Stage 4 on the pedagogical spectrum (beyond Socratic, before independent research)
- "Scaffolded environment generated on demand" — the AI's role in guided discovery

**Seven-Stage N-of-1 Guided Discovery Workflow**
- Stage 1: Raw case narrative capture (clinician-led, unstructured)
- Stage 2: Structured case synthesis (AI-assisted organisation)
- Stage 3: Hypothesis generation (open differential, no premature closure)
- Stage 4: Evidence interrogation (literature mapped to case)
- Stage 5: Two-Tier Analysis (see below)
- Stage 6: Structured output generation (CARE-aligned, publication-compatible)
- Stage 7: Reflection and gap identification (what remains unknown)

**Two-Tier Analysis Methodology**
- Tier 1: Surface-level pattern matching (what does this look like?)
- Tier 2: Deep mechanistic interrogation (why does this pattern exist? what's the underlying biology?)
- Applied to both rare and common presentations
- Prevents diagnostic closure — common case example (diabetic presentation) used to show the same protocol stops premature anchoring

**N-of-1 Research Synthesis**
- Single-patient research paradigm
- Rare disease use case: suspected convergence of G6PD + AMPD1 + GSD III/IX deficiencies (triple energy-pathway disorder)
- Common hospital presentation use case: routine diabetic case
- Mapping from raw case narrative → structured, publication-compatible output in a single session
- CARE-aligned outputs (Case Report guidelines)

**Preventing Diagnostic Closure at the Bedside**
- Diagnostic closure = premature commitment to a diagnosis before full data is in
- AI role: surface what hasn't been asked, what hasn't been checked
- "What would make you regret starting anticoagulation?" question type
- "What could kill the patient?" as a forced reconsideration prompt

## 🧩 CLINICAL COMPLEXITY CONSTRUCTS

**Complexity Dimensions for Prompts**
- Single diagnosis vs. multimorbidity
- Single system vs. multi-system involvement
- Acute vs. chronic vs. acute-on-chronic
- Reversible vs. irreversible pathology
- Time-critical vs. time-permissive presentations
- High-stakes (low-frequency) vs. low-stakes (high-frequency) presentations
- Diagnostic certainty spectrum: clear / ambiguous / diagnostically unknown
- Resource-rich vs. resource-limited setting adaptation
- Shift fatigue context ("3 AM on the 12th hour of a shift")
- High cognitive load context (6 patients, 40 minutes)

**Case Complexity Layers for Prompt Design**
- Simple (single diagnosis, single system, typical presentation)
- Moderate (two competing diagnoses, one atypical feature)
- Complex (multimorbidity, atypical presentation, treatment interaction)
- Very complex (rare disease, diagnostic uncertainty, no clear guideline)
- Crisis (haemodynamically unstable, immediate decision required)

**Clinical Vignette Progression (Faculty Use)**
- Progressively harder case series (as in sepsis example: basic → advanced)
- Case 1: typical presentation, named criteria (qSOFA)
- Case 2: complication layer (confusion, lactate)
- Case 3: atypical host (elderly, immunocompromised)
- Case 4: paediatric adaptation (same disease, adjusted scoring)
- Case 5: resource-limited or unusual context

**What/Where/Do Template (Vibe Rounds Clinical Reasoning Scaffold)**
- What: what is wrong with this patient? (diagnosis)
- Where: where in the system / anatomy / pathophysiology? (localisation)
- Do: what do I do next? (management / investigation / escalation)
- AI identifies soft spots in each of the three limbs
- Captures initial reasoning before AI engagement

## 📊 PEDAGOGICAL SPECTRUM (Full 5-Stage Model)

- Stage 1 — Lecture: educator as expert/source, learner as passive receiver; information delivery and foundational framing
- Stage 2 — Dyadic: educator as facilitator, learner as active collaborator; peer-to-peer articulation, encoding, social learning
- Stage 3 — Socratic: educator as questioner, learner as critical thinker; challenging assumptions, surfacing logical gaps (Vibe Rounds home base)
- Stage 4 — Guided Discovery: educator as architect of experience, learner as problem-solver; constructing mental models through scaffolded experience (Vibe Rounds extends here)
- Stage 5 — Research: educator as mentor, learner as independent scholar; generating original insight, mastery

**Spectrum Design Principles**
- Cognitive load theory maps to stage selection (start at Research = overwhelmed; stay at Lecture = no independent muscle built)
- Agency and responsibility shift from educator to student across the spectrum
- Meaning-making transfers stage by stage
- Vibe Rounds is the tool purpose-built for Stages 3 and 4

## 🤖 AI CONSTRAINT SYSTEM (Full Set)

1. Forced commitment first
2. Minimum effort threshold ("idk" redirect)
3. 10-second rule / delayed rescue (deliberate pause)
4. Tiered hints, never full answer upfront (framework → direction → partial → teaching summary)
5. Effort-weighted assistance (more thoughtful attempt → deeper teaching)
6. Reasoning grading, not just correctness (logic / prioritisation / uncertainty-flagging)
7. Adaptive difficulty (novice → aggressive Socratic)
8. Role-calibrated rigor (intern / resident / attending / examiner posture)
9. Reflection before reveal ("Why do you think that?" / "What could kill the patient?" / "What are you missing?")
10. Answer-withholding policy (full answer released only after attempt + reasoning + revision OR explicit surrender)

**Appending Constraint Line to Any Prompt**
- "Apply effort-weighted, tiered-hint Socratic constraints — require a committed first attempt, withhold the full answer until I've reasoned through it, and grade my logic and uncertainty as much as my final answer."
