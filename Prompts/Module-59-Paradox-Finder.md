# Module XX — The Paradox Finder

**Objective:** Surface and interrogate genuine clinical paradoxes within a case — findings, trajectories, or treatment responses that contradict each other or defy the working diagnosis — and use the contradiction itself as the primary diagnostic signal.

**Indication:** Use when a patient's clinical course contains findings that should not coexist under the working diagnosis; when treatment is producing the opposite of the expected response; when two reliable data points flatly contradict each other; or when a case is generating unease that cannot yet be articulated but feels internally inconsistent.

## Lifecycle

Phase 1 · Initiation → Phase 2 · Execution → Phase 3 · Closure / Review

## Phase 1 · Initiation — Name the contradiction and contract the role

### Step XX.0: Session Setup — Declaring the Paradox Hunt

**Prompt:**

```
#VibeRounds You are a clinical paradox analyst. Your only job in this
session is to find things in this case that should not logically coexist
and force me to explain why they do. You are not here to reassure me
that the case makes sense. You are here to find where it does not.
A paradox, for our purposes, is any pair of findings, responses, or
trajectories that are mutually inconsistent under a single unifying
explanation. You will not let me rationalise my way past a contradiction
without first naming it precisely. Before I present the case, confirm
your role in one sentence and tell me what you will be listening for.
```

> [!NOTE] **Application Note:** The paradox frame is distinct from differential diagnosis — it is not asking "what else could this be?" but "what in this case is internally contradictory?" A well-formed paradox is a stronger signal than a missing diagnosis because it cannot be explained by a simple omission; it requires a structural revision of the reasoning. This module is highest-yield in complex, chronic, or treatment-refractory cases.

## Phase 2 · Execution — Systematic paradox extraction and interrogation

### Step XX.1: The Contradiction Inventory

**Prompt:**

```
#VibeRounds I will now present the case. As I do, construct a
Contradiction Inventory: a running list of every finding, result,
or trajectory in this case that cannot simultaneously be true under
a single explanation. Do not filter for importance — list every
inconsistency you detect, even minor ones. After I finish presenting,
read the inventory back to me and ask me to rank the contradictions
from most to least clinically significant before we interrogate any
of them.
```

### Step XX.2: The Treatment-Response Paradox Check

**Prompt:**

```
#VibeRounds For this case, ask me the following three questions in
sequence and wait for my full answer before proceeding to the next:
(1) Has any intervention in this case produced the opposite effect
to what the working diagnosis would predict — and if so, have I
documented that inversion explicitly?
(2) Has the patient improved on a treatment that should not work
for the working diagnosis, or failed to improve on one that should?
(3) Is there any finding that resolved spontaneously when it should
not have, or persisted when it should have resolved?
Label each confirmed instance a Treatment-Response Paradox and add
it to the Contradiction Inventory.
```

### Step XX.3: The Temporal Paradox Audit

**Prompt:**

```
#VibeRounds Now examine the timeline of this case for temporal
paradoxes — events or findings that occurred in the wrong sequence
for the working diagnosis to be correct. Ask me:
(1) Did any symptom appear before its presumed cause in the
working model?
(2) Did any finding resolve before the treatment that was supposed
to cause its resolution was started?
(3) Is there a lag — longer or shorter than the working diagnosis
would predict — between any cause and its supposed effect in
this case?
For each confirmed temporal paradox, name the two events involved
and state precisely what the working diagnosis predicts the sequence
should have been.
```

### Step XX.4: The Physiological Impossibility Test

**Prompt:**

```
#VibeRounds Apply a physiological impossibility test to the working
diagnosis. Ask me to defend, in mechanistic terms, how the working
diagnosis simultaneously accounts for the two most contradictory
findings in the Contradiction Inventory. Then ask:
(1) Is there a known pathophysiological mechanism that permits
these two findings to coexist, or am I relying on coincidence?
(2) Would a pathologist or physiologist, reading only these two
findings without my working diagnosis, propose the same unifying
explanation I have?
(3) If I cannot provide a mechanistic bridge, what does that
absence tell me about the working diagnosis?
Do not accept "it is unusual but possible" as an answer without
a named mechanism.
```

### Step XX.5: The Occam's Razor vs. Hickam's Dictum Decision

**Prompt:**

```
#VibeRounds This case contains contradictions that may reflect either
a single unifying diagnosis I have not yet identified, or multiple
simultaneous diagnoses that are each internally consistent.
Ask me to argue both positions explicitly:
(1) Occam's Razor position — state the single diagnosis that best
unifies all contradictions, even if it is not the current working
diagnosis.
(2) Hickam's Dictum position — state the combination of two or
three diagnoses that, taken together, resolve every contradiction
without requiring a mechanistic stretch.
Then ask me: which position is more consistent with this specific
patient's epidemiological profile, risk factors, and trajectory —
and why?
Force me to commit to one before moving on.
```

### Step XX.6: The Paradox-as-Signal Reframe

**Prompt:**

```
#VibeRounds Take the single most clinically significant contradiction
from the Contradiction Inventory and reframe it not as a problem to
be explained away but as the primary diagnostic signal — the finding
most likely to point toward the correct diagnosis.
Ask me:
(1) If I had presented this case to a grand rounds audience
and led with this paradox rather than the working diagnosis,
what alternative diagnoses would the audience have generated?
(2) What investigation, if ordered specifically to interrogate
this paradox rather than to confirm the working diagnosis,
has the highest likelihood of resolving the contradiction?
(3) Am I avoiding this investigation for any reason other than
clinical necessity — and if so, name the reason explicitly?
```

## Phase 3 · Closure / Review — Resolve, flag, or escalate

### Step XX.7: Paradox Resolution Statement

**Prompt:**

```
#VibeRounds The paradox hunt is now complete. Ask me to produce a
Paradox Resolution Statement structured as follows:
(1) List every item from the Contradiction Inventory and assign
each one of three statuses: RESOLVED (a mechanism has been
identified), FLAGGED (unresolved but being actively monitored),
or ESCALATED (requires immediate further investigation or
specialist input before management proceeds).
(2) State whether the Paradox Resolution Statement changes,
modifies, or maintains the working diagnosis.
(3) Name the single unresolved paradox, if any, that would — if
left unexplained — represent the greatest risk to this patient,
and state the safety net I am putting in place for it.
```

### Step XX.8: Reasoning Pattern Debrief

**Prompt:**

```
#VibeRounds Review the reasoning I displayed during this session.
Identify the single most revealing moment — the point at which
the case's internal contradiction was clearest and I either
engaged with it or rationalised past it.
Then ask me:
(1) What cognitive strategy — pattern recognition, anchoring,
satisficing — caused me to accept the contradiction rather
than pursue it earlier in the case?
(2) What is the one clinical habit, question, or checkpoint I
should build into every future complex case to surface
paradoxes earlier in the diagnostic process?
End with a one-sentence paradox-literacy observation — a
reflection on what this case reveals about how I handle
clinical contradiction in general.
```
