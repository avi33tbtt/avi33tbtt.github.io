Title

A Case of Chronic Non-Cardiac Chest Pain in a Patient with Multi-System Chronic Illness and Psychosomatic Overlay: A Global Health Perspective

Abstract

This case report details the complex clinical journey of a female patient in her late 20s presenting with chronic non-cardiac chest pain, superimposed on a history of extra-pulmonary tuberculosis, irritable bowel syndrome-like symptoms, gastroesophageal reflux disease, and significant anxiety and depression. The narrative highlights the diagnostic challenges, the interplay between organic disease and psychosomatic factors, and the importance of an integrated, patient-centered approach in managing such multi-faceted chronic conditions within a global health context. Key recommendations include objective diagnostic evaluations, optimized medical management, targeted psychotherapy, and physical rehabilitation.

Keywords: Non-cardiac chest pain, GERD, Anxiety, Tuberculosis sequelae, Psychosomatic, Global health, Integrated care, Case report

01. Introduction

Chronic non-cardiac chest pain (NCCP) is a prevalent and often debilitating condition, frequently associated with gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) and functional esophageal disorders, but also with musculoskeletal and psychological factors. Globally, the burden of chronic diseases, including infectious sequelae and mental health conditions, presents significant challenges to healthcare systems. This case report describes the complex clinical presentation and management considerations for a female patient in her late 20s with a history of extra-pulmonary tuberculosis, chronic gastrointestinal issues, and significant anxiety, who presented with persistent NCCP. The case highlights the intricate interplay between organic pathology, functional disorders, and psychosomatic components, emphasizing the need for an integrated, patient-centered approach in resource-constrained or globally diverse settings.

03. Results

A female patient in her late 20s presented with persistent, intermittent central chest pain, radiating across the chest, which commenced approximately 16 months prior to the conversational log (Day 0). The pain was exacerbated by movement, pressure, sneezing, and physical activities like kneading dough. Concurrently, she reported breathlessness during talking or minor physical exertion, often linked to anxiety. A chest X-ray revealed hyperinflated lungs.

The patient's medical history was extensive and complex. Approximately 13-14 years prior to Day 0, she was diagnosed with extra-pulmonary tuberculosis affecting both lungs and intestines, which was successfully treated. This was followed by a long-standing history of recurrent infections, including typhoid, jaundice, and mumps. Since approximately 14 years prior to Day 0, she experienced chronic irritable bowel syndrome-like symptoms, characterized by severe abdominal pain, bloating, and vomiting. She also reported a 'slow bowel' with a 3-day cycle for emptying. Gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) symptoms, including acid reflux and globus pharyngeus ('lump in throat'), had been present for approximately 5 years prior to Day 0, managed with intermittent proton pump inhibitor (PPI) therapy, including Omeprazole 40mg, and sucralfate. She had previously found success with a low FODMAP diet.

Significant psychiatric comorbidity was noted, with a history of Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD) and Panic Disorder, which peaked approximately 4-5 years prior to Day 0. These conditions manifested with somatic symptoms such as chest tightness, breathlessness, pounding heart, and a 'burning body' sensation. She had also experienced major depression, characterized by extreme exhaustion, social withdrawal, and significant weight loss. An allergy to Fluoxetine was reported, leading to hesitancy regarding mental health medications. Nutritional deficiencies included severe Vitamin D deficiency (level 6), and she maintained a low body weight (52 kg) with fluctuations. Other reported symptoms included oligomenorrhea, intermittent nerve entrapments, severe headaches, and eye floaters.

Clinical assessment highlighted a 'vicious cycle' where anxiety triggered GERD, and the resulting atypical chest pain fueled further health anxiety, including a fear of cancer. The primary clinical problems identified were chronic non-cardiac chest pain, hyperinflated lungs and breathlessness, chronic GERD and intestinal dysmotility, psychosomatic comorbidity (GAD and depression), and recurrent vitamin deficiencies. Suggested interventions included an upper GI endoscopy to rule out structural esophageal changes and provide reassurance, pulmonary function tests to differentiate causes of breathlessness, consistent PPI use with potential addition of H2 blockers, dietary rigor, re-engagement with psychiatry for targeted psychotherapy (CBT, gut-directed hypnotherapy) and careful medication review, physical therapy for musculoskeletal pain, and nutritional counseling with a registered dietitian.

04. Discussion

This case illustrates the profound diagnostic and therapeutic challenges encountered in patients with chronic multi-system illnesses, especially when compounded by significant psychosomatic overlays. The patient's history of extra-pulmonary tuberculosis, followed by chronic gastrointestinal issues and the development of GERD, created a fertile ground for the emergence of non-cardiac chest pain (NCCP). The 'vicious cycle' where anxiety exacerbates GERD symptoms, which in turn fuels health anxiety and chest pain, is a well-recognized phenomenon in NCCP, often requiring a multi-modal approach to management. The debate between esophageal versus musculoskeletal origins of the chest pain is common in NCCP. While the movement-exacerbated nature suggests a musculoskeletal component (e.g., costochondritis), the long-standing GERD makes esophageal pain a strong contender. The patient's fear of cancer, though understandable given the chronicity of GERD, highlights the psychological burden of chronic illness and the therapeutic value of objective diagnostic reassurance, such as an upper GI endoscopy. The reluctance towards mental health medication, stemming from a prior adverse reaction, is a common barrier to care, necessitating careful medication selection and framing of treatment to address patient concerns. The presence of hyperinflated lungs on X-ray and exertional breathlessness further complicates the picture, requiring differentiation between pulmonary pathology and anxiety-driven hyperventilation. The global health implications of this case are significant. Access to comprehensive diagnostic tools (e.g., endoscopy, manometry, PFTs), integrated mental health services, and specialized dietary and physical therapy support can be limited in many settings. The patient's journey also highlights the potential for patient advocacy platforms to facilitate better understanding and coordination of care for complex cases. Limitations of this report include reliance on a de-identified conversational log, which may not capture all granular clinical details or specific test results, and the absence of long-term follow-up data.

05. Conclusion

This case underscores the critical need for a holistic, integrated approach to managing complex chronic conditions, particularly when significant psychosomatic components are present. Addressing both the physical and mental health aspects concurrently, alongside patient education and empowerment, is paramount for improving outcomes and quality of life. The 'vicious cycle' observed in this patient emphasizes that effective care requires breaking down silos between specialties and fostering a comprehensive understanding of the patient's lived experience.
