Read against the article's own boundary lines

Job 01 — Trivial automation

VibeRounds covers none of this job by design. It's a Socratic prompt paradigm, not a symbolic reasoner — the site says so directly: "prompt-level uncertainty framing, not automated verification." Domain 3 (bedside decision support) is marked early-stage with no EMR/FHIR integration.

Gap is total, and named openly by the source, not hidden.

Job 02 — Post-hoc audit

The Trust Layer's prioritised claim verification and evidence-anchoring modules are the strongest match — risk-based, tied to source, checking a claim already made. But it's still a prompt asking a model to reason about consistency, not a rule engine hard-blocking a violation.

Real coverage, capped below symbolic-audit precision.

Job 03 — Case-based sandbox

This is where the paradigm was purpose-built: forced commitment, tiered hints, answer-withholding, effort-weighted depth, deliberate case variation across dozens of demo tools (Case Simulator, Decision Lab, Case Lens, Phronesis).

Closest thing to full coverage anywhere in the matrix.

Job 04 — Longitudinal analytics

Case Analytics and PaJR are real, built examples of pattern-over-time. But the domain-maturity map on the site rates Guided Discovery research at "medium maturity — worked case complete, awaiting multi-case validation," so the pattern-across-learners claim isn't validated at scale yet.

Built and demoed, not yet proven across many learners.
Same discipline both articles insist on: none of these tools notices for the clinician or the learner. VibeRounds itself states this outright — "all outputs are learning observations, not clinical decisions."
