Post-Hoc Clinical AI Audit

A six-stage decision framework. Each stage offers a menu of ways to proceed — with the pros and cons weighed against each other — so the full protocol is composed one deliberate choice at a time, rather than assumed.

"Post-hoc audit" is the auditor's-eye view of this pipeline — checking a finalized answer against a standard, after the fact. Run through a learner's or researcher's persona, the same pipeline reads more like case-based problem exploration or error-pattern analysis. Stage 0 makes that choice explicit, since it colors every stage after it.

Stage 00

Persona & Purpose

Who is this pipeline being run for, and what do they do with the result? This is upstream of everything else — it changes what counts as an acceptable error, what the audit is really checking, and what "done" looks like.

First — the user persona
PersonaDescriptionProsCons
Learner A trainee working a case to build reasoning skill. The "audit" is formative feedback, not a verdict. Low stakes — safe to surface every near-miss and reasoning gap, no record follows the learner Feedback needs pedagogical framing, not just a pass/fail flag, or it teaches nothing
Clinician A practicing clinician checking their own reasoning on a real or simulated case, at the point of care or shortly after. Directly actionable — catches errors close to the moment they'd matter Time-pressured; audit has to be fast and low-friction or it won't be used
Auditor A reviewer checking a specific, already-finalized plan against a binding standard, with real consequences on failure. Highest-stakes, highest-rigor mode — this is "post-hoc audit" in the strict original sense False negatives are costly; needs the most conservative, best-validated methods from Stage 4
Researcher Studying patterns across many cases/answers to publish a finding (e.g. does scaffolding reduce anchoring bias?). Aggregate view surfaces systematic patterns single-case audits can't Needs the full rigor of Stage 5 (blinding, statistics) or findings won't hold up
Second — what kind of audit this actually is
Audit typeDescriptionProsCons
Self-audit Formative — a learner or clinician checks their own reasoning to improve, no external record kept. Encourages honest self-scrutiny with nothing on the line No external validation; blind spots can go uncaught indefinitely
Clinical research Pattern-finding across a corpus of cases and answers, aimed at a publishable finding. Can detect systematic effects (e.g. scaffolding's real impact) invisible at single-case scale Requires much heavier methodological rigor — underpowered or unblinded studies mislead
Quality / compliance audit A finalized plan checked against a binding guideline or protocol, with real consequences if it fails. The only mode with teeth — findings have direct real-world stakes A missed error here costs a patient, not a habit — demands the most conservative method stack
Problem exploration Open-ended case work aimed at insight or teaching rather than a verdict — closer to what "audit" means for a learner. Generates richer, more transferable understanding than a pass/fail check Harder to score or compare across arms; not a substitute for compliance-grade checking
If unsure Name the persona and audit type before choosing anything downstream. A learner running problem exploration and an auditor running quality/compliance audit should not share a scoring rubric — the same pipeline structure, but the error tolerance and audit rigor at Stage 4–5 need to shift accordingly. Mixing personas without saying so is the most common way this kind of protocol quietly loses validity.
Stage 01

Scenario Generation

How do you produce the underlying clinical case — the patient, the history, the data — before any question is asked about it?

OptionDescriptionProsCons
A · Real cases De-identified pulls from actual patient records or case reports. High ecological validity; captures real messiness Ethics/IRB overhead; de-identification risk; hard to control difficulty or violation placement
B · Synthetic Clinician hand-writes cases from scratch, seeded with known ground truth. Full control over difficulty, planted errors, rule coverage Time-intensive; risk of unrealistic "textbook" cases that don't stress-test real reasoning
C · AI-drafted AI drafts scenarios, human vets and edits for realism and correctness. Fast to scale a large bank; consistent format Generator bias may leak in; needs careful review to avoid subtle errors baked in from the start
D · Published corpus Existing peer-reviewed cases (e.g. N-of-1 reports, journals) used as-is. Ground truth already vetted by literature; citable Limited pool; may already be in training data — contamination risk for LLM arms
If unsure B for the core validated bank (control), C to scale volume once the format is proven, D as an external contamination check.
Stage 02

Query Generation

Once the scenario exists, how is the question posed to the answering arms formulated — and who formulates it?

First — who writes the query
OptionDescriptionProsCons
A · Human-authored Clinician or educator writes the question directly. Reflects real pedagogical/clinical intent; avoids AI framing bias Slower; one author's phrasing habits may unintentionally hint at the answer
B · AI-generated Model drafts the question from the scenario, given a style instruction. Fast, scalable, consistent structure across a large case bank May generate leading, oddly-scoped, or answer-revealing questions without a human catching it
C · AI-drafted, human-edited AI proposes the query, human revises for neutrality and fit. Balances speed with quality control Still bottlenecked by human review time, though less than fully manual
Second — what format the query takes
FormatDescriptionProsCons
1 · Socratic / open "What would you do next, and why?" — no structure imposed. Surfaces genuine reasoning process, not just a lookup Hard to score consistently; answers vary wildly in length and format
2 · Best-answer / MCQ Forces a discrete choice among options. Easy to score, easy to compare across arms Loses the reasoning trace; can be gamed by pattern-matching rather than understanding
3 · EBM / PICO Formulated as Population–Intervention–Comparison–Outcome. Ties directly to literature-grounding and RAG checks downstream Feels artificial for bedside-style scenarios; not every case maps cleanly to PICO
4 · Differential dx "List and rank the differential diagnosis." Tests breadth of consideration, not just the final answer Doesn't test management/treatment reasoning, only diagnostic reasoning
5 · Management plan "What is the management plan?" — diagnosis assumed settled. Directly testable against guidelines and rule engines Skips the diagnostic-reasoning step entirely
6 · Adversarial framing Scenario includes a deliberately misleading or biased frame to test resistance. Tests robustness to anchoring/bias, not just baseline correctness Harder to construct well; the "trap" can end up too obvious or too obscure
If unsure C for who writes it — AI-drafted, human-edited scales without losing quality control. Rotate through formats 1, 3, and 5 (Socratic, EBM/PICO, management-plan) across the case bank rather than locking into one — each stresses a different failure mode.
Stage 03

Answer-Generation Arms

Who or what produces the answer that will later be audited?

OptionDescriptionProsCons
A · Human only Clinician or trainee answers; no AI involved. Gold-standard baseline; no AI confound Doesn't test AI at all — useful only as a reference arm, not standalone
B · Raw AI Model answers with a plain, unscaffolded prompt. Tests default model behavior; cheapest to run Conflates model capability with prompting quality — a weak, easy-to-beat baseline
C · Scaffolded AI Model answers via a structured framework (e.g. Socratic/EBM module). Isolates whether scaffolding improves quality/safety over raw AI The scaffold itself needs its own validation; more variables to control
D · Human–AI collaborative Clinician drafts with AI co-writing, or vice versa. Reflects likely real-world deployment Hardest to standardize; error attribution becomes ambiguous
If unsure Run A + B + C together as the three-arm comparison — the minimal set that separates "does AI help at all" from "does scaffolding help beyond raw AI." Add D later as a real-world-deployment follow-up.
A pipeline can stop here

Stages 0–3 alone — without Stage 4 (Audit) or Stage 5 (Scoring) — are a complete, self-contained product: a case-based sandbox / simulator, not an audit tool.

In this mode the scenario at Stage 1 can be user-supplied (a learner brings their own case) or AI-guided (the scaffold generates and steers the case, Guided-Discovery style) — either way, the learner works the query through to an answer and the experience ends there. No claim is checked against a rule set, no second pass audits it, nothing is scored against another arm. That's not a shortfall; it's a different product with a different job — closer to Job 03 (case-based sandbox) from the original coverage matrix than to Job 02 (post-hoc audit). This is where VibeRounds' Case Simulator / Socratic Learning already lives.

The moment Stage 4 or 5 gets added on top, the product's claim changes — from "practice space" to "checked judgment" — and the rigor bar in the earlier stages of this document applies.

Stage 04

Audit / Grounding Method

How do you check each answer for errors, bias, and hallucination?

OptionDescriptionProsCons
A · Shadow LLM pass A separate model instance critiques the answer for gaps, bias, contradictions. Cheap, fast, scales to large case banks Unvalidated auditor — may share blind spots with the arm it audits; needs its own accuracy check
B · RAG grounding Retrieve relevant guideline/textbook text, check claims against it. Ties claims to a citable source; reduces free-floating hallucination Only as good as retrieval quality; doesn't catch reasoning errors, only factual mismatches
C · GraphRAG Structured relationships (drug–drug, disease–symptom) checked against a knowledge graph. Catches interaction-type errors a flat-text RAG misses Heavier to build and maintain; graph coverage gaps are silent failures
D · Neuro-symbolic rules Hard-coded rules (dosing ceilings, contraindications) run as a symbolic pass. Deterministic, auditable, no hallucination risk in the checker itself Only catches what's explicitly encoded; brittle to phrasing and context
E · Human expert Clinician reads and scores each answer directly. Highest trust; catches things no automated method would Slow, expensive, doesn't scale; risk of reviewer bias/fatigue if unblinded
If unsure Stack rather than pick one: D (rule engine) for anything with hard, codifiable limits; B/C (RAG or GraphRAG) for guideline-adherence questions; A as a cheap first-pass triage flagging candidates for review; E as the final adjudicator.
Stage 05

Scoring & Blinding

The connective layer — decides whether Stages 1–4 actually produce a valid result.

First — who acts as the auditor
OptionDescriptionProsCons
A · Human A clinician or trained reviewer scores each answer directly. Highest trust; catches things no model would; carries real accountability Slow, expensive, doesn't scale; subject to fatigue and reviewer drift
B · Same LLM The identical model that generated the answer also audits it. Cheapest, fastest; no extra infrastructure needed Shares the generator's own blind spots and biases — least independent option, weakest as a real check
C · Different LLM A separate model (different family or version) audits the answer. Some independence from the generator's specific failure modes; still cheap and fast Still unvalidated as an auditor — needs its own accuracy check against human judgment before being trusted
D · Ensemble (LLM panel + human spot-check) Multiple LLM auditors vote or flag disagreement; a human reviews only the flagged/contested cases. Scales further than pure human review while keeping a human in the loop where models disagree More moving parts to build and calibrate; disagreement threshold has to be tuned carefully
Second — blind or open
OptionDescriptionProsCons
A · Blind audit Arm identity hidden, style-normalized before review — applies whether the auditor is human or an LLM. Prevents halo/stigma bias toward "AI-sounding" or "human-sounding" text Requires normalizing tone/formatting, extra prep work; for an LLM auditor, may need explicit prompting to ignore stylistic tells
B · Open audit Auditor — human or LLM — knows the source of each answer. Simpler to run; useful if studying trust/bias toward AI answers directly, including an LLM's own bias toward AI-generated text Confounds answer quality with source bias — invalid for a pure quality comparison
If unsure Pair C (a different LLM, not the same one that generated the answer) with A (blind) for a fast first pass, and route anything flagged or borderline to a human under the same blind protocol — this is Option D above. Never use B (same LLM) as the sole auditor for a compliance-grade check: a model auditing its own output is the weakest form of independence in this framework, since it inherits the very blind spots it would need to catch.

Reading this row VibeRounds fills real, working stages of the framework — Persona, Audit type, Query, and the Scaffolded-AI answer arm are all genuinely built.
