Clinical AI Literacy

A four-part series on where LLMs are actually reliable in clinical reasoning, where they quietly fail, and how to prompt them so they check your thinking instead of replacing it.

⚡ Short on time? Skip the theory and go straight to Part 4: One Case, Every Mode — a single worked example that shows everything the series argues, without the three-part backstory.

The Series

  1. Part 1 — Clinical AI Literacy: Mapping Where LLMs Are Actually Usable

    Lays out the core framework: LLMs are highly reliable at recall (facts, guidelines), shakier at synthesis, and weakest when weighing a messy real patient. The root failure mode is premature closure — the model anchors on an early plausible story and stops looking for reasons it's wrong, all while sounding equally confident whether right or wrong. Take-away: use these tools to check reasoning, not to make the decision.

    Read Part 1 →

  2. Part 2 — The Common-Case Blind Spot

    Applies the Part 1 framework to the case you'd expect to be easiest: the boring, low-acuity patient. Because medical literature is written about the interesting or dangerous version of a symptom, an LLM's instinct on a mundane case skews toward over-testing and over-caution, delivered in the same confident tone as a genuine emergency. Introduces the fix: use the model to audit a plan you've already formed, not to generate one from scratch.

    Read Part 2 →

  3. Part 3 — From Diagnosis to Practice

    Turns the Part 2 finding into an actual technique. Shows that a prompt's structure — not its tone — determines whether the model genuinely pushes back or just rubber-stamps you, and that how you present a case (what you include vs. withhold) decides whether you're testing the model's reasoning or just leading it to your own answer. Includes before/after prompt examples in Analytics and Socratic modes.

    Read Part 3 →

  4. Part 4 — One Case, Every Mode: A Complete Walkthrough

    Quick ReadRuns a single genuinely ambiguous case — a 52-year-old woman with weeks of fatigue, weight loss, and vague abdominal discomfort — through every mode from the series (Answer, Analytics, Socratic) so you can see the risky version and the safe versions side by side. The fastest way to get the whole series' point without reading the other three first.
