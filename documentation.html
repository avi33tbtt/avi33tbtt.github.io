01 · The paradigm

Vibe Rounds is a paradigm, not a product. Just as “vibe coding” lets developers build software through natural language, Vibe Rounds applies that same paradigm to clinical reasoning: an AI mentor that questions, not one that answers.

“AI that questions, not answers.”

The clinician / learner stays the decision-maker. The AI plays the attending: it challenges assumptions, surfaces biases, offers a second opinion, flags oversimplification, and guides via structured questioning. It is a learning tool, not a diagnostic tool.

The pedagogical spectrum

StageCentringWhat Vibe Rounds claims
1 · LectureInstructorFoundation: shared vocabulary and framing.
2 · DyadicPeerPeers explain to each other, forcing encoding.
3 · Socratic ★SelfHome base of Vibe Rounds — stress-test understanding with “Why?” and “What if?”
4 · Guided discovery ★Self (scaffolded)Tiered hints build the environment on demand.
5 · Research ★Self / researcherN-of-1 protocols, case analytics, evidence mapping.

Mapping to sites: Stage 3 = Site 1 (Hub), Stage 4 = Site 2 (Prompts Library), Stage 5 = Site 3 (Clinical Intelligence Suite).

The progression is a design hypothesis informed by Cognitive Load Theory[1] and the expertise-reversal effect[2]: heavy open-ended work can overload novices, while guidance that helps novices can become redundant for experts. Scaffolded, fading support[3] motivates the tiered hints. Committing to an answer before help draws on the generation effect[4] and “desirable difficulties”[5]; the bias-surfacing prompts draw on work on cognitive errors in diagnosis[6]. These sources support the design rationale; this page does not report a learner outcome study of Vibe Rounds itself.

03 · Guided discovery — CCOS

CCOS is a prompt-pipeline framework — the structured prompt architecture behind Vibe Rounds. It is not software or an operating system with persistent state, but an orchestrated pipeline of prompt modules that run in a defined order on a case. Structured prompts based on clinical cognitive models to analyze case data using LLM.

3.1 · What CCOS is

  • Six cognitive layers: (1) clinical reasoning — illness scripts, differentials, Bayesian; (2) workflow engine — sequencing, pipelines; (3) metacognitive monitoring — bias, assumptions, uncertainty; (4) uncertainty framing — prompts that elicit explicit uncertainty statements, risk-based claim-verification prompts for the human to act on, decision spectrum (conservative→maximal); (5) decision architecture; (6) learning & documentation.
  • Not persistent state. CCOS is a prompt-level uncertainty-framing architecture — not automated verification, and confidence an LLM states is not a calibrated probability.

3.2 · CCOS Builder

The ccos-builder.html page lets you tick clinical-reasoning prompt modules in the order you'd run them on a case and get a plain-language read on the likely analytic yield of that pipeline:

① Case Under Review→ ② Chosen Pipeline→ ③ Pick Modules, In Order
  • Modules are colour-coded by category (e.g. clinical reasoning cyan, advocacy amber, analytics violet, safety red, education emerald, operations bronze, meta slate).
  • You build an ordered pipeline, get a "likely analytic yield" readout, and can Clear chain or Copy pipeline.
  • This is how you compose the 57+ modules into a case-appropriate sequence rather than running them ad hoc.

04 · Research (Stage 5)

Research is Stage 5 of the pedagogical spectrum: generating or testing something that could hold true beyond the one patient in front of you. It has three components that share one principle — every output traces back to a source, and a human verifies it.

4.B · NARRATIVES

4.C · FHIR · SITE 3

Clinical Intelligence Suite

4.A · Structured evidence summary

The flagship is an ASV (anti-snake-venom) evidence map: a full evidence-tier synthesis on ASV mortality benefit, built from PubMed primary literature rather than a single case. Every case report, systematic review, meta-analysis and RCT from a documented, filtered search was extracted one paper at a time into a running trajectory map, keeping each evidence tier separate instead of pooling incompatible units.

What it found

The signal sits in case reports

RCTs and meta-analyses don’t establish an ASV mortality benefit — they weren’t designed or powered to. The signal lives in case reports and observational data. An RCT withholding antivenom would be unethical, so that trial will likely never exist.

What kind of research

Scoping / evidence-mapping review

A recognised secondary-research genre, one rung below a formal systematic review. Every claim traces to a specific source; nothing is pooled that shouldn’t be.

  • Named limitations: partial sampling (started at 12 of 78 available case reports, being expanded with an exclusion log); single LLM-assisted extractor with no independent second reviewer yet; no pre-registered protocol (e.g. PROSPERO); RCT/meta-analysis and case-report tiers still separate passes.
  • What it isn’t: a substitute for a registered systematic review, a statistical meta-analysis, or peer review. Outputs are drafts to verify against primary literature.
  • Demo: ASV Full — the complete hub-node network ↗

Supporting material lives in articles/: clinical AI best-of-both-worlds, trivial-vs-deep reasoning, human-vs-LLM energy efficiency, thinking process.

4.B · Auditable case record / narrative analytics

A research plan titled “Turning case narratives and chats into auditable clinical analytics” — a project plan for extracting structured features from narrative patient records and daily care chats with LLMs, validating them, and analyzing them deterministically or with a second LLM pass. One extraction layer feeds teaching, reasoning review, and research. Read the full plan ↗

Pilot

Diabetes + kidney disease

Reads diabetes and kidney disease records, turns them into checked structured data, and analyzes that data with code or a second LLM pass. Every result links back to the original words.

Structure

Six phases + gates

Raw notes → checked result in six steps; do them in order and stop at any step whose check fails. Each phase ends with a gate.

Two data sources

Case narratives + daily care chats

Narratives give the clinical picture at admission; chats give the day-by-day course, the team’s reasoning, and life at home. A case index links nearly a thousand public medical e-log posts.

  • Variable dictionary first, then prompts — one prompt per extraction unit, each returning strict JSON with a source span and certainty on every item, allowing “not stated”, all versioned.
  • Extract with LLM, derive with code — anything derived is calculated by tested code, so the same input always gives the same output. Derived values are computed by code rather than generated by the LLM; errors can still arise upstream (LLM extraction, query building), which is why extraction is validated and outputs are human-reviewed.
  • Validation (planned — no results reported on this page): clinician labels development and held-out splits; measure precision, recall, and kappa; fix prompts at version 1.0 and log model IDs/parameters on every call.
  • Two analysis engines: deterministic (rule-based, reproducible) or LLM-based (nuance/interpretation/critique); each task specifies its recommended approach.
  • Template for other diseases: diabetes/CKD is the worked pilot; the same structure can regenerate the plan, dictionary, prompts, and analysis modules for heart failure, COPD, tuberculosis, stroke, sepsis, etc.
  • Outputs: case report, cohort dashboard, exportable datasets for R/SPSS/Stata; every output traceable to an extracted feature and every feature to a sentence in source text.
Scope: For research and medical education, not clinical advice. All outputs require independent clinical verification. Adapted versions for other diseases should state what changed and keep the disclaimer.

4.C · FHIR discovery — import FHIR data, run deterministic analytics

This is the Clinical Intelligence Suite (repository VibeRounds-Clinical-Analytics-App), a standalone Node/Express + PostgreSQL + FHIR R4 application. You import FHIR data, run deterministic analytics (e.g. mortality rate), and use an LLM as assistant to the human to interpret and explore data — automating the process where the human is the analyst, the LLM is the assistant that plans the analysis, and all analysis runs are deterministic (or deterministic presets such as mortality rate). Analyses that would otherwise need a manual query-and-extract cycle can be run interactively; no timing benchmark is reported on this page.

4.C.1 · Purpose

You bring FHIR patient data (your own files, the public HAPI test server, or a deterministic synthetic generator), load it into PostgreSQL, define cohorts, run a catalog of clinical analytics (prevalence, medication use, labs, readmissions, care gaps, safety, data quality) in three reading methods (narrative text / codes / codes+time), and let the AI assistant read results, flag findings, propose next analyses — with every number coming from a tested analytic or a human-reviewed query.

4.C.3 · End-to-end workflow

  1. Bring data and check it. Import your own FHIR files, fetch from HAPI FHIR test server (https://hapi.fhir.org/baseR4), or generate a synthetic batch. Run Analytics readiness to see how much of the data is coded, dated and narrative, and which analytics can run in which method.
  2. Ask, get ideas, or define. Plain-English question → assistant fills PICO/PECO study form → run it. Or Suggest a few studies. Or build a cohort; Build SNOMED CT Query writes criteria SQL for you.
  3. Run an analytic. Pick from the catalog, or run the dashboard to cover everything for the cohort.
  4. Get a read. Ask for insights on one run or all runs; flagged findings & proposed next steps; results can be explained in plain words.
  5. Steer. Run, skip, mark important or dismiss. Or start the AI researcher to chain rounds automatically.
  6. Go deeper. Browse individual patients (Patient data explorer) and the cohort knowledge graph.

4.C.6 · Key deep modules

Inpatient trajectory (V4)

Generator option inpatient; follows each stay through nine stages: Comorbidities, Diagnosis, Presentation, Initial plan, Medications, Procedures, Escalation, Advanced/recurrent, Disposition. Sections: scope, nodes, ribbons, stage_check, trace.

Disease module (V4)

disease: type 2 diabetes or hypertension; controlled = latest control result in 12 months below goal (HbA1c 7 / SBP 140); untested = care gap. Passes per age band, sex, burden, regimen, testing frequency, concordance, payer, income, social needs, adherence tier, CKD stage.

Cohort knowledge graph

GET /api/kg/cohort builds graph from data of scope: nodes (conditions/drugs/labs/procedures) with patients/prevalence/% abnormal; links with lift, z, Jaccard, P(b|a), P(a|b), order/median gap; curated tags; reference-only links n=0.

AI researcher

Optional LLM loop: 1–20 rounds; start from analytic or plain-English question; each round runs analytic, asks insight, asks next steps, writes summary, runs top suggestion; pause-to-review default on for start; audit logged.

Analytics readiness

POST /api/readiness/profile, POST /api/readiness/check, GET /api/readiness/jobs/:id; labels Runnable / No rows / Needs a choice / Error / Too slow; insights; Download JSON.

Custom analytic (plain English)

POST /api/analytics/custom/*; LLM writes one read-only SELECT over scoped views (p, dx, ob, rx, pr, en, al, im, ref); mode enforced (V1 text-only, V3 codes-no-dates, V4 codes+dates); refused query repaired once; READ ONLY txn, 60s, 1000-row cap; only the question is sent to the model (audit ai.request).

Research, not clinical support: outputs are descriptive for exploration and education; unadjusted comparisons; V3 outcomes are modelled at import so stratifying them is circular. Not a medical device, not for real patient care.
De-identified records only: the Clinical Intelligence Suite and the narrative-analytics work must be used only with synthetic or properly de-identified records, never with identifiable patient data. Validation status: this page does not report validation of the 14 analytics against reference datasets or independent checks of the narrative-extraction pipeline.

Related work

Vibe Rounds sits between educational and analytic work. Its approach to bias and cognitive error follows the diagnostic-reasoning literature[6]. The Clinical Intelligence Suite overlaps in purpose with established cohort-discovery and observational-data platforms such as i2b2[7] and OHDSI/OMOP[8]. Those are mature, community-validated platforms; the Suite differs by adding an LLM assistant and human-gate workflow over FHIR data, and it is not presented as a replacement for them.
