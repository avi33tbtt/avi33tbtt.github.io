Case 80: 70-Year-Old Man with Cough, Fever, and Breathlessness — Viral Pneumonia Secondary to COVID-19 with New-Onset Diabetes

Source case log: https://166sasirakasinadhuni.blogspot.com/2021/05/viral-pneumonia-secondary-to-covid-19.html

Case Narrative

A 70-year-old man presented with dry cough and fever of ten days' duration and shortness of breath for four days.

He had been apparently asymptomatic ten days prior when he developed an insidious, intermittent dry cough without diurnal or positional variation, followed by intermittent low-grade fever without chills or rigors that responded to medication. Four days before presentation he developed shortness of breath of insidious onset and gradual progression, without vomiting, chest pain, or loss of taste or smell. He tested positive for SARS-CoV-2 by RT-PCR with a CT severity score of 10/25 and was admitted to a COVID intensive care unit. He had no known history of hypertension, diabetes, coronary artery disease, asthma, or tuberculosis, and was a non-smoker and non-drinker, with no significant family history.

On examination he was moderately built and nourished, without pallor, icterus, cyanosis, clubbing, lymphadenopathy, or edema, though mildly dehydrated. On the day of admission his oxygen saturation was 86% on room air, improving to 93% with 10 litres of supplemental oxygen, with a pulse of 82/min and blood pressure 110/60 mmHg. Cardiovascular, respiratory, abdominal, and neurological examinations were otherwise unremarkable, with bilateral air entry and no organomegaly.

A random glucose check during admission returned markedly elevated at 401 mg/dl despite no prior history of diabetes, raising the question of either COVID-19-triggered new-onset diabetes or previously undiagnosed diabetes unmasked by the acute illness — a recognized association with COVID-19, thought to relate to both stress hyperglycemia and possible direct pancreatic beta-cell effects of the virus. Chest imaging and ECG were performed as part of the workup. The working diagnosis was viral pneumonia secondary to COVID-19 with de novo diabetes mellitus.

He was managed with prone positioning, supplemental oxygen, dexamethasone, bronchodilator and mucolytic nebulization, cough syrup, a proton pump inhibitor, vitamin C, sliding-scale regular insulin with six-hourly glucose monitoring, prophylactic low-molecular-weight heparin (enoxaparin) once thromboprophylaxis was added on day 3, and incentive spirometry. Over the following days his oxygen requirement progressively decreased — from 10 litres on admission down to 2 litres by day 6 with saturation of 93% on room air improving to 96% on minimal oxygen — while his respiratory and hemodynamic parameters otherwise remained stable. He was discharged on day 6 on a tapering course of oral medications including a stepped-down anti-inflammatory/multivitamin regimen, cough syrup, and continued incentive spirometry with advice to use supplemental oxygen as needed to maintain saturation above 90%.
