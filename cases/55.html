45-Year-Old Woman with Recurrent Right-Sided Weakness — Acute Ischemic Stroke (Left Internal Capsule and Basal Ganglia)

Source case log: https://12avirnenivaishnavi.blogspot.com/2021/06/45-year-old-female-with-hemiparesis.html

Case Narrative

A 45-year-old woman was brought to casualty with involuntary movements and weakness of the right upper and lower limbs. Five years earlier she had experienced a similar episode of right-sided weakness, during which she was unable to stand, had slurred speech, and altered sensorium, including a period of four to five days during which she failed to recognize family members. She recovered gradually over six months of medication and returned to walking without support and performing her usual activities, although her daughter had noticed she dragged her leg while walking.

On the day of presentation she again developed weakness of the right upper and lower limbs, this time associated with involuntary movements and slurred speech, occurring early in the morning. There was no history of involuntary micturition or loss of consciousness during this episode. Her past history was notable for hypertension of four years' duration and diabetes diagnosed three months earlier, both on medication.

On examination she was conscious, coherent, and cooperative, with slurred speech but normal higher mental functions and intact cranial nerves; gait could not be assessed given her acute presentation. Motor examination showed normal bulk, reduced tone in both right-sided limbs, and reduced power (3/5 on the right versus 4/5 on the left), with absent reflexes throughout the right upper and lower limbs. Finger-nose coordination was preserved, and sensory examination was normal.

Given the acute right hemiparesis with slurred speech in a patient with vascular risk factors and a prior similar event, the working diagnosis was cerebrovascular accident secondary to ischemic stroke, subsequently characterized on imaging as an acute infarct involving the left internal capsule and basal ganglia. She was managed with dual antiplatelet therapy, a statin, B-complex supplementation, insulin according to glucose monitoring, and a cerebral vasodilator/calcium channel agent, with antihypertensive medication withheld pending further assessment given the acute stroke.
