62 Year Old Woman with CKD on Maintenance Hemodialysis — Hypertensive Crisis with Hypoxia

Source case log: https://102-crazymandadi.blogspot.com/2021/10/a-62-year-old-female-patient-came-to.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

A 62-year-old daily-wage laborer presented with pedal edema, decreased urine output, and shortness of breath over four days. Seven years earlier she had been diagnosed with hypertension after presenting with giddiness and fever, and was started on atenolol by a local practitioner. A year before this admission she developed pedal edema, facial puffiness, and abdominal distension, which were attributed to her hypertension at the time. Four months before admission she developed grade IV breathlessness with worsening edema, distension, and facial puffiness; ultrasound at that time revealed underlying kidney disease, and she was referred for further care, also reporting decreased appetite from that point.

She was subsequently transferred from a dialysis unit with breathlessness and sweating. During this admission, while undergoing dialysis, she developed a hypertensive emergency with blood pressure of 210/130 mmHg, managed in a stepwise fashion with nicardipine and clonidine, then furosemide and labetalol, and finally nitroglycerin, with blood pressure gradually brought down to 140/90 mmHg over the course of treatment. She concurrently developed hypoxia (oxygen saturation 80%), managed initially with BiPAP (improving saturation to 90%) despite arterial blood gas showing mild acidosis, before being transitioned to supplemental oxygen once stabilized. Her background included hypertension for seven years on medication, with no diabetes, coronary disease, asthma, tuberculosis, or epilepsy.

On examination she was conscious, coherent, and cooperative, afebrile, with a settled blood pressure of 140/90 mmHg, pulse 84 bpm, and oxygen saturation of 94% on room air. Cardiovascular, respiratory, and abdominal examinations were unremarkable. She was diagnosed with chronic kidney disease on maintenance hemodialysis with hypertension, and received three blood transfusions and three hemodialysis sessions during the documented admission window. She was managed with fluid and salt restriction, diuretics, calcium channel blockade, phosphate binders, iron and vitamin D supplementation, and — from the fourth day — a course of intravenous antibiotics (augmentin and levofloxacin) alongside nebulization for presumed respiratory involvement, continuing through the ninth documented day of treatment.
