21 Year Old Man with Vomiting, Loose Stools, and Abdominal Pain — Acute Gastroenteritis

Source case log: http://jeeharikasahu.blogspot.com/2021/02/21year-old-with-complaints-of-vomitings.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

A 21-year-old man, working as a waiter at a local restaurant and recently married, presented with a week of vomiting, two days of loose stools, and two days of abdominal pain. Two years earlier he had been hospitalized briefly for fever with thrombocytopenia. His current illness began after eating home-made fried rice, with recurrent non-projectile, watery, food-containing vomiting — four episodes on the first two days, escalating to seven or eight episodes on subsequent days. Two days before admission he developed abdominal pain, initially a burning sensation around the umbilicus that later localized to the epigastrium, along with loose, watery stools that became darker in color and occasionally blood-tinged by the morning of admission, without tenesmus.

He was thin-built and clinically dehydrated on examination, with a pulse of 120 bpm, blood pressure 120/80 mmHg, and a temperature of 101°F. Cardiovascular examination was normal and the lungs were clear. The abdomen was tender in the epigastrium with normal bowel sounds. The clinical picture — food-associated vomiting, evolving diarrhea with blood-tinging, epigastric tenderness, dehydration, and fever — was consistent with acute gastroenteritis, likely of infective origin given the temporal relationship to the suspect meal.

He was resuscitated with intravenous fluids and started on empirical intravenous ceftriaxone, antiemetics, a proton pump inhibitor, and probiotics, with oral rehydration solution encouraged between vomiting episodes. He was monitored closely for bleeding manifestations given the blood-tinged stools. The same regimen was continued on the second day of admission, and the documented record breaks off as the third day of treatment begins.
