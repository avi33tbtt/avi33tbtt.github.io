31 Year Old Man with Epigastric Pain — Acute Interstitial Pancreatitis

Source case log: http://shriyareddyk.blogspot.com/2021/03/31-year-old-male-complaints-of.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

A 31-year-old man presented with epigastric pain that began the previous night. The pain was pricking in character, radiated to the back, and was accompanied by a single episode of non-bilious, non-projectile vomiting containing food material. He denied fever, chest pain, pedal edema, constipation, breathlessness, giddiness, headache, or nausea, and had no history of diabetes, hypertension, tuberculosis, or epilepsy, nor any similar complaints in the past. He had a twenty-year history of regular alcohol use, drinking around 180 mL of whisky daily.

On examination he was conscious, coherent, and cooperative, with a blood pressure of 110/80 mmHg and pulse of 84 bpm. Abdominal examination revealed tenderness localized to the epigastric region, consistent with the character and radiation of his pain. Given the classic presentation — epigastric pain radiating to the back, in the context of significant chronic alcohol use — he was diagnosed with acute interstitial pancreatitis with underlying alcohol dependence syndrome.

He was managed conservatively with bowel rest (kept nil by mouth with nasogastric tube placement), intravenous fluid resuscitation, analgesia, antiemetics, and thiamine/vitamin supplementation, with close monitoring of vitals. Over the following days, as his pain settled, his intravenous fluid rate was gradually tapered and he was kept nil by mouth through day three. From day four, broad-spectrum antibiotic cover (metronidazole and ciprofloxacin) was added, and by day six he was tolerating oral analgesia (paracetamol) alongside a short course of lorazepam, suggesting a step-down in acuity and possible attention to alcohol withdrawal prevention. The documented record ends at day six of his admission.
