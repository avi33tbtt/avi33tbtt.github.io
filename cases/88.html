65 Year Old Woman with Fever, Abdominal Pain, and Loose Stools — Acute Gastroenteritis

Source case log: https://22bangarilaxmiraj.blogspot.com/2021/04/65-year-old-female-with-polyuria.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

A 65-year-old woman, a homemaker, presented with fever and chills for eight days, along with abdominal pain and loose stools that had begun six days earlier. Her fever was low-grade with associated chills and rigors, easing with medication, while her abdominal pain was crampy, centred in the lower abdomen, and worsened after eating. She also had two to three episodes of non-bilious, non-projectile vomiting daily, multiple large-volume watery stools without blood or mucus, and four days of burning micturition with high-coloured urine, though without frank haematuria — a combination pointing toward an infective gastrointestinal process with possible concurrent urinary tract involvement.

She had a ten-year history of type 2 diabetes and hypertension, both on regular medication, but no history of tuberculosis, asthma, epilepsy, thyroid disease, coronary disease, or stroke. On examination she was oriented and moderately nourished, with pallor but no jaundice, clubbing, or lymphadenopathy. Her abdomen was generally distended with stretch marks and old surgical scars, tender diffusely but most in the right iliac fossa, without organomegaly, shifting dullness, or a fluid thrill — findings more in keeping with an inflamed, gas-filled bowel than ascites or a mass lesion. Cardiovascular, respiratory, and neurological examinations were unremarkable.

Given her clinical picture of fever, crampy abdominal pain, and profuse watery diarrhoea without blood, together with renal function tests showing a raised creatinine and urine examination showing increased pus cells, the working diagnosis was settled on acute gastroenteritis, with inflammatory bowel disease, food poisoning, malabsorption, and pseudomembranous colitis considered and set aside as less likely. She was planned for stool studies, possible endoscopic evaluation if symptoms persisted, and antibiotic cover with a cephalosporin (cefixime); her subsequent course and outcome are not detailed in the source record.
