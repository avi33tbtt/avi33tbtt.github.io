70 Year Old Man with Fever and Dry Cough — Left Lower Lobe Pneumonia with Thrombocytopenia

Source case log: http://jeeharikasahu.blogspot.com/2021/03/70m-with-fever-since-4days-and-dry.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

A 70-year-old man, living at home, was apparently well until his son suffered a serious accident with multiple fractures eight days before admission. Distressed by this, he continued visiting his son but three days before admission had an episode of syncope lasting two to three minutes, after which he recovered and resumed his usual activities including cooking. He lived with his wife, who was also unwell. From the following day, he became progressively confused, spoke irrelevantly, and had an episode of involuntary urination, with associated abnormal picking movements. He remained able to walk independently until the day before admission.

On the day of admission he became unable to walk unaided and developed stiffness in all four limbs, with worsening confusion — disoriented to time, place, and person. He developed high-grade intermittent fever that afternoon without chills, and had an intermittent dry cough over the preceding four days, without breathlessness, chest pain, or palpitations. There was no history of seizures, headache, vomiting, loose stools, or urinary symptoms, and he had no known diabetes, hypertension, asthma, coronary disease, or tuberculosis.

On examination he was febrile (102°F) with a pulse of 110 bpm, blood pressure 120/70 mmHg, oxygen saturation 94% on room air, and normal blood glucose. He was disoriented in all three spheres with slurred speech. Neurologically, tone was increased in all four limbs, power was reduced to 3/5, and reflexes were notably asymmetric and abnormal — absent biceps and supinator jerks bilaterally, brisk (+3) knee jerks, absent ankle jerks, and bilateral withdrawal plantar responses — a pattern suggesting a diffuse encephalopathic or mixed central process rather than a single focal lesion. Cardiovascular and abdominal examination were unremarkable, and the chest showed normal vesicular breath sounds with good air entry despite the cough. Given the combination of fever, altered sensorium, and radiological findings, he was diagnosed with left lower lobe pneumonia complicated by thrombocytopenia, with the altered mental state most likely reflecting an infective/toxic-metabolic encephalopathy in an elderly patient. The documented record ends at the point of diagnosis.
