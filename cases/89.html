55 Year Old Man with Decreased Urine Output — AKI on CKD with Analgesic Nephropathy, Fungal UTI, and Cellulitis

Source case log: https://2018-21batchpgy3gmpracticals.blogspot.com/2021/08/18100006008-case-presentations.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

A 55-year-old mechanic was brought in with three days of decreased urine output, in a patient already on maintenance dialysis. His story began about three weeks earlier with sudden breathlessness that required hospitalisation and oxygen support, at which point his creatinine was found to be elevated and dialysis was advised. During preparation for a central line he suffered a cardiac arrest and was successfully resuscitated, after which his first dialysis session was performed. Around the same time he underwent aspiration of both swollen knee joints, draining a substantial volume of fluid on two occasions. After a total of six dialysis sessions he was discharged home, but soon developed reduced urine output again, prompting this readmission.

His background offered an important clue: a fall from a pole decades earlier had left him with chronic joint pain, for which he had used analgesics — most likely NSAIDs — continuously until just a month before this admission, raising strong suspicion for analgesic nephropathy as a contributor to his chronic kidney disease. He also had long-standing bilateral pedal edema, a recent ankle injury from a bike accident with associated swelling and discolouration suggestive of cellulitis, and a twenty-five-year history of occasional alcohol use.

On examination he was alert and cooperative, with clubbing and bilateral pitting pedal edema extending to the knees, and a swollen, warm, discoloured left ankle. His oxygen requirement was notable — saturating 98% only on four litres of supplemental oxygen — and he had bibasal inspiratory crackles on chest examination, in keeping with volume overload from heart failure with preserved ejection fraction, which was later confirmed on echocardiography (ejection fraction 52% with mild global hypokinesia and a mildly dilated IVC).

Blood work over two consecutive days showed worsening renal function (creatinine 5.4 rising to 5.8 mg/dl), hyponatremia, hypoalbuminemia, and a markedly elevated alkaline phosphatase at 636 IU/L. His urine was cloudy and acidic with heavy albuminuria, pus cells, and — notably — fungal hyphae, confirming a fungal urinary tract infection. Abdominal ultrasound additionally revealed gallstones and small, structurally altered kidneys consistent with chronic disease superimposed by an acute injury. Taken together, his final working diagnosis combined acute tubular necrosis on chronic kidney disease from analgesic nephropathy, a fungal urinary tract infection, right lower-limb cellulitis, heart failure with preserved ejection fraction, gallstones, and possible asymmetric rheumatoid arthritis given his joint symptoms.

He was treated with intravenous piperacillin-tazobactam for infection, oral fluconazole for the fungal UTI, fluid and salt restriction, protein supplementation to counter his low albumin, and diuretics, with close monitoring of vitals and glucose. His course over the two documented days showed a slight worsening of renal parameters despite treatment; his ultimate outcome beyond this point is not recorded in the source material.
