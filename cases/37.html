48 Year Old Woman with Severe DKA Secondary to Pneumonia and Septic Shock

Source case log: http://suryapradeep01.blogspot.com/2021/02/sjndb.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

A 48-year-old woman presented to casualty with grade IV breathlessness, uneasiness, low-grade fever with chills, and a week of on-and-off suprapubic pain radiating to both flanks, without burning micturition. On the morning of presentation she developed fever with chills and had not eaten since late morning; by afternoon she developed severe breathlessness and was admitted at another hospital, where she was diagnosed with diabetic ketoacidosis and treated with intravenous fluids and insulin before being referred for further management. She had a history of spinal surgery in 2016 following a road traffic accident with a lumbar vertebral fracture, at which time she was also diagnosed with type 2 diabetes and started on metformin. She had no known hypertension, coronary disease, or stroke.

On arrival she was tachypneic (respiratory rate 32/min) and tachycardic (pulse 123 bpm) though afebrile, with blood pressure 110/70 mmHg. Chest examination revealed coarse end-inspiratory crepitations in the left axillary and infra-axillary regions with decreased breath sounds at the left base. Investigations revealed a markedly deranged arterial blood gas with severe metabolic acidosis (pH 6.77, bicarbonate 3.8, anion gap 38.3) requiring urgent bicarbonate correction, alongside urinary ketones, a very high total leukocyte count (27,400), and HRCT chest showing peribronchial, centrally distributed ground-glass opacities in both lungs with a CT severity score of 24/25 — raising differentials of an infective interstitial pneumonia (possibly PCP or COVID-19) versus acute interstitial pneumonia, with a CO-RADS III categorization. She was diagnosed with severe diabetic ketoacidosis, most likely precipitated by an underlying pneumonic/septic process.

She was managed aggressively with bicarbonate correction, high-volume intravenous fluids, a continuous insulin infusion titrated to hourly glucose readings, broad-spectrum antibiotics, and BiPAP respiratory support with head-end elevation. Serial arterial blood gases over the following 36 hours showed gradual correction of her acidosis (pH improving from 6.77 to above 7.4). A cardiology opinion was sought for a screening echocardiogram, which showed mild left ventricular dysfunction and dilatation; a pulmonology opinion, prompted by non-resolving tachypnea, recommended azithromycin and further microbiological workup. By the first hospital day she required vasopressor support (noradrenaline and dopamine) to maintain adequate blood pressure, indicating evolving septic shock, alongside continued insulin infusion, steroids (hydrocortisone), and antimicrobial coverage extended to include antivirals (oseltamivir) and Pneumocystis coverage (co-trimoxazole) given the radiological picture.

Over the following days her respiratory status gradually improved — breathlessness decreased, fever spikes ceased, and she was weaned from BiPAP to intermittent CPAP support — though she remained on titrating vasopressor infusions to maintain adequate blood pressure through at least day four. By day five, her diabetic ketoacidosis was documented as resolved, felt to be secondary to Pneumocystis pneumonia in the context of known type 2 diabetes, with her vital signs stable, oxygen saturation maintained on room air, and vasopressor requirements being tapered. She continued on antimicrobial therapy, structured insulin dosing with 7-point glucose monitoring, and supportive measures through this point, which marks the end of the documented record.

Clinical course
  1. Admission (Day 0): Presented with severe DKA (pH 6.77) and respiratory distress; started on bicarbonate correction, IV fluids, insulin infusion, and empirical antibiotics; ABG monitored 4th-hourly.
  2. Day 1: Persistent tachypnea; cardiology and pulmonology opinions obtained; BiPAP support, head-end elevation, and HRCT-guided antimicrobial escalation; low potassium corrected.
  3. Day 2: Breathlessness decreasing; developed hypotension requiring noradrenaline/dopamine vasopressor support; antivirals and PCP coverage added; intermittent CPAP trialed.
  4. Day 3: Further improvement in breathlessness and nausea; vasopressor doses being titrated to maintain mean arterial pressure; regular diabetic diet resumed.
  5. Day 4: Continued improvement; vasopressor support ongoing at reduced rates; structured 7-point glucose monitoring instituted.
  6. Day 5: No further breathlessness or fever spikes; DKA documented as resolved (secondary to PCP pneumonia); vasopressor doses further tapered; antimicrobial and insulin regimen continued.
Outcome
At the last documented review (Day 5), the patient's DKA had resolved and her respiratory status and hemodynamics were improving on tapering vasopressor support, with ongoing antimicrobial therapy for the underlying pneumonia and structured insulin dosing.
