47 Year Old Man with Abdominal Distension and Fever — Decompensated Alcohol-Related Chronic Liver Disease with Ascites, SBP, and Hepatic Encephalopathy

Source case log: https://2018-21batchpgy3gmpracticals.blogspot.com/2021/08/18100006007-case-presentations.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

A 47-year-old man presented with abdominal distension and bilateral lower-limb swelling that had been present for six months but had worsened sharply over the preceding ten days, along with two days of intermittent high-grade fever. His history stretched back eighteen months, to a first episode of abdominal distension and leg swelling that required hospitalisation, diuretics, and abdominal paracentesis; he also had a one-time episode of hematemesis around fifty millilitres at that time, managed with band ligation of oesophageal varices. He discontinued his medications six months ago and had two further hospitalisations since, each requiring repeat large-volume paracentesis.

In the ten days before this admission his abdominal girth and leg swelling progressed from the ankles up to the knees, accompanied by high-grade intermittent fever, anorexia, fatigue, and disturbed sleep with daytime somnolence — a pattern suggestive of early hepatic encephalopathy. He gave a clear history of heavy daily alcohol use for twenty years, with a recent binge three days before admission, strongly pointing to alcohol-related decompensated chronic liver disease as the underlying process, now complicated by ascites, possible spontaneous bacterial peritonitis given the fever, and grade 1 hepatic encephalopathy.

On examination he was oriented but showed pallor, flapping tremor, and a distended abdomen with dilated veins, shifting dullness, and a positive fluid thrill; his liver span was mildly increased and his abdominal girth measured 98 cm. Laboratory testing supported the clinical picture: he was anemic with a raised white cell count, his renal function was impaired (urea 116 mg/dl, creatinine 4.8 mg/dl) with hyponatremia and hyperkalemia, and his liver panel showed low albumin and total protein with a mildly raised bilirubin, consistent with synthetic dysfunction. Ascitic fluid analysis showed a high serum-ascites albumin gradient with a raised neutrophil count, supporting spontaneous bacterial peritonitis in the context of portal hypertension, and his Child-Pugh score was calculated as class C, indicating advanced disease severity. Viral hepatitis and HIV screening were negative, keeping the aetiology anchored to alcohol.

He was managed with rifaximin and lactulose for encephalopathy, ursodeoxycholic acid, intravenous ciprofloxacin for presumed SBP, salt restriction, and daily abdominal girth monitoring, alongside proton-pump inhibition. His diagnosis was framed as decompensated chronic liver disease secondary to alcohol, with ascites, spontaneous bacterial peritonitis, and hepatic encephalopathy, with hepatorenal syndrome considered given his rising creatinine. The source record does not document his final discharge status.
