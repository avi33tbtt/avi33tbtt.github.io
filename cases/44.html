23-Year-Old Man with Bilateral Lower-Limb Weakness — Paraparesis with Disseminated Tuberculosis

Source case log: https://102-crazymandadi.blogspot.com/2020/08/23-year-old-patient-with-paraparesis.html

Case Narrative

A 23-year-old man presented with a five-day history of bilateral lower-limb weakness accompanied by tingling and numbness. He had also had a fall while getting up to urinate, requiring assistance from a family member, and gave a history of five to six episodes of non-projectile, non-bilious vomiting five days earlier. He additionally reported a gluteal abscess of five months' duration and a scrotal abscess of twenty days' duration, both of which had previously been drained surgically.

Neurological examination showed normal tone and full power in both upper limbs, but hypotonia in both lower limbs with markedly reduced power — grade 2/5 on the right and 0/5 on the left. Deep tendon reflexes were exaggerated at the knee and ankle bilaterally (3+ on the right, 2+ on the left), plantar responses were extensor bilaterally, and ankle clonus was present on the right. This combination — flaccid tone with hypotonia alongside exaggerated deep tendon reflexes and extensor plantars — was interpreted as an evolving spinal cord process rather than a pure lower motor neuron lesion.

Magnetic resonance imaging of the brain and spine was obtained given the combination of paraparesis, a history of drained abscesses, and systemic features. Imaging revealed multiple nodular exudates in the pulmonary apices suggestive of disseminated tuberculosis, together with findings on spinal imaging supporting cord involvement, and ring-enhancing lesions were noted in both cerebral hemispheres.

The working diagnosis was paraparesis due to infective spondylodiscitis at the lower lumbar spine with an associated left psoas abscess, together with tuberculous ring-enhancing cerebral lesions and a healing ulcer at the site of the previously drained gluteal abscess, in the setting of disseminated tuberculosis. He was started on four-drug anti-tubercular therapy with pyridoxine supplementation, an anticonvulsant-class agent for neuropathic pain, local wound care with topical dressing and antiseptic sitz baths, and regular repositioning to prevent pressure injury. Further investigation for an underlying immunocompromised state, including HIV testing, was considered appropriate given the extent of disseminated infection.
