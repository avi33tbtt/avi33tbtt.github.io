36-Year-Old Woman with Pedal Edema, Facial Puffiness, and Pancytopenia Under Evaluation

Source case log: original article

Case Narrative

A 36-year-old woman, a seed packer by occupation, presented with five days of bilateral pedal edema, facial puffiness, and breathlessness on exertion. Her history was notable: five years earlier she had developed heavy menstrual bleeding with clots, was found to be anemic with reduced hemoglobin, platelets, and white cell counts at that time, and required a packed red cell transfusion. She now returned with pitting pedal edema extending to the knees, facial puffiness, and gradually progressive grade II exertional breathlessness, without associated sweating, chest pain, or palpitations — a picture that, combined with her known history of multi-lineage cytopenia, raised concern for a hematological process with secondary cardiac or renal fluid overload.

She had no history of hypertension, diabetes, asthma, coronary disease, epilepsy, or tuberculosis, and had tolerated her prior transfusion without reaction. She was an occasional drinker of toddy and beer, with her last drink about a year before this presentation, and came from a low socioeconomic background with no similar family history. On examination she was thin, pale, with pedal edema and flattened nails, but no icterus, cyanosis, clubbing, koilonychia, or lymphadenopathy; her jugular venous pressure was not raised, and vitals were largely unremarkable aside from a mildly low blood pressure.

Abdominal examination revealed mild splenomegaly, a clue pointing toward a bone marrow or hemolytic process rather than a purely nutritional cause for her cytopenias. Cardiac and respiratory examinations were otherwise normal. A peripheral smear showed anisopoikilocytosis with hypochromia, microcytes, normocytes, and tear-drop cells on the red cell line, along with reduced white cells and platelets — an impression of pancytopenia. Echocardiography and abdominal ultrasound were pursued alongside chest imaging and ECG to evaluate the cardiac and structural contribution to her symptoms, and her working diagnosis was framed as pancytopenia under evaluation with dilated right atrium and inferior vena cava, suggesting an element of right heart strain possibly related to her hematological disease.

Outcome: She was started on oral iron supplementation and close vital monitoring while the underlying cause of her pancytopenia — together with the finding of a dilated right atrium and IVC — remained under evaluation; a final diagnosis was not documented in the source.
