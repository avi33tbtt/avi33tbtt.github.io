16 Year Old Boy with Right-Sided Facial Weakness — LMN Facial Palsy

Source case log: http://sravyakandala.blogspot.com/2021/02/16-yo-male-with-lmn-facial-palsy.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

A 16-year-old boy presented with a five-day history of deviation of the mouth and inability to close his right eyelid. He had been well until five days before admission, when he first noticed the mouth deviating to the left, followed twelve hours later by inability to fully close the right eye. He also described difficulty chewing food, and reported sleeping outdoors and exposure to cold roughly ten days before symptom onset. He denied fever, sore throat, altered taste, reduced salivation or lacrimation, ear infection or discharge, hearing loss, or limb weakness, and had no relevant past medical history.

On examination he was conscious, coherent, and cooperative with stable vitals. Cranial nerve examination was entirely normal except for the seventh nerve on the right: he was unable to raise his right eyebrow, had lost forehead wrinkling on that side, could not close the right eye completely, had mouth deviation to the left, and could not blow out his cheeks or whistle. Taste sensation over the anterior two-thirds of the tongue was preserved, and lacrimation and salivation were unaffected — localizing the lesion to a peripheral (extracranial) branch of the facial nerve rather than more proximally within the facial canal. Reflexes and limb power were entirely normal, with no evidence of a more widespread neurological process.

He was diagnosed with House-Brackmann grade IV lower motor neuron facial palsy, most consistent with an idiopathic (Bell's) palsy given the antecedent cold exposure and absence of other localizing features. He was managed with multivitamin supplementation, physiotherapy, and an eye patch on the right side to protect against exposure keratitis given his incomplete eyelid closure. The documented record ends at this point of diagnosis and initial management.
