Case 79: 48-Year-Old Woman with Fever, Vomiting, and Abdominal Pain — Dengue Fever with Thrombocytopenia

Source case log: https://127rohithsomani.blogspot.com/2021/06/medicine-case-discussion-9th-june-2021.html

Case Narrative

A 48-year-old woman presented with fever of five days' duration together with vomiting and abdominal pain for three days.

She had been apparently asymptomatic five days prior when she developed high-grade, intermittent fever without associated chills or rigors. Three days into the illness she developed colicky periumbilical abdominal pain along with two to three episodes of non-projectile, non-bile-stained vomiting per day, accompanied by generalized weakness and reduced appetite. She was evaluated at a local hospital, tested positive for dengue, and was referred onward given a low platelet count. She denied cough, cold, breathlessness, loose stools, bleeding gums, or skin rash, and had no known history of diabetes, hypertension, asthma, epilepsy, coronary artery disease, or tuberculosis.

On examination she was conscious, coherent, cooperative, and well-nourished, with a temperature of 100°F, respiratory rate 18/min, pulse 86/min, blood pressure 130/80 mmHg, and oxygen saturation 98%. There was no pallor, icterus, cyanosis, lymphadenopathy, or clubbing. The abdomen was scaphoid in contour with tenderness in the right hypochondrium, epigastrium, and umbilical region, without a palpable mass, and with a normal liver span; bowel sounds were present. Cardiovascular, respiratory, and neurological examinations were unremarkable.

Ultrasound of the abdomen showed gallbladder wall edema, mild ascites, and mild right hydronephrosis — findings consistent with the vascular leak phenomena seen in dengue infection. Hematology showed a lymphocytic leucocytosis pattern with thrombocytopenia, and both total and direct bilirubin were elevated. With a positive NS1 antigen test, the diagnosis of dengue fever was confirmed. She was managed with intravenous fluid resuscitation (normal saline or Ringer's lactate), a proton pump inhibitor, parenteral multivitamin supplementation, paracetamol for fever, and close vital sign monitoring with watch for bleeding manifestations, reflecting the standard supportive approach to dengue with warning signs given the gallbladder wall edema and ascites noted on imaging.
