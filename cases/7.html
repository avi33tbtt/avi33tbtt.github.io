60-Year-Old Woman with Chronic Cough, Hemoptysis, HFrEF, and Suspected Bronchogenic Carcinoma

Source case log: original article

Case Narrative

A 60-year-old woman presented with a month-long history of cough with expectoration, breathlessness, and fever, together with vomiting and loose stools for the preceding four days. She had a two-decade history of intermittent cough that had previously responded to medication, but roughly a month before this admission she developed high-grade fever with chills, low backache, and worsening breathlessness. She had been evaluated elsewhere, found to be anemic, and started on ceftriaxone, but her symptoms progressively worsened — she began coughing up blood-streaked sputum with wheeze, and over the preceding week lost a striking four to five kilograms of weight. She denied burning micturition, dark stools, or headache. Notably, both a sibling with similar chronic respiratory complaints who had died many years earlier and a second sibling with ongoing chronic cough and inhaler use raised the possibility of a familial or longstanding chronic pulmonary process.

On examination she was severely malnourished, with a body mass index of only 13.25, marked temporal and muscle wasting, pallor, clubbing, and a nail-bed sign in keeping with chronic illness. Oral findings included a shiny red tongue and a whitish patch over the soft palate, suggestive of nutritional deficiency and possible candidiasis. A firm, mobile left supraclavicular lymph node was noted — a finding that, together with a left-sided chest lesion, raised strong suspicion for a bronchogenic malignancy. Respiratory examination showed marked asymmetry, with reduced movement, dullness, and diminished air entry over the left hemithorax, mediastinal shift, and coarse crepitations bilaterally — a pattern consistent with a large left-sided mass lesion with associated collapse and possible pleural involvement. Abdominal findings of distension, everted umbilicus, and shifting dullness raised the possibility of portal hypertension or ascites from a separate or contributory process.

Arterial blood gas showed a mixed picture with metabolic acidosis and mild hypoxemia. Echocardiography demonstrated moderate left ventricular systolic dysfunction with regional wall hypokinesia, moderate tricuspid regurgitation with pulmonary arterial hypertension, and mild valvular regurgitation — establishing a background of heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF). Chest imaging showed bilateral lung collapse with a probable cavitary lesion in the right upper lobe and mediastinal shift. High-resolution CT of the thorax and a lung biopsy from the left upper lobe mass were pursued to characterize the lesion, ultimately staged as T3N3M0 — locally advanced disease with nodal involvement. Renal function tests showed mild impairment with elevated urea, creatinine, and uric acid.

Her hospital course extended well over ten days. She was managed with nebulization (ipratropium, later with budesonide), antipyretics, nutritional and vitamin support (vitamin E, B-complex), antitussive syrup, and intravenous fluids. Vomiting and diarrhea settled within the first few days, but she had recurring fever spikes through the second week, for which she was started on intravenous amoxicillin-clavulanate. Her fever gradually subsided over the following days alongside her respiratory symptoms, and RT-PCR testing for COVID-19 returned negative partway through the admission.

Outcome: By the last documented days, her fever had largely subsided (down to ~99°F) and her cough was improving on continued antibiotics, nebulization, and supportive care, though the malignancy workup (staged as T3N3M0) remained the dominant long-term concern; no final discharge outcome was recorded in the source.

Clinical course
  1. Admission: extensive respiratory and abdominal findings noted; ABG showed metabolic acidosis with mild hypoxemia; started on nebulization, antipyretics, head-end elevation.
  2. Chest imaging showed bilateral lung collapse with a right upper lobe cavitary lesion; lung biopsy taken from a left upper lobe mass; diagnosis revised to suspected bronchogenic carcinoma with acute gastroenteritis and oral candidiasis.
  3. Early course: two fever spikes (~100.3°F); hemoptysis decreasing; vomiting and diarrhea subsiding; vitamin supplementation and antitussive syrup added.
  4. Mid course: generalized weakness noted, vitals stable, continued supportive nebulization and supplementation.
  5. Fever recurred (up to 101°F) with cough over several subsequent days; malignancy staging returned as T3N3M0; IV fluids continued.
  6. Fever persisted with hypotension noted (BP as low as 80/50 mmHg) on one day; IV amoxicillin-clavulanate started.
  7. Over the following days, fever gradually trended down (101°F → 99°F) with continued antibiotics, nebulization (ipratropium and budesonide), and supportive care; RT-PCR for COVID-19 returned negative during this period.
Outcome: Fever and cough showed gradual improvement by the last documented days on antibiotics and supportive respiratory care, but definitive outcome of the suspected malignancy (staged T3N3M0) was not recorded in the source.
