33-Year-Old Woman with Recurrent Oral Ulcers and Skin Lesions

Source case log: original article

Case Narrative

A 33-year-old homemaker presented with recurrent painful oral erosions and skin lesions over the abdomen and back, both present for about ten months. The history, given by her daughter as the patient was unable to speak clearly herself, described a possible trigger: roughly ten months earlier she sustained a minor injury after falling from a vehicle, was treated with analgesics for about a week, and shortly afterward went through a period of emotional distress and a day of not eating following a family conflict. The following day she developed a single small, painful red lesion in the oral cavity above the molars, without any pus point, which she initially ignored. Over the following week a second lesion appeared adjacent to the first, and this pattern continued — new lesions appearing in succession as older ones healed within seven to ten days each.

She sought care from several practitioners without lasting relief. A dermatologist reassured her the condition was not life-threatening and prescribed treatment that brought about a month of relief, but the lesions recurred, this time beneath the tongue. Subsequent treatment gave only partial benefit, and she was eventually referred to a tertiary center, where the case was transferred from the dermatology team to the medicine team after some difficulty reaching consensus on pain management. She also reported occasional painful red eyes, which family members had attributed to poor sleep rather than a separate mucosal process. Family members noted that eating chicken and brinjal seemed to aggravate her lesions, while medications provided relief. There was no history of joint pain, chest pain, palpitations, breathlessness, seizures, or focal neurological deficits, and no lesions were reported in the genital or anal regions.

On examination she was conscious and oriented, without pallor, cyanosis, lymphadenopathy, clubbing, icterus, or edema. Local examination revealed multiple erythematous papules over the abdomen and posterior trunk, a curdy white vaginal discharge, and multiple erosions over the buccal mucosa and hard palate with a white-coated tongue bearing an adherent plaque on its dorsal surface — findings suggesting a possible superimposed candidal infection on top of the primary mucocutaneous process. Systemic examination, including cardiovascular, respiratory, and neurological systems, was unremarkable, with a full Glasgow Coma Scale of 15/15.

Serum electrolytes were within normal limits, urinalysis showed mild albuminuria with a few pus cells and epithelial casts, and HIV testing was non-reactive. Given the recurrent, self-limiting-but-relapsing pattern of oral and skin lesions with possible ocular involvement, the differential diagnosis centered on recurrent erythema multiforme (often secondary to herpes simplex virus), chronic aphthous ulceration, Behçet's disease, and chronic candidiasis — conditions that can overlap clinically and require careful distinction based on lesion pattern, recurrence, and associated systemic features.

Outcome: She had symptomatic pain relief after the medicine team's initial treatment (IV fluids, analgesia, and topical mucosal gel); the underlying diagnosis among the differential possibilities (erythema multiforme, aphthous ulcers, Behçet's disease, chronic candidiasis) was not finalized in the documented record.
