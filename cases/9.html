66-Year-Old Woman with Fever, Increased Stool Frequency, Nausea and Vomiting

Source case log: original article

Case Narrative

A 66-year-old woman presented with on-and-off fever, increased stool frequency, and marked nausea and vomiting, all over the preceding two days. Her stools had increased to three episodes a day, normal in consistency and without blood or mucus. Vomiting was more prominent, occurring up to ten times a day, non-projectile and non-bilious, containing food particles, and typically starting around half an hour after eating — a pattern suggesting a gastric or upper gastrointestinal irritant process rather than an obstructive one. She denied any significant weight loss, though she had experienced a similar episode three months earlier. She had no history of hypertension, diabetes, tuberculosis, coronary disease, asthma, stroke, or thyroid dysfunction. She had been a farmer but had stopped working two years prior, and used betel nut and betel leaf regularly.

On examination she was conscious and cooperative, appeared malnourished, and was mildly febrile at 100.2°F with a heart rate of 96 bpm; her blood pressure and oxygen saturation were normal, and her capillary glucose was low-normal at 83 mg/dL. There was no pallor, icterus, cyanosis, clubbing, lymphadenopathy, or pedal edema. Abdominal examination showed a generally obese contour with diffuse tenderness but no organomegaly, and cardiac and respiratory exams were unremarkable.

The clinical picture of vomiting, increased stool frequency, and fever in an older adult raised concern for volume depletion affecting renal perfusion, and she was accordingly started on aggressive intravenous fluid resuscitation alongside antiemetics, an antispasmodic, and empirical antimicrobial cover, with the working diagnosis of acute gastroenteritis complicated by acute kidney injury of an intrinsic (renal) pattern.

Outcome: Management on Day 1 focused on fluid resuscitation and symptom control for gastroenteritis with associated intrinsic AKI; the source record does not extend beyond this initial treatment day, so the ultimate outcome is not documented.
