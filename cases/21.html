85 Year Old Male with Shortness of Breath, Pedal Edema, and Decreased Urine Output

Source case log: http://mohithak.blogspot.com/2021/03/dr-mohitha-dr-supriya-dr-sanjay-dr.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

An 85-year-old man presented to casualty with a week-long history of pedal edema, shortness of breath, abdominal distension, and decreased urine output. He had a background of intermittent pedal edema over the preceding year — insidious in onset, gradually progressive, and previously controlled with medication from a local practitioner. In the week before admission, the edema had progressed from the feet up to the thighs (grade III), and he developed exertional breathlessness that progressed from grade I to grade II, associated with wheeze but without orthopnea or paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea. Abdominal distension and reduced urine output accompanied this decline. He denied fever, cough, burning micturition, chest pain, palpitations, or gastrointestinal symptoms, and had no loss of appetite or weight.

He had no known history of diabetes, hypertension, cerebrovascular disease, coronary artery disease, tuberculosis, asthma, or epilepsy. On examination he was conscious, coherent, and cooperative, with pallor, icterus, and pedal edema present, but no cyanosis, clubbing, or lymphadenopathy. He was afebrile with a blood pressure of 140/90 mmHg, pulse 96 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, and oxygen saturation of 94% on room air. Cardiovascular, respiratory, abdominal, and neurological examinations were otherwise unremarkable.

The clinical picture — longstanding pedal edema, breathlessness, and fluid retention in an elderly man — was interpreted as heart failure with preserved ejection fraction, considered secondary to underlying coronary artery disease. He was started on antiplatelet and statin therapy along with fluid restriction. The documented record ends at this point of initial workup and treatment initiation.
