Case 75: 90-Year-Old Man with Acute Right-Sided Facial Deviation and Slurred Speech — Acute Ischemic Stroke

Source case log: https://157divyasri.blogspot.com/2022/02/cbble-udhc-similar-cases.html

Case Narrative

A 90-year-old man, a farmer by occupation who had been largely homebound for the preceding 10–15 years, was brought to the casualty department with deviation of the mouth to the right side and slurring of speech, both noticed since the previous morning.

He had been apparently well until about a year prior, when he developed nocturia (10–12 times overnight) and was diagnosed with type 2 diabetes at a local hospital, after which he was started on regular medication. He awoke the prior day with new mouth deviation and slurred speech. He denied involuntary micturition or defecation, involuntary movements, dysphagia, limb weakness, fever, cough, cold, burning micturition, or breathlessness. His blood pressure recorded outside the hospital was notably elevated at 190/100 mmHg.

His only known comorbidity was diabetes of one year's duration, managed with metformin; he was not a known hypertensive, and had no history of coronary artery disease, tuberculosis, or asthma. He had occasional alcohol use and had smoked but quit five years earlier.

On examination he was conscious with slurred speech, without signs of meningeal irritation. Blood glucose was well-controlled at 129 mg%. Cardiovascular and respiratory exams were normal, and the abdomen was soft and non-tender. Neurologically, tone and power were normal and symmetric (5/5) in all limbs with 100% hand grip, and deep tendon reflexes were symmetric and present except for absent ankle jerks bilaterally, with mute plantar responses. Cerebellar signs (finger-nose and knee-heel coordination) were intact.

Given the acute-onset facial weakness and dysarthria, with a background of hypertension (noted outside) and recently diagnosed diabetes, cerebrovascular disease was the leading concern. Investigations included a hemogram, renal and liver function panels (largely unremarkable), a carotid Doppler, 2D echocardiogram — which showed trivial tricuspid regurgitation without significant valvular disease, preserved LV systolic function, and diastolic dysfunction without pulmonary hypertension — and an MRI brain, which demonstrated an acute lacunar infarct in the right corona radiata along with old lacunar infarcts in the left thalamus and left corona radiata and chronic small-vessel ischemic change, confirming an acute ischemic stroke superimposed on pre-existing small-vessel disease. The final diagnosis was acute ischemic cerebrovascular accident with upper motor neuron-type left facial palsy corresponding to the acute right corona radiata infarct. He was started on antiplatelet and statin therapy, parenteral multivitamin supplementation, and physiotherapy.
