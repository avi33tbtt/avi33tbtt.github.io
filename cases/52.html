42-Year-Old Woman with Multiple Health Events Since Birth — Complex Multisystem Case for Discussion

Source case log: https://102-crazymandadi.blogspot.com/2020/05/a-42-year-old-female-having-multiple.html

Case Narrative

This case concerns a 42-year-old woman with a complex history of multisystem health events dating back to birth, presented as an analytical discussion of a case originally documented elsewhere rather than as a standard admission record. Her history included longstanding severely reduced sleep (2 to 4 hours nightly without REM sleep), which improved with L-serine and cimetidine to 7-8 hours with restored REM sleep; recurrent urinary tract infections, considered possibly related to a prior streptococcal throat infection or an anatomical predisposition; and headaches later diagnosed as migraine with aura in her thirties, managed with triptans.

She had longstanding mood and behavioral features since childhood, including temper outbursts, anxiety, marked distress when required to eat, and a past suicide attempt, alongside features suggestive of autism spectrum and attention-deficit/hyperactivity presentations. She also described sensory sensitivity — intolerance of clothing and physical contact, with an unusual pattern of high pain tolerance but difficulty focusing through pain.

Hematologically, she had been diagnosed with hemolytic anemia of uncertain cause, with a laboratory work-up (bilirubin, hemoglobin, red cell count) that did not clearly support the leading candidate diagnosis. She had a history of multiple joint sprains from childhood and a leg fracture in her thirties, recurrent facial rashes, warts, and reactivation of Epstein-Barr virus, features which were considered together as potentially reflecting a connective tissue or autoimmune predisposition. She also described episodic swelling of the face, neck, and abdomen triggered by eating, smoking, or exertion, which improved with weight loss, fasting, and increased urination, and which responded to ribose and cimetidine; she reported an absence of sweating despite these triggers. Her gynecologic history included polycystic ovary syndrome and a prior ectopic pregnancy. She had had lung infections in childhood and again in her twenties, was vaccinated against pneumonia in her later twenties, and had experienced an episode of transient left-sided weakness with stuttering and memory loss lasting about a month, along with positional dizziness. She had recently been diagnosed with Behçet's disease, which was felt to potentially explain her migraine symptoms.

Her family history included two chain-smoking parents, a mother with fibromyalgia (raising the possibility of a similar diagnosis given overlapping symptoms and female preponderance), a father who had a heart attack at 40, a grandfather with early cardiac death, and a grandmother with a history of intraoperative awareness. Genetic risk profiling suggested elevated risk for several conditions including Alzheimer's disease, ADHD, autoimmune thyroid and other autoimmune disorders, lung cancer, cluster headache, obesity, raised intracranial pressure, diabetes, rheumatoid arthritis, and bipolar disorder. She had documented allergies to fava beans and sulfa drugs, with a severe reaction to antimalarial medication previously. She was maintained on a regimen including L-serine, ribose, cimetidine, N-acetylcysteine, and iron-folate supplementation, understood as palliative management of a presumed underlying genetic disorder without a specific cure, with blood transfusion reserved for significant hemolytic episodes.
