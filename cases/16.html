22-Year-Old Woman with CKD on Maintenance Hemodialysis, Presenting with Hypertensive Visual Symptoms

Source case log: original article

Case Narrative

A 22-year-old woman, a student preparing for a postgraduate physics admission exam, had her illness begin about five months before this encounter. Just before her exam she developed unusually heavy menstrual bleeding, was treated by an informal practitioner, and improved. The very next day, however, she began experiencing blurred vision — persistent and non-progressive throughout the day. Around the same time she experienced a personal loss in the family, which delayed her seeking specialist eye care for a few days. When she finally saw an ophthalmologist, she was told she had swelling of the retinal veins and fluid accumulation in the eye, along with anemia, and was advised to see a specialist. Due to logistics, she was taken to a different city where a family doctor practiced, but the treating ophthalmologist there was unable to fully explain her eye findings. During that visit, however, her blood pressure was checked multiple times and found to be severely elevated at around 220/100 mmHg. A general physician nearby found her urea and creatinine markedly elevated, and she was urgently started on dialysis via a central line, with a fistula planned. She underwent two months of dialysis at that facility, with some improvement in her blurred vision, before transferring closer to home for further workup and continued dialysis.

She had no prior history of similar complaints, and no known diabetes, hypertension, stroke, coronary disease, tuberculosis, or asthma before this illness. Her menstrual cycles were otherwise regular. On examination she was conscious, alert, and oriented, with pallor present but no icterus, clubbing, cyanosis, lymphadenopathy, or pedal edema noted at this visit.

The overall picture — a young woman presenting with malignant-range hypertension, hypertensive retinopathy causing visual symptoms, and markedly elevated urea and creatinine — is consistent with chronic kidney disease that had likely been silently progressing, only unmasked when severe hypertension caused acute visual symptoms. She was managed with maintenance hemodialysis and a standard renal supportive regimen including blood pressure control, phosphate binders, calcium and vitamin D supplementation, iron repletion, and erythropoietin for renal anemia.

Outcome: She was established on maintenance hemodialysis with supportive management for chronic kidney disease and renal anemia, with some improvement in her visual symptoms already noted; her longer-term outcome and underlying cause of CKD were not detailed further in the source.
