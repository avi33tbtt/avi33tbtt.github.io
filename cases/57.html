52-Year-Old Woman with Uncontrolled Blood Sugars — Diabetic Ketoacidosis Secondary to Gastroenteritis

Source case log: https://12avirnenivaishnavi.blogspot.com/2022/03/52-year-old-female-with-uncontrolled.html

Case Narrative

A 52-year-old woman who worked as a farmer presented to casualty with shortness of breath, vomiting, abdominal pain, and dyspepsia, all beginning that afternoon. Her breathlessness had progressed from NYHA grade 2 to grade 3 over the course of the day. She had three episodes of non-bilious, non-projectile vomiting containing food particles, diffuse abdominal pain associated with two episodes of loose stools that were non-bloody and non-foul-smelling, and associated dyspepsia.

She had a similar presentation nine months earlier, for which she had been admitted and treated at the same hospital. She was a known diabetic of four years' duration, managed on insulin (a mixed regimen of 17 units and 10 units), and was not known to have hypertension, asthma, epilepsy, or tuberculosis.

On examination she was conscious, coherent, and cooperative. Vitals showed blood pressure 120/60 mmHg, pulse 112/min (regular), and respiratory rate 30/min — tachypneic, in keeping with a compensatory response to acidosis. Cardiac examination was normal, respiratory examination showed bilateral air entry, and the abdomen was soft and non-tender.

Urine ketone testing was positive, and arterial blood gas analysis on admission showed severe metabolic acidosis (pH 6.94, bicarbonate 1.9), consistent with diabetic ketoacidosis, most likely precipitated by an underlying gastroenteritis given her vomiting and loose stools. She was managed with an intravenous insulin infusion titrated from an initial bolus, aggressive intravenous fluid resuscitation, gastric protection, and an antiemetic. Serial arterial blood gases over the following hours and days showed progressive correction of her acidosis, with pH and bicarbonate normalizing toward the reference range by the second and third days of admission.
