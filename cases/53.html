43-Year-Old Man with Fever and Body Pains — Dengue Fever (NS1 Positive) with Thrombocytopenia

Source case log: https://102-crazymandadi.blogspot.com/2021/11/a-43-year-old-male-farmer-by-occupation.html

Case Narrative

A 43-year-old farmer presented with four days of high-grade, intermittent fever that decreased with medication, accompanied by generalized body pains and weakness, a three-day history of constant non-productive cough, and three days of abdominal pain. He denied nausea, vomiting, loose stools, or any bleeding manifestations. He had first sought care at an outside hospital with similar complaints before being referred to this facility.

He had no past history of diabetes, hypertension, bronchial asthma, epilepsy, tuberculosis, or coronary artery disease, ate a mixed diet with decreased appetite, and drank alcohol about once weekly. On examination he was conscious, coherent, and cooperative, moderately built and nourished, without pallor, icterus, clubbing, or edema. Vitals showed a temperature of 101.2°F, pulse 80/min, blood pressure 110/90 mmHg, respiratory rate 20/min, SpO2 98% on room air, and a capillary glucose of 154 mg%. Cardiac and respiratory examinations were normal, though the abdomen was tender, and the neurological examination was unremarkable.

Laboratory work-up showed a low platelet count of 55,000/µL with an otherwise largely preserved hemogram, alongside markedly elevated liver enzymes (AST 574 IU/L, ALT 354 IU/L) with low serum albumin. Dengue NS1 antigen testing returned positive, establishing the working diagnosis of dengue fever with thrombocytopenia; imaging also raised the possibility of left upper lobe consolidation, which was carried as a query alongside the primary diagnosis.

He was managed with intravenous fluids, broad-spectrum antibiotic cover, an antipyretic regimen, gastric protection, a cough expectorant, and close monitoring for bleeding manifestations and vital signs. Over the following two days his cough decreased and he reported feeling subjectively better with no further fever spikes, and his treatment was de-escalated from intravenous to oral formulations by day three while bleeding-manifestation surveillance continued.
