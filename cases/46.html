42-Year-Old Man with Anasarca and Alcohol-Associated Liver Disease — HFpEF with Alcohol Dependence

Source case log: http://swarna46.blogspot.com/2021/03/42-m-with-alcoholic-liver-disease-with.html

Case Narrative

A 42-year-old man presented with pedal edema and scrotal swelling, each of three weeks' duration, followed by shortness of breath over the preceding two weeks. He had been asymptomatic fifteen days before presentation, when grade 3 pitting pedal edema developed without any clear aggravating or relieving factor. Breathlessness (grade 3) followed within the same period, and over the subsequent days he developed abdominal distension, scrotal swelling, facial puffiness, and pitting edema of both upper limbs. He denied nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, palpitations, syncope, fever, or cough, and had no known history of diabetes, epilepsy, thyroid disease, coronary artery disease, or asthma. His father had a history of stroke.

On examination he was conscious, coherent, and cooperative, with pallor but no cyanosis, clubbing, icterus, koilonychia, or lymphadenopathy. Vitals showed a pulse of 90/min and blood pressure 160/90 mmHg, with raised jugular venous pressure noted on general inspection. Cardiovascular and respiratory examinations were otherwise unremarkable, and the abdomen was distended without tenderness.

The combination of anasarca, raised jugular venous pressure, ascites, and a background suggestive of chronic alcohol use pointed toward alcoholic liver disease as the working diagnosis, with associated hypokalemia identified on initial work-up. He was managed with fluid and salt restriction, intravenous diuretic therapy titrated to systolic blood pressure, and parenteral thiamine and multivitamin (B-complex) supplementation to cover for possible nutritional deficiency related to chronic alcohol use, alongside gastric protection.

Over the following days his regimen was adjusted to include a combination diuretic with an aldosterone antagonist, a high-protein diet with a protein supplement given his hypoalbuminemic state, and daily weight monitoring with frequent vital sign checks. Later in his course, a short course of a benzodiazepine and a muscle relaxant were added, along with pregabalin, suggesting management of possible alcohol withdrawal features and neuropathic or myopathic symptoms alongside his fluid overload.
