60-Year-Old Woman with Chronic Back Pain and Breathlessness — Chronic Kidney Disease on Dialysis

Source case log: original article

Case Narrative

A 60-year-old woman presented with two years of right-sided lower back pain and one year of breathlessness. Her back pain had gradually worsened over time and was more pronounced with walking. By the time of this evaluation she had already begun twice-weekly dialysis two months earlier, and she also reported diffuse body pains. Her past history included an unusual ophthalmologic complication: an accidental thorn injury to her left eye years earlier that was initially asymptomatic for about three months before she noticed pus discharge from the eye, ultimately requiring removal of the eye on specialist advice. She had used eye drops frequently for six years since then. Two months before this presentation she developed pedal edema, and she had a one-year history of hypertension, without diabetes or epilepsy. Notably, she had used pain-relieving medications for six years, a detail that raises the possibility of analgesic-related nephropathy contributing to her renal decline, alongside hypertension as a more typical driver of chronic kidney disease.

On examination she was well-built, conscious, coherent, and well-nourished, with bilateral pedal edema but no pallor, icterus, cyanosis, or lymph node enlargement. Her vitals showed mild hypertension. Cardiac and respiratory examinations were unremarkable, with no murmurs or wheeze, and her abdominal exam showed no palpable liver, spleen, or mass, though bowel sounds were notably absent on this particular exam.

Investigations for renal function, electrolytes, and urinalysis were sent, culminating in a diagnosis of chronic kidney disease. She was managed with ongoing dialysis and supportive medications including an antibiotic, analgesics, and gastric protection.

Outcome: She was established on maintenance dialysis for chronic kidney disease with supportive medical therapy; the source does not document further follow-up or long-term outcome.
