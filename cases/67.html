Case 67 — 50-Year-Old Man with Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction

Source case log: https://12avirnenivaishnavi.blogspot.com/2021/06/50y-old-male-with-heart-failure.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

A 50-year-old man, previously a driver by occupation, presented with three days of shortness of breath and chest pain. His background included a left-sided hemiplegia four years earlier, at which time he was diagnosed with both hypertension and diabetes and started on regular medication; he stopped working after this event but remained functionally independent for daily activities.

On this admission, he described breathlessness (grade 2–3), chest pain, and palpitations over four days, with the breathlessness accompanied by orthopnea and paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea — both hallmark features of left heart failure. He also reported pedal edema that had been present intermittently for about a year, worse in the evenings, and nocturnal urinary frequency. He denied fever.

His COVID-19 PCR test was negative. Arterial blood gas showed a mild compensated metabolic acidosis (pH 7.34, pCO₂ 35.3, HCO₃ 18.6). Renal function and electrolytes remained within normal limits throughout admission, and complete urine examination was unremarkable. Chest X-ray and ECG were obtained, and abdominal ultrasound showed grade 1 fatty liver. The overall clinical picture — breathlessness with orthopnea and PND, pedal edema, in a patient with long-standing hypertension and diabetes, but with preserved renal function and unremarkable initial cardiac work-up — supported a provisional diagnosis of heart failure with preserved ejection fraction.

He was treated with intravenous diuretic (furosemide) therapy from day one, alongside empirical antibiotic cover (amoxicillin-clavulanate, later augmented with azithromycin), a proton pump inhibitor, and aspirin. Hydrocortisone was added on day three. Over the course of four days, he lost approximately 7 kg of fluid weight with diuresis, and his breathlessness improved significantly within two days, allowing for stabilization and eventual discharge.

Outcome: Discharged in relieved condition after four days of inpatient diuretic-focused therapy, with breathlessness resolved and significant fluid weight loss achieved. He was discharged on an ACE inhibitor, beta-blocker, diuretic, an antidiabetic agent, and multivitamin supplementation, with advice to follow up in the outpatient clinic after fifteen days and to avoid self-medication.
