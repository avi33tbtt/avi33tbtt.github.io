42 Year Old Woman with Multiple Health Events Since Childhood — Structured Differential Reasoning Exercise (G6PD and AMPD1 Deficiency)

Part 1 — Case Narrative

This case was used as a teaching exercise in patient clinical data analysis rather than a standard ward admission, and no formal examination, investigation results, or outcome were recorded. A 42-year-old woman was described as having a lifelong history of multiple overlapping problems, previously labelled as G6PD deficiency and AMPD1 deficiency, and the exercise asked students to organise her complaints into a coherent differential.

The dominant complaint was recurrent swelling of the face and abdomen going back to infancy, at one point causing a 25-lb weight gain over two weeks that resolved with fasting. The episodes were aggravated by stress, exertion, certain foods, and smoke exposure, and eased with rest and cimetidine — a pattern that raised the possibility of an angioedema-like or hemolytic process related to her underlying G6PD deficiency, in which oxidative stress from reduced NADPH production can trigger hemolysis and secondary fluid shifts.

She also reported migraine-like episodes since early childhood, accompanied by transient left-sided weakness, visual loss, stuttering, and memory lapses that resolved after a lucid interval, along with zigzag and rainbow-coloured visual aura and a history of clear nasal discharge from the left nostril. This constellation was felt to be more consistent with hemiplegic migraine than a straightforward vascular event, though structural and infectious causes were considered.

A third theme was anhidrosis and reduced urine output, present even in warm environments, again hypothesised to relate to oxidative renal stress from the underlying enzyme deficiency, with Horner syndrome and Bell's palsy raised as alternative explanations for the localised autonomic findings. No confirmatory investigations, treatment course, or outcome are recorded for this teaching case; it stands as a structured reasoning exercise rather than a documented clinical encounter.
