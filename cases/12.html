Case Narrative

This case centers on a woman with a complex, multisystem symptom picture that unfolded and was reasoned through as a diagnostic exercise rather than documented as a conventional admission. She reported frequent falls to the left side, with progressive worsening of her left foot and hand function, alongside facial rashes occurring two to four times a year. She had developed swelling, hair loss, and fatigue, and described left-sided jaw pain radiating to the face. She also had breathlessness and reduced urine output, with swelling that seemed to worsen after eating many foods or exposure to smoke. She noted a striking diurnal pattern — appearing slim in the morning but bloated by midday — along with exertional weakness and intense salt cravings, at times ingesting two to four tablespoons of salt directly when feeling unwell.

The reasoning in the source considered several possible explanations for these findings. Her prominent swelling and breathlessness were hypothesized to relate to glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency, an enzyme defect that can predispose to hemolysis and, through downstream oxidative stress, to heart failure and even kidney injury — offering one possible unifying explanation for her fluid retention. Supporting investigations considered for this line included a complete blood count (looking for anemia), liver enzymes, chest X-ray (which was noted to show left atrial enlargement), ECG (right heart enlargement), and echocardiography; she reportedly had symptomatic improvement in her swelling with cimetidine.

Her episodes of incoordination and left-sided numbness, along with what was described as migraine-type headache with aura, mild memory changes, and speech stuttering, were considered separately — attributed tentatively to low vitamin B12 levels causing nerve demyelination, though the source also flagged the possibility of an underlying structural brain process such as infection or tumor as still needing evaluation. She reportedly improved with nattokinase, an enzyme supplement, though this is not an established treatment for either vitamin B12 deficiency or structural brain disease.

Her recurrent facial rash was considered in the context of a possible Behçet's syndrome diagnosis, with autoimmune vasculitis proposed as a mechanism potentially also linked to her blurred vision and a previously failed LASIK surgery. Her reduced urine output was considered possibly related to the same oxidative stress process proposed for the G6PD hypothesis, or alternatively to a urinary infection, with urine output monitoring, urinalysis, abdominal imaging, blood tests, and cystoscopy suggested as next steps, along with increased fluid intake and antibiotics if infection was confirmed.

Outcome: No formal outcome or confirmed unifying diagnosis was documented; the source ends with a set of hypothesized mechanisms and suggested investigations for each symptom cluster rather than a resolved clinical course.
