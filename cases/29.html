52 Year Old Man with Dimorphic Anemia and Hyponatremia

Source case log: http://soumya9814.blogspot.com/2021/01/this-is-online-e-log-book-to-discuss.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

A 52-year-old shopkeeper presented with ten days of exertional breathlessness (NYHA grade II–III), cough productive of whitish, non-foul-smelling sputum for two days, and reduced sleep and appetite. He had a similar episode roughly a year earlier for which a local practitioner had started him on vitamin supplements. He denied palpitations, chest pain, orthopnea, PND, melena, pedal edema, burning micturition, weight loss, or night sweats. He was a known diabetic and hypertensive on teneligliptin and a telmisartan/metoprolol combination, ate a predominantly vegetarian diet with occasional non-vegetarian food, and did not smoke or drink alcohol.

On examination he was conscious and cooperative, moderately built, with pallor but no icterus, cyanosis, clubbing, generalized edema, or lymphadenopathy. Chest examination revealed bibasal crepitations in the infrascapular regions with otherwise normal air entry, and cardiac auscultation revealed a pansystolic murmur. Neurological examination was entirely normal. Laboratory testing revealed hyponatremia (sodium 127 mmol/L) with mild hypokalemia (potassium 3.0 mmol/L), for which he was given tolvaptan; sodium corrected to 139 mmol/L over the following two days. He was diagnosed with dimorphic anemia, thought to be nutritional in origin, complicated by dyselectrolytemia. He was treated with empirical antibiotics, iron and vitamin supplementation, insulin per sliding scale, and telmisartan continuation, with a plan for packed cell transfusion once a concurrent urinary tract infection had cleared. He was discharged on insulin (mixed and short-acting), oral hypoglycemics, tolvaptan, intramuscular vitamin B12, oral folic acid, and iron supplementation, and did well at home with regular blood sugar monitoring.

Roughly three weeks after discharge, he returned with markedly elevated home glucometer readings (around 400–509 mg/dL) despite continuing his antidiabetic medications, without other new complaints — no fever, vomiting, loose stools, pain abdomen, pedal edema, breathlessness, headache, fatigue, or tinnitus. His hypertension remained controlled on telmisartan/amlodipine, and his earlier severe hyponatremia (treated with a week of tolvaptan) had resolved, though his nutritional anemia still required ongoing correction. On this readmission he was again conscious, oriented, and cooperative, with pallor but no other abnormal findings; vitals showed blood pressure 160/90 mmHg, pulse 96 bpm, and he was afebrile with saturation of 99% on room air. Respiratory and abdominal examination were unremarkable at this visit. The documented record ends with this presentation for uncontrolled hyperglycemia.
