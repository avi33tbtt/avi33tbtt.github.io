16-Year-Old Boy with Fever and Vomiting — Viral Pyrexia with Thrombocytopenia

Source case log: https://102-crazymandadi.blogspot.com/2021/10/blog-post.html

Case Narrative

A 16-year-old boy presented to the outpatient department with a five-day history of fever and vomiting. The fever was low-grade and intermittent, without chills or rigors, and relieved with medication. He had had seven to eight episodes of non-projectile, bilious vomiting containing food particles five days earlier. There was no associated abdominal pain, loose stools, cold, cough, or breathlessness. A platelet count checked elsewhere had returned at 73,000/µL, prompting referral to this hospital.

He had no past history of diabetes, hypertension, asthma, epilepsy, or tuberculosis, and his personal history was notable only for decreased appetite, with regular bowel and bladder habits and no addictions.

On examination he was conscious, coherent, and cooperative, with no pallor, icterus, cyanosis, clubbing, or lymphadenopathy. Vitals showed a temperature of 99°F, pulse 86/min, blood pressure 100/80 mmHg, respiratory rate 20/min, and SpO2 99%. Cardiac and respiratory examinations were normal; the abdomen was soft with mild tenderness in the umbilical region and audible bowel sounds. Neurological examination was fully intact with no meningeal signs.

The combination of fever, vomiting, and thrombocytopenia in the absence of localizing signs supported a working diagnosis of viral pyrexia with thrombocytopenia. He was managed with intravenous fluids and antipyretics, along with a gastroprotective agent, and improved steadily: by the second day he remained afebrile with normalizing vital signs and no fresh complaints, and management was stepped down to oral antipyretics as needed.
