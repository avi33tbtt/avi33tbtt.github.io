80 Year Old Man with Progressive Breathlessness — Chronic Kidney Disease with Acute Exacerbation of COPD and Hypertensive Emergency

Source case log: https://22bangarilaxmiraj.blogspot.com/2022/05/a-80m-with-ckd-with-ae-of-copd-cbble.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

An 80-year-old man presented with progressively worsening breathlessness over ten days, starting at grade 2 and escalating to grade 4 after he stopped using his inhaled bronchodilators two days before admission. He had been managing his breathlessness with ipratropium-levosalbutamol inhalation, an oral theophylline-etophylline combination, and a montelukast-levocetirizine combination for the prior ten days, and also reported intermittent pedal edema over the previous four months. He denied fever, chest pain, or palpitations. His background included a childhood episode of chronic suppurative otitis media that left him with right-sided hearing loss, but no known diabetes, hypertension, tuberculosis, or epilepsy prior to this presentation.

On arrival he was markedly hypertensive at 220/110 mmHg with a respiratory rate of 30 per minute, though maintaining reasonable oxygen saturation at 97% on room air — a combination suggesting both a hypertensive emergency and a significant respiratory exacerbation occurring together. He was pale, and his abdomen was distended and tender in the right iliac fossa, lumbar, and hypochondrial regions. Chest examination confirmed the reduced air entry and wheeze expected with an acute COPD exacerbation, more pronounced on the right side.

Renal ultrasound showed small kidneys (right 6.4 x 3 cm, left 5.7 x 2.9 cm) with loss of the normal corticomedullary differentiation, consistent with underlying chronic kidney disease that had not previously been diagnosed. The final working diagnosis combined chronic kidney disease, an acute exacerbation of COPD, metabolic acidosis, and a hypertensive emergency. He was managed with fluid and salt restriction, intravenous furosemide, sodium bicarbonate supplementation (Nodosis), vitamin D and iron replacement, nebulised bronchodilators and steroids, and weekly subcutaneous erythropoietin for his renal anaemia. His subsequent course and outcome are not documented in the source record.
