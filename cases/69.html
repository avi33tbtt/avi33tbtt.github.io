Case 69 — 42-Year-Old Woman with Multiple Health Events Since Birth: A Differential/Genetic Reasoning Exercise

Source case log: https://138sanjaygeneralmedicine.blogspot.com/2020/05/general-medicine-case-presentation-by.html

This case differs from the usual admission log: rather than a single acute presentation, it is a longitudinal analytical exercise attempting to explain a patient's lifelong, multisystem symptom complex through a set of candidate genetic and metabolic mechanisms. It is presented here in the same structured format for consistency, with the caveat that much of Part 1 reflects proposed explanatory reasoning rather than confirmed diagnoses.

Part 1 — Case Narrative

A 42-year-old woman had experienced a wide array of health problems dating back to birth, prompting an extended analytical review of her case rather than a single-admission work-up. She had been jaundiced at birth, persisting for some time — a pattern that raised the possibility of a hemolytic process such as erythroblastosis fetalis, in which red cells are destroyed during pregnancy.

She had also experienced lifelong sleeplessness, which was considered in the context of possible G6PD or AMPD1 enzyme deficiency, supported by a history of anemia and symptom flares after eating fava beans or taking antiviral medications (classic G6PD triggers), together with muscle dysfunction suggestive of AMPD1 deficiency.

A hallmark feature was markedly fluctuating edema, appearing and resolving within days, triggered by emotional stress, certain foods, excessive exercise, or smoke exposure, and associated with shortness of breath and dark urine. This pattern was interpreted as consistent with G6PD deficiency causing free-radical-mediated renal injury and disturbed ion and water handling, with the associated hemolysis contributing to her fatigue, reduced red cell mass, and dark urine.

She also had a long history of neurological symptoms — delayed development, aggression, anxiety, sensory disturbances, and severe headaches preceded by aura and accompanied by numbness, vertigo, and a history of cerebrospinal fluid rhinorrhea — proposed to relate to glycine handling abnormalities and oxidative brain injury secondary to G6PD deficiency.

Additional findings included excessive body hair from age three, an unusually high pain tolerance, genital ulcers, uveitis, and increased susceptibility to opportunistic infections. Family history was notable for fibromyalgia in her mother, a heart attack in her father during his forties, and early death in a grandfather, suggesting a familial predisposition to cardiovascular disease.

Genetic testing identified several variants proposed to explain elements of her phenotype, including G6PD deficiency (Seattle-type), heterozygous AMPD1 deficiency, homozygosity for the MTHFR C677T variant (associated with reduced folate metabolism and elevated homocysteine), a WNK1 variant, and additional variants linked in the literature to peanut allergy risk, Von Willebrand disease, osteoarthritis and mood disorders, glaucoma risk, alcoholic liver disease risk, Alzheimer's risk, Crohn's disease, and autoimmune thyroiditis, among others — collectively framed as a polygenic explanation for her varied, lifelong symptom burden.

Proposed management centered on addressing the presumed underlying mechanisms: topical therapy for oral and genital ulcers and for uveitis, immunosuppressive therapy (such as infliximab) and corticosteroids for inflammatory manifestations, iron and folate supplementation for her anemia, antioxidant support, and dietary strategies (including a ribose-supplemented and ketogenic approach) aimed at reducing oxidative stress, along with avoidance of NSAIDs given her G6PD status.

Outcome: This case remained a reasoning and discussion exercise built around a complex, longstanding multisystem symptom history and a panel of genetic findings, rather than a single confirmed unifying diagnosis; proposed management was directed at her most likely underlying mechanisms (G6PD deficiency, AMPD1 deficiency, and related genetic findings) with supportive and symptom-targeted therapy.
