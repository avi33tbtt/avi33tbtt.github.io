24 Year Old Man with Decreased Urine Output and Breathlessness — Obstructive Uropathy with AKI on Dialysis

Source case log: https://22bangarilaxmiraj.blogspot.com/2022/04/a-24m-with-hydrouretronephros-and.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

A 24-year-old man, a laborer, presented with a ten-day history of decreased urine output, breathlessness, pedal edema, and fever. His background was notable for hydronephrosis identified on ultrasound three years earlier, when he first developed reduced urine output; he had otherwise been asymptomatic since. Ten days before this admission he sustained trauma to his left little finger and the back of his right foot, managed with wound dressing at a local hospital, and shortly afterward developed progressively worsening breathlessness, ankle swelling, and a continuous low-grade fever without chills. Given his rising serum creatinine, dialysis was initiated the day before this evaluation.

He had no prior history of diabetes, hypertension, asthma, epilepsy, tuberculosis, or cardiac disease, and no previous surgeries or transfusions. On examination he was conscious and comfortable, with a blood pressure of 130/80 mmHg and a regular pulse of 88 beats per minute; cardiovascular, respiratory, and abdominal examinations were unremarkable, without signs of volume overload on the documented findings. Renal function tests confirmed severe impairment, with a blood urea of 175 mg/dl and creatinine of 9.3 mg/dl, alongside a metabolic acidosis on arterial blood gas (pH 7.3). Doppler study of the left limb, presumably performed to assess vascular access for dialysis, showed no critical stenosis.

The clinical picture — longstanding hydronephrosis culminating in acute kidney injury requiring dialysis, in the setting of recent minor trauma and infection — supported obstructive uropathy with superimposed acute kidney injury as the working diagnosis. The source record ends at this point in his evaluation; his subsequent laboratory trend, further imaging, and outcome are not documented.
