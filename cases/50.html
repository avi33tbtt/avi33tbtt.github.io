35-Year-Old Man with Progressive Skin Lesions — Leprosy

Source case log: https://102-crazymandadi.blogspot.com/2021/04/1601006114-short-case.html

Case Narrative

A 35-year-old man, a daily-wage laborer with a long history of chronic smoking and chronic alcohol use spanning two decades, presented with a three-month history of painless papules with surrounding erythema. The lesions began on the face and gradually extended to the upper limbs, lower limbs, and trunk. He reported no areas of numbness, fever, or discharge from the lesions, and no genital involvement.

He initially sought care at a local pharmacy, using over-the-counter topical cream, soap, and oral medication for two months; while some lesions regressed, others persisted on the nose, earlobes, hands, legs, and trunk. Fourteen days before presentation he developed blackish discoloration of the skin over the hands and feet with peeling, cracking, ulcer formation, burning sensation, and erythema spreading across the body. In the four days prior to admission he developed continuous high-grade fever with chills and rigors, along with loss of appetite. There was no similar history in the past.

On examination, facial findings included loss of the eyebrows and a thickened earlobe, with dark erythematous lesions noted on the palate. Multiple lesions of varying size were present on the trunk, limbs, and face, with ill-defined erythematous borders and pale, hypopigmented centers showing peeling and raw areas. Scaly, hyperpigmented plaques with fissuring were noted on the feet, extending from the sole to the dorsal aspect.

The combination of chronic, slowly evolving hypopigmented and erythematous plaques with ill-defined borders, madarosis (loss of eyebrows), thickened earlobes, and palatal involvement — together with the more recent development of acute skin changes, ulceration, and systemic fever — supported a diagnosis of leprosy, with the acute skin and systemic findings raising concern for a leprosy reaction requiring further management.
