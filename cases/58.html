50-Year-Old Woman with Pedal Edema and Breathlessness — Chronic Kidney Disease on Maintenance Hemodialysis

Source case log: https://102-crazymandadi.blogspot.com/2021/12/is-online-e-log-book-to-discuss-our_22.html

Case Narrative

A 50-year-old woman from a rural area presented to the outpatient department with bilateral pedal edema of twenty days' duration and shortness of breath of five months' duration. She had been asymptomatic six months earlier, when she developed breathlessness that progressed to NYHA grade 3-4, followed by gradually progressive, pitting bilateral pedal edema. She had sought care at a local hospital, where high creatinine levels were detected, prompting referral for dialysis. She denied decreased urine output, fever, cough, or burning micturition.

She had no prior similar complaints, was a known hypertensive of one year's duration on regular medication, and was not known to have diabetes, asthma, tuberculosis, coronary artery disease, or epilepsy, with no history of prior blood transfusion or surgery. Her diet was mixed with normal appetite, bowel movements were regular, and she had no addictions or known drug or food allergies.

On examination she was conscious, coherent, and cooperative, moderately built and nourished, with pallor and bilateral pitting pedal edema over the dorsum of both feet, but no icterus, clubbing, cyanosis, or lymphadenopathy. Vitals showed she was afebrile with a pulse of 84/min, respiratory rate 22/min, blood pressure 140/90 mmHg, and SpO2 94% on room air. Respiratory examination showed normal vesicular breath sounds, cardiac examination was normal, the abdomen was soft and non-tender without organomegaly, and the neurological examination was unremarkable.

The clinical and laboratory picture supported a diagnosis of chronic kidney disease on maintenance hemodialysis, with associated bilateral pleural effusion and known hypertension of one year's duration. She was managed with twice-weekly hemodialysis sessions, fluid and salt restriction, an antihypertensive (nicardia), a diuretic, iron and phosphate-binder-type supplementation, and calcium supplementation, and received a blood transfusion for coexisting anemia during her admission.
