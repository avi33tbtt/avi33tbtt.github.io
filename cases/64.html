Case 64 — 60-Year-Old Man with Chronic Kidney Disease

Source case log: https://12avirnenivaishnavi.blogspot.com/2022/05/a-60-year-old-male-came-with-complaints.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

A 60-year-old man presented with a month of bilateral pedal edema and four days of tingling sensations in his hands and feet. He had been well until a month prior, when a febrile illness was followed by pitting edema up to the ankles, without associated breathlessness. Notably, three years earlier a routine health check-up had flagged abnormal renal function, for which he received unspecified treatment for three months before returning to asymptomatic baseline and continuing to work without further evaluation — a detail suggesting long-standing, previously under-investigated renal impairment now presenting acutely.

His past history was significant for diabetes mellitus of eighteen years' duration, initially managed with subcutaneous insulin for twelve years and four months before being switched to vildagliptin, and hypertension for four years managed with amlodipine — both established risk factors for progressive diabetic and hypertensive nephropathy.

On examination he was hemodynamically stable (pulse 90/min, respiratory rate 18/min, blood pressure 130/70 mmHg, capillary glucose 106 mg%), without pallor, cyanosis, or icterus, but with pedal edema up to the ankles. Systemic examination of the cardiovascular, respiratory, abdominal, and neurological systems was unremarkable. Investigations were sent on the day of admission, and he was diagnosed with chronic kidney disease.

Management followed a standard CKD supportive protocol: continuation of his diabetic medication (vildagliptin) and antihypertensive (amlodipine, increased to 10 mg), along with iron, phosphate binder, calcium, vitamin D, and diuretic therapy, plus dietary fluid and salt restriction.

Outcome: Managed as chronic kidney disease in the context of long-standing diabetes and hypertension, with symptomatic and supportive renal care instituted.
