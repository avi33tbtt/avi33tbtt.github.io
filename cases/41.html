45-Year-Old Male with Pedal Edema, Facial Puffiness, and Chronic Renal Failure

Source case log: https://04kavyasreekaluvala.blogspot.com/2021/08/general-medicine-e-log-aug-07082021.html

Case Narrative

A 45-year-old man presented to the outpatient department with a two-month history of pedal edema and shortness of breath. He had been well until two months prior, when he began to notice facial puffiness alongside gradually progressive, pitting pedal edema extending up to the knees. Breathlessness developed over the following month, reaching a grade 2 level of exertional dyspnea. His appetite had declined over this period, and he had stopped eating non-vegetarian food.

He had no prior history of diabetes, tuberculosis, epilepsy, or asthma, but was a known hypertensive of one year's duration. He was an occasional alcohol user with no tobacco history, and there was no significant family history.

On examination he was conscious, coherent, and cooperative, moderately built and nourished, with pallor and pitting pedal edema extending to the knees; there was no icterus, cyanosis, or lymphadenopathy. Vitals showed a pulse of 82/min, blood pressure 140/90 mmHg, respiratory rate 18/min, SpO2 98%, and a random glucose of 109 mg%. Cardiovascular, respiratory, abdominal, and neurological examinations were unremarkable, with a scaphoid, non-tender abdomen and no organomegaly.

The clinical picture — progressive pitting edema, facial puffiness, pallor, and hypertension in the absence of an alternate cause — was taken as consistent with chronic renal failure, and this became the working diagnosis. Two-dimensional echocardiography and ECG were obtained as part of the work-up. The patient underwent one session of hemodialysis.
