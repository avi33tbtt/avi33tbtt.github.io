55 Year Old Man with Abdominal Discomfort and Hypoglycemic Seizure — Suspected Malignancy with Liver and Lung Metastases

Source case log: https://22bangarilaxmiraj.blogspot.com/2022/04/55-year-old-male-with-abdominal.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

A 55-year-old man presented with three days of abdominal discomfort accompanied by belching and indigestion, followed by a single episode of involuntary movements the previous night with loss of consciousness, excessive sweating, and giddiness. He was initially taken to a local hospital where he was given intravenous dextrose (25% dextrose, suggesting a hypoglycemic episode was already suspected) before being referred onward. He also reported unintentional weight loss, and had a twenty-year history of alcohol use and smoking, having abstained from alcohol for the week before this presentation.

On examination he was conscious and oriented, but hypotensive at 90/70 mmHg with a capillary glucose of just 42 mg/dl, confirming significant hypoglycemia as the likely trigger for his seizure-like episode. He had epigastric and left hypochondrial tenderness on abdominal examination, without other focal findings. Laboratory testing showed mild anemia, deranged liver enzymes with a low total protein and albumin, and renal impairment (creatinine 2.5 mg/dl) — a pattern suggesting a systemic process rather than an isolated pancreatic or biliary issue.

Imaging proved decisive: chest X-ray showed multiple well-defined opacities in both lung fields with a widened right hilum, concerning for metastatic disease, while abdominal ultrasound revealed multiple well-defined hypoechoic lesions in both lobes of the liver, the largest around 33 by 34 mm, consistent with multiple liver abscesses or metastatic deposits, alongside incidental small bilateral renal calculi and a dilated right vesicoureteric junction. Taken together, the constellation of spontaneous hypoglycemia, weight loss, and multifocal liver and lung lesions raised strong suspicion for an underlying malignancy — either a primary tumour with liver and lung metastases, or a paraneoplastic process causing non-islet-cell tumour hypoglycemia. Further characterisation of the primary source and the patient's subsequent course are not documented in the source record.
