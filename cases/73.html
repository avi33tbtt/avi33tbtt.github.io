Case 73: 56-Year-Old Man with Generalized Lymphadenopathy and Fever (TB/HIV)

Source case log: https://12avirnenivaishnavi.blogspot.com/2022/05/56-year-old-male-with-generalised.html

Case Narrative

A 56-year-old man, a daily wage labourer, presented with abdominal pain of twenty days' duration, multiple palpable abdominal masses noticed over the preceding week, and high-grade fever with chills and rigors for one week.

Five months earlier he had developed dry cough with insidious onset and gradual progression, followed by high-grade fever with chills and rigors that responded only transiently to treatment at an outside hospital and recurred once medication was stopped. He also noted progressive weight loss and anorexia. Two months into this illness, on the advice of a government physician, he was tested for tuberculosis and HIV — both returned positive — and he was started on antiretroviral therapy and anti-tubercular treatment. He now presented with worsening periumbilical abdominal discomfort and multiple palpable masses, together with fever, chills, and rigors, but without burning micturition, cough, or breathlessness at this presentation.

On examination he was conscious, coherent, and cooperative, afebrile at the time of assessment, with normal vitals and blood glucose. He had no pallor, icterus, cyanosis, or clubbing, but had marked lymphadenopathy with multiple palpable nodes in the neck and throughout the abdomen. Cardiovascular and respiratory exams were unremarkable; the abdomen was soft and non-tender with multiple palpable lymph nodes.

The clinical picture — fever with generalized lymphadenopathy in a patient with known HIV on ART and concurrent tuberculosis treatment — raised differential considerations of disseminated/relapsed tuberculosis versus lymphoma occurring in the context of retroviral disease. He was managed symptomatically with antipyretics and multivitamins, with a surgical referral placed for further evaluation of the abdominal masses.
