42 Year Old Man with Abdominal Distension and Jaundice — Alcoholic Liver Disease with Hepatic Encephalopathy

Source case log: https://22bangarilaxmiraj.blogspot.com/2022/04/a-42m-with-abdominal-distension-since-4.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

A 42-year-old butcher presented with lethargy for a week, abdominal distension for four days, and no bowel movements for two days. His story stretched back four years, to an episode of epigastric pain that led to a diagnosis of type 2 diabetes, and ten days before this admission he had a further episode of epigastric pain for which he was started on insulin, B-complex, an antacid, ursodeoxycholic acid, and a combination proton-pump inhibitor. A week before admission he developed non-bilious, non-projectile vomiting containing food particles three times a day for two days, along with a reversed sleep-wake cycle and yellowish discolouration of his eyes — early signs pointing toward evolving liver dysfunction. Over the following four days his abdomen distended progressively to a girth of 82 cm, and for the last two days he had lost his appetite entirely and stopped passing stools.

A CAGE-style alcohol screen suggested a degree of alcohol dependence, with morning drinking ("eye-opener") present, though the patient denied ever feeling the need to cut down or feeling guilty about his drinking — findings that, together with his jaundice, ascites, and behavioural change, supported a working diagnosis of alcoholic liver disease with hepatic encephalopathy rather than a purely diabetic or biliary process.

On examination he had visible jaundice without pallor or pedal edema, was oriented but scoring E4V4M5 on the Glasgow Coma Scale (indicating mild confusion), and his abdomen showed elliptical distension with full flanks, an everted umbilicus, and faint bowel sounds, without tenderness, guarding, or a fluid thrill on the documented examination. Investigations taken a week earlier already hinted at hepatic dysfunction (bilirubin 4.2 mg/dl) and had shown roughly 100 ml of free abdominal fluid and an enlarged, coarse-textured liver on ultrasound. By the day of this admission his liver profile had worsened further — bilirubin 3.79 mg/dl with a rising direct fraction, transaminases elevated, albumin low at 2.6 g/dl, and coagulation markedly deranged (INR 1.8) — while his white cell count had risen sharply to 26,900 with a normocytic, normochromic picture and leucocytosis, and repeat ultrasound showed moderate, septated ascites with an altered liver echotexture.

The final working diagnosis was hepatic encephalopathy in the setting of alcoholic liver disease. He was treated with intravenous ceftriaxone (Monocef), a proton-pump inhibitor, rifaximin and lactulose for encephalopathy, ursodeoxycholic acid, and intravenous vitamin K to address his deranged coagulation. His subsequent course and outcome are not detailed further in the source record.
