Part 1 — Case Narrative

This entry is a second, near-identical posting of the same patient documented in Case 94 — a 42-year-old butcher with a four-year history of type 2 diabetes who presented with a week of lethargy, four days of progressive abdominal distension, and two days without bowel movements, on a background of recent vomiting, a reversed sleep-wake cycle, and jaundice. The clinical narrative, examination findings, investigation results, and treatment recorded here mirror Case 94 in full, and likely represent a duplicate or revised republication of the same case log by the same author rather than a separate patient encounter.

In short, his presentation of abdominal distension, cessation of bowel movements, jaundice, and mild disorientation (GCS E4V4M5), together with a CAGE screen suggesting alcohol use and laboratory findings of worsening hepatic synthetic function (falling albumin, rising INR to 1.8), marked leucocytosis, and progressive septated ascites on ultrasound, supported the same diagnosis of hepatic encephalopathy secondary to alcoholic liver disease. He was managed with the same regimen of intravenous ceftriaxone, pantoprazole, rifaximin, lactulose, ursodeoxycholic acid, and vitamin K. Readers are directed to Case 94 for the full narrative and structured record, reproduced here for completeness given the duplicate source posting.
