70-Year-Old Man with Chest Pain and Breathlessness — CAD with HFrEF, LAD, and Atrial Fibrillation

Source case log: http://swarna46.blogspot.com/2021/03/70-yrs-m-with-cad-wirh-hfref-with-ef-35.html

Case Narrative

A 70-year-old farmer, who had stopped working a year earlier because of chest discomfort and breathlessness, presented with a one-year history of palpitations, three months of exertional breathlessness, and chest pain that had worsened over the preceding fifteen days. His breathlessness had progressed from NYHA grade 2 to grade 4 over three months, and the chest pain was described as burning in character, accompanied by palpitations.

He also reported three months of epigastric pain associated with postprandial abdominal distension, belching, and nausea, and had been hospitalized two months earlier for abdominal pain elsewhere. He had no known history of diabetes, hypertension, tuberculosis, asthma, or epilepsy.

On admission his pulse was 96/min and blood pressure 150/100 mmHg; bilateral air entry was equal on respiratory examination, heart sounds were normal, and the neurological examination was unremarkable. Given his combination of exertional dyspnea, chest pain, and palpitations, he was worked up for underlying structural and ischemic heart disease alongside his gastrointestinal symptoms.

The working diagnosis settled on coronary artery disease with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (EF 35%), involving the left anterior descending territory, complicated by atrial fibrillation with fast ventricular rate and left bundle branch block. He was managed over several days with fluid and salt restriction, diuresis, beta-blockade, antiplatelet therapy, and gastroprotective and prokinetic agents for his epigastric symptoms, with an antispasmodic added for abdominal pain and later a muscle relaxant and a low-dose calcium channel blocker for symptom control.
