62 Year Old Man with Chest Pain and Rectal Bleeding — Stable Angina with Chronic Lower GI Bleeding

Source case log: https://22bangarilaxmiraj.blogspot.com/2022/04/62-yr-old-man-with-severe-chest-pain.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

A 62-year-old man presented with a two-year history of intermittent chest pain, alongside breathlessness and a longstanding pattern of constipation with visible blood in his stools. His gastrointestinal symptoms had started two years earlier with a single episode of around 100 ml of blood passed with stool; since then he described cyclical bleeding lasting ten to fifteen days followed by two-week symptom-free intervals, without pain, itching, or other local symptoms to suggest a straightforward haemorrhoidal source, though this remained a leading possibility given the painless, cyclical nature of the bleeding.

His cardiac history began in 2019 with a febrile illness during which diabetes was first diagnosed; he was treated with oral medication for about eighteen months before stopping six months prior to this presentation, with reportedly good glycaemic control since. In 2021 he developed severe, squeezing chest pain radiating across the right chest, triggered by exertion and relieved by rest — a pattern of stable exertional angina — for which he underwent angiography and was advised to have bypass surgery, though it is not clear from the record whether this was performed. He had smoked roughly a pack of bidis daily for twenty years before quitting three years ago under family pressure after his diabetes diagnosis, and did not drink alcohol.

Beyond the physical illness, his history captured a significant social dimension: worsening, increasingly frequent chest pain forced him to give up farming, and he had to sell his farmland due to financial strain, leaving his son as the family's sole earner on an insufficient income — a context of considerable psychosocial stress alongside his medical conditions.

On examination he was cooperative and oriented, mildly underweight at 46.7 kg, with an ejection systolic murmur noted on cardiac auscultation but no thrills; respiratory examination showed mild dyspnoea with otherwise clear, normal breath sounds, and his abdomen was soft, non-tender, and without organomegaly despite his obese habitus as documented. He was continued on his home cardiac regimen — including antiplatelet therapy, a statin, an SGLT2 inhibitor, and nicorandil — and daily glucose monitoring over several days showed generally reasonable control with occasional high readings. Progress notes over three consecutive days documented a stable clinical picture with unchanged vitals and a continuing diagnosis of stable angina, without recorded acute events during the observed period; his longer-term outcome, and whether the underlying cause of his rectal bleeding was ever established, are not documented in the source.
