27 Year Old Man with Breathlessness and Burning Micturition — Acute Kidney Injury on Chronic Kidney Disease

Source case log: https://22bangarilaxmiraj.blogspot.com/2022/05/27-year-old-male-pt-with-shortness-of.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

A 27-year-old man presented with breathlessness of grade 4 severity since that morning, along with burning micturition and bilateral flank pain, both present for three days. He had a known history of hypertension and diabetes for about a year, first identified when he was admitted elsewhere with similar complaints and diagnosed with underlying kidney disease at that time, and had been managed conservatively since.

On examination he was conscious and cooperative, mildly tachycardic at 113 beats per minute with a blood pressure of 130/100 mmHg, and his capillary glucose was mildly elevated at 136 mg/dl. Cardiovascular, respiratory, abdominal, and neurological examinations were otherwise unremarkable, without signs of fluid overload on the documented findings. Given his combination of acute breathlessness, flank pain, and urinary symptoms on a background of known chronic kidney disease, the working diagnosis was acute kidney injury superimposed on chronic kidney disease, and he was started on diuretic and antihypertensive therapy along with basal-bolus insulin coverage. The source record does not document his subsequent laboratory results, clinical course, or final outcome.
