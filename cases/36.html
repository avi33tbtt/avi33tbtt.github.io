49 Year Old Man with Resting Tremor — Parkinson's Disease vs Multiple System Atrophy

Source case log: http://sravyakandala.blogspot.com/2021/03/parkinsonsmsa-p.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

A 49-year-old lecturer presented with a two-month history of progressive, asymmetric involuntary movements of his right index and middle fingers, which he had first noticed around six months earlier as small-amplitude movements that worsened with rest and improved with activity — a resting tremor. Over the two months before presentation, the tremor had extended to involve his thumb, impairing his ability to correct exam papers and maintain hand stability, and had spread to the left hand over the preceding month. He also described progressively small, illegible handwriting (micrographia), ascending stiffness in his wrists and elbows (right greater than left), and a stooped, short-stepped gait, along with a subjective sense that his body felt far heavier than his actual weight — features suggestive of bradykinesia and rigidity.

Beyond the classical motor triad, he reported prominent autonomic symptoms: loss of morning erections and libido for two months, and erratic bowel habits alternating between urgency and multi-day constipation. He denied orthostatic dizziness, lower-limb stiffness, sensory disturbance, or knee buckling, but described marked difficulty walking in the dark, feeling he would fall without support — raising concern for a postural instability or sensory ataxia component. His brother, an informant, corroborated the history and additionally noted a loss of vocal inflection and increasingly monotonous speech over the same two months, without memory loss or behavioral change. He had no relevant past or family medical history.

On examination, supine blood pressure was markedly elevated (190/110 mmHg) and fell substantially on standing (to 160/110 mmHg at three minutes), indicating orthostatic hypotension against a background of new hypertension. Cardiac exam showed a raised JVP and displaced apex beat; ECG showed sinus tachycardia with a pseudo-infarct pattern and voltage criteria for left ventricular hypertrophy, and echocardiography suggested grade II diastolic dysfunction. Cognitive screening (MMSE 29/30) showed no impairment. Cranial nerve examination was essentially normal aside from a subtle tongue tremor. Motor examination revealed cogwheel rigidity at the right wrist, mildly reduced power in the lower limbs bilaterally (4/5 proximally), brisk knee reflexes, and a classic low-amplitude, 3–4 Hz resting tremor of the right upper limb with reduced arm swing on gait testing. Sensory examination showed mildly prolonged vibration thresholds. Cerebellar testing, including the HINTS exam, was entirely normal, and formal bradykinesia testing (toe and foot tapping) showed slowing on the right side, with positive micrographia testing.

Taken together — an asymmetric rest tremor with rigidity and bradykinesia, alongside prominent early autonomic failure (orthostatic hypotension, erectile dysfunction, bowel dysregulation) and disproportionate gait/balance difficulty — the presentation straddled two diagnostic possibilities: idiopathic Parkinson's disease (stage 1, with new-onset hypertension), and multiple system atrophy, parkinsonian type (MSA-P), given the degree of autonomic involvement disproportionate to typical early Parkinson's disease. He was started on carbidopa-levodopa combination therapy (Syndopa) along with an antihypertensive, with regular vitals monitoring. The documented record concludes with this diagnostic formulation and treatment initiation, alongside education on the generally favorable prognosis of stage 1 disease if the diagnosis proves to be idiopathic Parkinson's.
