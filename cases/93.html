42 Year Old Woman with Lifelong Migraine, Episodic Swelling, and Sleep Disturbance — Suspected G6PD Deficiency, AMPD1 Deficiency, and Related Metabolic Disorders (Reasoning Exercise)

Source case log: https://22bangarilaxmiraj.blogspot.com/2020/05/a-42-year-old-female-with-multiple.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

Like several related teaching entries in this series, this case is a structured diagnostic-reasoning exercise built from a linked real patient history rather than a first-hand admission note, working through a woman with a lifelong constellation of headaches, episodic swelling, transient neurological symptoms, sleep disturbance, and exercise-related fatigue. The exercise organised her problems by priority and worked through a distinct hypothesis for each.

Her headaches began in childhood and worsened at menarche and with hormonal contraceptive use, evolving into classic migraine — throbbing, unilateral, aggravated by movement, and at times preceded by a self-resolving aura lasting under an hour — for which triptans were considered the appropriate treatment. Her episodic swelling of the face, neck, and abdomen, present since age two and triggered by emotional stress, exercise, or smoke exposure, was hypothesised to reflect haemolytic crises related to an underlying G6PD deficiency, an X-linked enzyme defect that impairs the production of NADPH needed to keep red cell membranes protected from oxidative stress, leading to intravascular haemolysis when triggered by oxidant drugs, infections, or fava bean ingestion; peripheral smear findings of Heinz bodies and bite cells with a negative Coombs test were noted as the expected supporting picture, distinguishing it from an autoimmune haemolytic process.

Left-sided numbness and weakness, which began at age 34, were interpreted as a possible hemiplegic migraine variant, sometimes accompanied by a spinning sensation and a subjective tight mass feeling in the neck and jaw despite unremarkable imaging. Her lifelong sleep deprivation — historically only two to four hours nightly — was tentatively linked to low NADPH and glycine levels together with a possible AMPD1 (adenosine monophosphate deaminase 1) deficiency, with L-serine suggested as a glycine-mimicking supplement that might help. Her exercise-related fatigue, muscle pain, and cramping were similarly attributed to AMPD1 deficiency, in which excess adenosine accumulation is thought to drive exercise intolerance, with oral ribose proposed as a potential energy-substrate treatment.

The exercise also raised two genetic considerations as possible unifying threads: an MTHFR (methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase) mutation, which can raise homocysteine and lower folate and B12, and has been linked to migraine, digestive symptoms, and peripheral neuropathy, treatable with folate, B12, and methionine supplementation; and a WNK mutation, associated with familial hyperkalemic hypertensive syndrome (pseudohypoaldosteronism type II) through effects on chloride cotransporter regulation. Hair loss and possible cervical spine or hip degeneration were also mentioned as unexplained associated findings, with juvenile idiopathic arthritis or spondylosis raised as possibilities. No formal examination, investigation results, or outcome are documented; the entry functioned purely as a differential-reasoning framework built from a linked external case report.
