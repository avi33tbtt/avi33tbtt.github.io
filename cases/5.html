Case 5 — 46-Year-Old Man with HFrEF, Type 4 Cardiorenal Syndrome, DCMP, and Ischemic Hepatitis

Source: jessusanjay.blogspot.com — 46 yr old male with HFrEF with type 4 cardio renal syndrome

Case Narrative

A 46-year-old man presented with a 20-day history of abdominal distension, shortness of breath, and bilateral pedal edema — a triad that, taken together, pointed toward a congestive process affecting both the heart and the fluid balance of the body. His background gave an important clue to the chronicity of his renal involvement: six years earlier he had developed a thorn-prick injury to the left foot that progressed to swelling, probably due to necrotizing fasciitis, and required haemodialysis. After four to five sessions of dialysis he had remained well for the following six years, suggesting that his renal function had stabilised, at least until this new presentation.

Twenty days before admission he noticed swelling in both legs along with abdominal distension, of insidious onset and gradually progressive, associated with shortness of breath that improved somewhat with medication before he was referred for further evaluation. He denied decreased urine output, chest pain, palpitations, or symptoms of paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea or orthopnea, which helped narrow the differential away from an acute decompensation and more toward a subacute congestive process. His past history included the prior dialysis-requiring renal injury, along with known hypothyroidism and hypertension, the latter on irregular medication — a detail that likely contributed to both his cardiac and renal vulnerability.

On examination he was conscious, coherent, and cooperative, afebrile, with a pulse of 84/min, blood pressure 160/100 mmHg, and oxygen saturation 98% on room air. There was pitting pedal edema present for 20 days, but no pallor, icterus, cyanosis, clubbing, koilonychia, or lymphadenopathy. Cardiovascular examination showed a non-elevated jugular venous pressure, no parasternal heave, and an apex beat felt 0.5 cm lateral to the mid-clavicular line in the sixth intercostal space — a leftward and downward displacement suggestive of cardiac enlargement, consistent with a dilated cardiomyopathy. Heart sounds were normal without murmurs. The abdomen was distended, with a centrally inverted umbilicus and no engorged veins, scars, or visible pulsations; percussion revealed dullness over the flanks with a shifting quality and a fluid thrill, confirming ascites, while bowel sounds were normally present. Neurological examination, including higher mental functions, was unremarkable.

An ascitic tap was performed as part of the workup for his fluid overload. Putting the pieces together — a dilated, poorly contracting heart (DCMP) driving forward failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF), congestion of the kidneys through reduced renal perfusion and venous congestion (type 4 cardiorenal syndrome, in which chronic heart failure drives progressive kidney dysfunction), and passive hepatic congestion from right heart failure (ischemic hepatitis) — the treating team arrived at a working diagnosis of HFrEF with type 4 cardiorenal syndrome, dilated cardiomyopathy, and ischemic hepatitis.

He was managed with diuretics, a nitrate, sodium bicarbonate supplementation (nodosis), iron and calcium supplementation, and antiplatelet therapy with aspirin and clopidogrel, along with a statin; a beta-blocker (metoprolol) was added from the first day of the recorded inpatient course. This treatment regimen was continued essentially unchanged through the following day. The documented record ends here, with the patient established on this combined cardiac, renal-protective, and hepatic-supportive regimen; his subsequent course and final outcome are not recorded in the available source.
