45 Year Old Woman with Lower Backache and Left Hip Weakness — Chronic Osteomyelitis Under Evaluation

Source case log: http://nikhilreddykooluru.blogspot.com/2021/02/40f-with-lower-backache-under.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

A 45-year-old daily-wage worker presented with three months of progressive weakness in the left lower limb. The illness began three months earlier with high-grade, continuous fever with chills, unresponsive to medication, along with occasional dry cough and a few episodes of vomiting and loose stools. At a local hospital she was diagnosed with bilateral pneumonia, newly detected type 2 diabetes, and anemia, and improved over a 20-day admission on imipenem-cilastatin and amikacin, receiving blood transfusions during that stay. After discharge, she developed left lower-limb weakness, pain in the left hip with crepitus on walking, and progressive difficulty walking — eventually needing support and then becoming unable to walk even with assistance, and unable to rise from bed unaided. She also developed lower backache and pitting pedal edema extending to the knee, unresponsive to medication.

She had no prior history of hypertension, tuberculosis, asthma, epilepsy, stroke, or coronary disease, and diabetes had been newly diagnosed during the earlier admission, now managed with glimepiride/metformin combination. She drank toddy occasionally, and her elder brother had diabetes. On examination she had pedal edema but no pallor, icterus, cyanosis, clubbing, or lymphadenopathy, was afebrile with a pulse of 84 bpm, blood pressure 130/90 mmHg, and blood glucose of 182 mg/dL. Neurological examination showed reduced power around the left hip and knee (as low as 1/5 in some hip movements) with pain limiting full assessment, while power elsewhere in the limbs was largely preserved (4+/5); reflexes were symmetric and normal, sensory and cerebellar function were intact, and there was tenderness over the lumbosacral spine. Abdominal examination revealed a small reducible umbilical hernia but was otherwise unremarkable.

Given the localized hip pain, crepitus, preceding severe infection, and imaging changes, the working diagnosis was lower backache under evaluation, with chronic osteomyelitis or infective arthritis of the left hip versus lumbar disc disease as differentials, in a patient with new-onset diabetes. She underwent extensive imaging including pelvic and hip X-rays, spine films, MRI of both hips, echocardiography, and abdominal ultrasound. She was managed with analgesia (tramadol), antiemetics, and glycemic control, and her hip pain gradually improved over the following days, though tenderness over the left sacroiliac joint and anterior thigh persisted, with pulse rising transiently to 102 bpm on one review. By the fourth hospital day she was ultimately transferred to the orthopedic department for definitive management of chronic osteomyelitis of the hip.
