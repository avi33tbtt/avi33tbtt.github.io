Case 71: 45-Year-Old Woman with Pupil-Sparing Third Nerve Palsy

Source case log: https://12avirnenivaishnavi.blogspot.com/2022/04/45-year-old-with-pupil-sparing-3rd.html

Case Narrative

A 45-year-old woman, a tailor by occupation, presented with a three-day history of headache accompanied by a dragging sensation in both eyeballs, along with vomiting. One day prior to presentation she developed drooping of the right eyelid and double vision in the right eye. A month earlier she had been evaluated for diminished vision and prescribed corrective glasses by an ophthalmologist; shortly after starting to use them she developed the severe headache and ocular dragging sensation, for which she was given analgesics at another hospital. The following day she developed right eye swelling, ptosis, diplopia, and non-bilious, non-projectile vomiting.

She denied sudden-onset limb weakness or dizziness, and had no known history of diabetes, hypertension, tuberculosis, asthma, or stroke.

On examination she was conscious and oriented, afebrile, with a blood pressure of 130/90 mmHg, pulse 56/min, and normal oxygen saturation on room air. Cranial nerve examination revealed a normal left third nerve but a right third nerve palsy — lateral rectus function preserved while medial, superior, and inferior rectus functions were absent — with pupillary reactivity spared. This constellation is characteristic of a pupil-sparing third nerve palsy, a pattern classically associated with a microvascular (ischemic) rather than compressive cause, since pupillomotor fibers travel superficially in the nerve and are typically affected early by external compression (e.g. an aneurysm) but relatively preserved in ischemic infarction of the nerve core. Motor power, tone, and deep tendon reflexes in all four limbs were normal and symmetric, with flexor plantar responses and no neck stiffness.

She was managed with intravenous fluids, a proton pump inhibitor, broad-spectrum antimicrobial cover, antiemetics, and analgesia, with regular vitals monitoring. The working diagnosis at the time of this record was pupil-sparing right third nerve palsy; further outcome was not documented in the available source.
