Case 62 — 18-Year-Old Man with Uncontrolled Sugars, De Novo Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus

Source case log: https://12avirnenivaishnavi.blogspot.com/2022/05/18-year-old-male-with-uncontrolled.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

An 18-year-old man, whose parents worked as stone cutters and who had helped them from a young age, presented with a year-long history of weight loss and inability to gain weight despite eating regularly, having lost roughly 5–8 kg over that period. He also described increased appetite — eating six to eight meals a day — and polyuria with two to three episodes of nocturnal urination. He denied fever, cough, cold, bleeding manifestations, dark stools, palpitations, breathlessness, or pedal edema. This constellation of weight loss with polyphagia and polyuria is the classic triad pointing toward uncontrolled hyperglycemia.

His past history was notable only for a possible febrile seizure at age two; there was no family history of diabetes, hypertension, tuberculosis, asthma, or epilepsy. He was born by full-term normal vaginal delivery, with a history of parental consanguinity, and had delayed developmental milestones, walking only at three years of age. His diet was carbohydrate-heavy and rice-based across all meals, with occasional dal, leafy vegetables, and rare meat.

On examination he was conscious and coherent, without pallor, icterus, cyanosis, clubbing, lymphadenopathy, or edema. Vitals were essentially normal (temperature 98.3°F, pulse 98/min, respiratory rate 18/min, blood pressure 100/80 mmHg, SpO₂ 98% on room air), but his random blood glucose was markedly elevated at over 450 mg/dL. Systemic examination — cardiovascular, respiratory, abdominal, and neurological, including higher mental functions — was unremarkable.

A broad metabolic work-up was sent, including arterial blood gas, urine ketones, renal and liver function, electrolytes, hemogram, complete urine examination, thyroid profile, chest X-ray, and serology (which was negative), consistent with a new evaluation of markedly deranged glycemic control. He was diagnosed with uncontrolled sugars secondary to newly detected Type 1 diabetes mellitus and managed with intravenous fluids, an insulin infusion titrated by hourly glucose monitoring, sliding-scale correction doses, antiemetic cover, and strict input–output charting.

Outcome: Managed acutely as new-onset Type 1 diabetes mellitus with intravenous fluid resuscitation and an insulin infusion protocol guided by hourly glucose charting.
