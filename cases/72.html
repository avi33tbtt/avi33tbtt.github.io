Case 72: 70-Year-Old Woman with Uncontrolled Blood Sugars Post-Thyroidectomy

Source case log: https://12avirnenivaishnavi.blogspot.com/2022/04/70-year-old-female-with-uncontrolled.html

Case Narrative

A 70-year-old woman, a labourer by occupation, presented with a four-day history of non-radiating lower backache aggravated by work and relieved by rest, diffuse abdominal pain, grade 2–3 exertional breathlessness of ten days' duration, and ten days of generalized itching and dry cough.

She had been a known diabetic for twenty years, originally diagnosed incidentally during evaluation for fever, and was on an oral sulfonylurea-metformin combination. Five years earlier she had developed a thyroid swelling and was diagnosed with hypothyroidism, treated with levothyroxine; the swelling progressively enlarged and she underwent a total thyroidectomy roughly four years prior to this admission. She denied pedal edema, reduced urine output, burning micturition, or fever.

On examination she was conscious and afebrile, with a blood pressure of 130/80 mmHg and oxygen saturation of 97% on room air; her random glucose was unrecordably high ('Hi' on glucometer). She had pallor but no icterus, cyanosis, clubbing, or lymphadenopathy. Cardiovascular and respiratory exams were unremarkable, and the abdomen was soft and non-tender.

Initial workup showed markedly elevated blood glucose (454 mg/dl) with an elevated HbA1c of 7.6%, mild anion-gap metabolic derangement on arterial blood gas (pH 7.38, bicarbonate 14.5–17.3), hyponatremia (Na⁺ 131), and mild anemia (Hb 9.9 g/dl) with a low total protein and albumin. Urine ketones were negative, arguing against diabetic ketoacidosis despite the markedly elevated sugars and low bicarbonate — consistent instead with a hyperglycemic state without significant ketosis. She was managed with basal-bolus regular insulin dosed before meals with a seven-point glucose profile.

Over the following days her glucose readings remained labile — ranging from marked hyperglycemia in the 300–370 mg/dl range to a hypoglycemic dip to 54 mg/dl on one occasion overnight, prompting escalation to two-hourly monitoring. Repeat assessments through day 4 continued to show poorly controlled sugars in the 240–370 mg/dl range despite insulin titration, and monitoring was intensified accordingly. The final working diagnosis was uncontrolled blood sugars in a patient with longstanding type 2 diabetes and prior total thyroidectomy for hypothyroidism.
