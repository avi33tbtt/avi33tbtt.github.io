Case 68 — 18-Year-Old Woman with Abdominal Pain, Fever, and Constipation Under Evaluation

Source case log: https://12avirnenivaishnavi.blogspot.com/2022/04/a-18yr-old-married-women-who-is.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

An 18-year-old married woman, working as a laborer, presented with a week of constipation, abdominal pain, grade 3 shortness of breath, and fever. Her illness began two weeks before presentation with burning micturition that resolved with a week of medication. Roughly a week before this admission, she developed insidious, gradually progressive, squeezing-type abdominal pain associated with nausea and abdominal distension, the distension partially improving with medication given by a local doctor. She also developed constipation with obstipation (absence of both stool and flatus, raising concern for possible bowel obstruction), grade 3 breathlessness without orthopnea or PND, and fever with associated headache and body pains but no chills or rigors, which settled with medication.

She had no known diabetes, hypertension, asthma, or epilepsy. Her personal history noted inadequate sleep and decreased appetite. Her menstrual history recorded a last menstrual period on day 16 of that month, menarche at age 11, previously irregular cycles that had become regular after treatment, bleeding for four to five days per cycle associated with pain and clots. She had been married for one and a half years in a non-consanguineous marriage.

On examination she was conscious, coherent, and cooperative, afebrile at the time of assessment (98.8°F), with blood pressure 110/70 mmHg and pulse 98/min. Systemic examination showed normal heart sounds and bilateral air entry, with abdominal examination revealing tenderness in the left hypochondrium, epigastrium, and left iliac region, though bowel sounds were present.

Investigations showed mild anemia (hemoglobin 9.1 g/dL) with a peripheral smear showing mild anisopoikilocytosis, microcytic hypochromic cells, and occasional pencil forms — a pattern consistent with iron deficiency — alongside a mildly raised white cell count (11,000) with neutrophilic predominance. Renal function and electrolytes were normal, and complete urine examination showed only a few pus and epithelial cells without albuminuria or glycosuria. Liver function tests were essentially normal aside from a mildly raised alkaline phosphatase. Serum lipase and amylase were within normal range, effectively arguing against acute pancreatitis despite the epigastric component of her pain. Urine pregnancy test and serology were negative. Imaging with erect abdominal X-ray, chest X-ray, abdominal ultrasound, and 2D echocardiogram were obtained as part of the broader evaluation for her multifocal abdominal tenderness, fever, and breathlessness.

Outcome: The evaluation for her abdominal pain, fever, and breathlessness remained in progress at the time of this record, with pancreatitis, gynecological, and infective causes being worked through; iron deficiency anemia was also identified as a concurrent finding.
