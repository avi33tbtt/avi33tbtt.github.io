Case 74: 85-Year-Old Man with New-Onset Seizure on a Background of Chronic Cognitive Decline

Source case log: https://12avirnenivaishnavi.blogspot.com/2022/05/60-year-old-male-with-seizures.html

Case Narrative

An 85-year-old man was brought in with a staring look and frothing from the mouth followed by upward rolling of the eyeballs, along with three days of thick oral secretions.

According to his daughter, he had previously run his own business but developed alcohol dependence that eventually forced him to stop working. Approximately ten years prior to this presentation he began to lose the ability to retain new information, withdrew from his usual activities, became dependent on being fed and cared for, and stopped drinking alcohol around that time. Three years ago he had an episode of generalized tonic limb movements lasting about thirty minutes with frothing, associated with involuntary micturition and defecation and followed by postictal confusion; he was treated at a hospital with unspecified medication for two years, with the episode at the time attributed to a possible hyponatremic cause. On the day of this presentation he developed the acute staring spell and oral frothing with upward eye deviation, without loss of consciousness, vomiting, or involuntary micturition this time.

He was not a known diabetic, hypertensive, or tuberculosis patient. On examination he was stuporous, afebrile, with stable vitals and a normal oxygen saturation. There was no pallor, icterus, cyanosis, clubbing, or lymphadenopathy. Cardiovascular, respiratory, and abdominal examinations were unremarkable. Neurological examination showed normal speech (when assessable) and no meningeal signs, with normal tone and full power in all four limbs, but reflexes were slightly exaggerated and brisk (3+) bilaterally at the biceps, triceps, supinator, knee, and ankle jerks — a pattern suggesting an upper motor neuron process superimposed on his baseline chronic cognitive decline.

Given his decade-long progressive memory impairment, remote seizure history, and current presentation, the working diagnosis was seizures with a possible underlying left-sided acute ischemic process affecting the frontal and occipital lobes, in the context of long-standing cognitive decline of uncertain prior characterization (a differential that would include alcohol-related dementia given his history, though this was not formally established in the record). He was managed with levetiracetam for seizure control, parenteral multivitamin supplementation, and nebulization for his respiratory secretions.
