15 Year Old Girl with Easy Fatigability — Iron Deficiency Anemia

Source case log: http://sruthi995.blogspot.com/2021/04/a-15yr-old-female-came-with-complaints.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

A 15-year-old student presented with five to six months of easy fatigability, associated with giddiness on exertion lasting two to three minutes and relieved by rest, along with generalized weakness. She had had a brief low-grade fever ten days earlier that resolved with medication. She denied pedal edema, breathlessness, rectal bleeding, dark stools, altered bowel habits, or weight loss. Notably, she had required a blood transfusion six years earlier for low hemoglobin. Her diet was mixed with a good appetite and no pica. Menstrual history was significant: menarche at age 12 with initially regular cycles, but over the past year she had developed irregular cycles with heavier and longer bleeding (five days duration), without clots or overt menorrhagia — a plausible ongoing source of iron loss in an adolescent girl.

On examination she was conscious, coherent, and cooperative with normal vitals. She had marked pallor and koilonychia (spoon-shaped nails), both classic signs of iron deficiency. Cardiovascular and abdominal examination were unremarkable, and neurological examination was normal. Laboratory findings were consistent with microcytic hypochromic anemia, confirming a diagnosis of iron deficiency anemia — attributable to a combination of dietary factors typical of Indian diets (predominantly non-heme iron with poor bioavailability) and ongoing menstrual blood loss in the setting of adolescent growth demands.

She was started on oral iron replacement (ferrous ascorbate/iron polymaltose combination). The documented record ends with treatment initiation and a discussion of the pathophysiology of nutritional iron deficiency.
