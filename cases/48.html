47-Year-Old Man with Anasarca — Suspected Diabetic Nephropathy with Heart Failure

Source case log: https://102-crazymandadi.blogspot.com/2021/11/47-yr-male-with-ckd-diabetic.html

Case Narrative

A 47-year-old man who worked as an auto-rickshaw driver presented with pedal edema and facial puffiness of six days' duration, worsening shortness of breath over the same period, and reduced urine output over the preceding three days. His history stretched back eight years, when he was found to be hypertensive on a routine check-up; six years earlier he had developed generalized weakness and weight loss and was diagnosed with diabetes; and five years earlier he had an episode of acute pancreatitis.

Over the six days before admission, pedal edema progressed gradually to the knees alongside facial puffiness, with decreasing urine output and worsening breathlessness — advancing from NYHA grade 2–3 to grade 4 over the final three days, now accompanied by orthopnea and paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea. His appetite had also declined. He denied chest pain, palpitations, giddiness, fever, cold, cough, or burning micturition.

He was a known hypertensive on an angiotensin-receptor blocker and a known diabetic on a sulfonylurea-metformin combination, with a past episode of acute pancreatitis; he had stopped both alcohol and smoking five years earlier and was not known to have tuberculosis, epilepsy, asthma, or coronary artery disease. On examination he was conscious, coherent, and cooperative with pedal edema but no pallor, icterus, cyanosis, clubbing, or lymphadenopathy. Vitals showed a pulse of 94/min, blood pressure 180/100 mmHg, SpO2 97% on room air, and a capillary glucose of 166 mg/dl. Cardiac examination was normal; the chest showed bilateral basal and axillary crepitations, and the neurological examination was unremarkable.

Given his long-standing diabetes and hypertension presenting with volume overload, orthopnea, and reduced urine output, the differential lay between heart failure and diabetic nephropathy as the cause of chronic kidney disease, and both were carried as working diagnoses. Serology was negative and his admission glucose was 105 mg/dl. He was managed with fluid and salt restriction, intravenous loop diuretic (pressure-dependent dosing), gastric protection, insulin according to glucose monitoring, an ACE inhibitor and a beta-blocker for afterload and rate control, antiplatelet and statin therapy, and supplemental oxygen as needed, with strict monitoring of blood pressure, glucose, intake/output, and daily weight.
