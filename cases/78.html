Case 78: 42-Year-Old Woman with Multiple Health Events Since Birth — Analytical Discussion (G6PD Deficiency, Hemiplegic Migraine, and Genetic Findings)

Source case log: https://169sharathchandrareddy.blogspot.com/2020/05/general-medicine.html

Case Narrative

This entry is an analytical discussion of a 42-year-old woman with a complex, longstanding multisystem history dating back to birth, built around a set of documented findings and genetic testing results rather than a fresh admission record. The recurring theme was profoundly fluctuating edema — varying within days — with identified triggers including emotional stress, certain foods, excessive exercise, and smoke exposure, and spontaneous resolution between episodes. Episodes were also associated with shortness of breath and dark-coloured urine.

This pattern of edema, breathlessness, and dark urine was interpreted as suggestive of G6PD (glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase) deficiency, in which oxidative stress from various triggers — including recurrent urinary tract and pulmonary infections, antimalarial and sulfa-drug exposure, and fava bean ingestion — precipitates hemolysis. The reasoning offered was that resultant free-radical damage impairs renal tubular ion transport and water balance, driving the edema, while hemolysis-related anemia would account for the fatigue, breathlessness, and dark urine. Management strategies discussed included avoidance of oxidative triggers, pneumococcal vaccination to reduce infection-triggered hemolytic episodes, antioxidant-rich diet, and blood transfusion during acute hemolytic phases.

A separate, longstanding neurological symptom complex was also described, beginning with delayed early development and including aggression, anxiety, sensory disturbances, and severe headaches, with features suggestive of hemiplegic migraine — unilateral motor weakness, headache, visual and sensory aura, fever, variable impairment of consciousness, ataxia, nausea/vomiting, and photophobia/phonophobia — together with chronic sleep disruption (reduced REM sleep, short total sleep duration).

Family history was notable for fibromyalgia in the patient's mother, a paternal heart attack in the fourth decade of life, and early death in a grandparent. A panel of genetic findings was documented and used to build an explanatory framework for the patient's various symptoms, spanning variants related to oxidative and purine metabolism, folate/homocysteine handling, connective tissue and bleeding-disorder risk, psychiatric and substance-dependence susceptibility, and several complex-trait risk alleles (thyroid autoimmunity, glaucoma, hepatic fat accumulation, Alzheimer's risk modification, and inflammatory bowel disease susceptibility, among others). The discussion also proposed links between these genetic findings and additional issues raised by the patient, including excess body hair from an early age, dysmenorrhea, a prior ectopic pregnancy, ovarian cysts, and increased pain tolerance.

On the basis of this synthesis, an extensive empirical and largely nutraceutical management approach was suggested: a ribose-supplemented diet, L-serine for sleep, oxidative stress reduction, B-complex and antioxidant vitamin supplementation, iron and folate supplementation, cimetidine (reported to have helped with swelling), N-acetylcysteine, and dietary modification including a ketogenic approach with added salt and fat. The overall assessment was that the patient was doing better with ongoing symptomatic treatment, but that complete physical and neurological re-examination together with further imaging was recommended to guide any escalation of her treatment plan.
