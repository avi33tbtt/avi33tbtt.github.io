Case 76: 56-Year-Old Man with Progressive Easy Fatigability — Iron Deficiency Anemia

Source case log: https://169sharathchandrareddy.blogspot.com/2021/04/1601006187-short-case-final-practical.html

Case Narrative

A 56-year-old man, a sales manager by occupation, presented with easy fatigability of thirty days' duration.

He had been apparently well until a month prior, when he began to notice fatigue on exertion that was insidious in onset and gradually progressive — his exercise tolerance declining from being able to walk 500 metres to only about 100 metres by the time of presentation. This was accompanied by exertional breathlessness and generalized body aches. He denied chest pain, cough, or fever, and had no known history of diabetes, hypertension, asthma, or coronary artery disease.

On examination he was conscious, coherent, and cooperative, moderately built and nourished. His pulse was 94/min with a blood pressure of 100/70 mmHg and a respiratory rate of 29/min. Pallor was present, but there was no icterus, cyanosis, koilonychia, lymphadenopathy, or edema. Cardiovascular and respiratory examinations were essentially normal, and the abdomen showed no organomegaly with normal bowel sounds; neurological examination was unremarkable.

The combination of progressive exertional fatigue and breathlessness with pallor on a background of no other systemic symptoms raised nutritional anemia as the leading differential, most likely iron deficiency anemia given the demographic and presentation. Investigations planned included a complete blood picture and stool and urine examinations to evaluate for a source of iron loss. He was started empirically on an iron-rich diet and oral ferrous sulfate supplementation.
