65 Year Old Man with Compressive Myelopathy, Acute Temporal Infarct, and Vascular Dementia

Source case log: http://nikhilreddykooluru.blogspot.com/2021/03/this-is-online-e-log-book-to-discuss.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

A 65-year-old man, formerly a handloom weaver and drama artist, presented with a five-year history of lower backache and a progressive decline in mobility and cognition. Two years before admission, following his mother's death, he had a four-month period of low mood and depression. Eighteen months before admission he developed sudden difficulty rising from a squatting position; an MRI at that time reportedly showed possible infarcts, but follow-up was deferred because of COVID-19 lockdown restrictions. Since then he had progressively needed a walking stick, developed difficulty rising from sitting, and showed memory loss with reduced participation in conversation and difficulty expressing himself, without sleep disturbance. One week before admission he developed high-grade fever with chills and rigors, a productive cough he could not expectorate (leading to swallowed sputum) with associated breathlessness, reduced oral intake, difficulty swallowing, and drooling of food and saliva.

He had type 2 diabetes for 5 years on glimepiride/metformin and hypertension for 10 years on cilnidipine, was a heavy long-term smoker (50 years, tapering from 1.5 packs to 3–4 beedis/day), and drank alcohol occasionally. On admission he was disoriented to time, place, and person, febrile-range tachycardic (pulse 103 bpm), with a blood pressure of 130/90 mmHg and oxygen saturation of 95% on room air. Neurologically, he had slurred speech, a right facial hemiparesis with mouth deviation to the left, increased tone in the upper limbs, reduced power (3/5 upper limbs, 2/5 lower limbs), and generalized hyperreflexia (+3) with extensor plantar responses bilaterally — a pattern combining upper motor neuron signs with cognitive decline. Sensory and cerebellar testing could not be assessed given his disorientation.

He was diagnosed with compressive myelopathy, an acute infarct in the left anterior temporal lobe, suspected spondyloarthropathy, and vascular dementia (likely multi-infarct dementia), superimposed on an acute infective illness. He was managed with nasogastric (Ryles tube) feeding, intravenous fluids, empirical antibiotics (ceftriaxone), antiplatelet therapy, glycemic control with insulin, and supportive measures including pressure-area care for evolving bed sores. His fever and cough resolved over the first several days, though his sensorium remained impaired — he stayed disoriented to time, place, and person, with unchanged neurological deficits — through at least three weeks of inpatient care, during which he required ongoing tube feeding, physiotherapy, glycemic titration, and antithrombotic and lipid-lowering therapy for his presumed cerebrovascular disease.

  3. Day 3: Fever and cough subsided; sensorium remained impaired; continued antibiotics, tube feeding, and physiotherapy.
  4. Day 4: Fever/cough remained resolved; ongoing supportive care and physiotherapy of the lower limbs.

Outcome
At the last documented review, fever and cough had resolved but sensorium remained impaired (disoriented to time, place, and person) with persistent right-sided weakness and hyperreflexia; management continued with tube feeding, physiotherapy, and secondary stroke prevention.
