20 Year Old Woman with Anasarca and Decreased Urine Output — Nephrotic Syndrome with Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus

Source case log: https://22bangarilaxmiraj.blogspot.com/2022/05/nephrotic-syndrome-with-type-i-dm.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

A 20-year-old woman, a homemaker, was brought to hospital with decreased urine output since the previous day and swelling all over her body for four days. Two months earlier she had a similar episode of facial and leg swelling that led to hospitalisation and treatment, after which she was discharged; this current presentation represented a more severe recurrence, with generalised anasarca and near-anuria. She had a known history of type 1 diabetes mellitus, an important detail given the well-recognised link between long-standing type 1 diabetes and diabetic nephropathy, which can present with nephrotic-range proteinuria and progressive renal impairment.

On examination she was conscious, coherent, and cooperative, afebrile, but markedly hypertensive at 160/80 mmHg with bilateral basal crackles on lung auscultation, suggesting volume overload from her renal fluid retention. Cardiovascular, abdominal, and neurological examinations were otherwise unremarkable. Serial urine and blood investigations were performed across several days, though individual values are not detailed in the source record. She was managed intensively with high-dose diuretics, an antibiotic, an angiotensin receptor blocker, erythropoietin, scheduled insulin, a calcium channel blocker, strict fluid intake-output charting, and intravenous albumin given for her presumed hypoalbuminemia — a treatment pattern consistent with severe nephrotic syndrome, most likely secondary to diabetic nephropathy in the context of her type 1 diabetes. Her subsequent laboratory trend and outcome are not documented in the source record.
