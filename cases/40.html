37 Year Old Man with Fever and Petechiae — Suspected Leptospirosis with Thrombocytopenia

Source case log: http://swarna46.blogspot.com/2021/02/dengue-feverleptispirosis-alcoholic.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

A 37-year-old daily-wage laborer presented with five days of fever and generalized weakness, and four days of loss of appetite. The fever was high-grade with chills occurring two to three times a day, transiently relieved with medication, and was accompanied by myalgia that had prompted an earlier visit to a local hospital where he received symptomatic treatment. He had two episodes of non-bilious, non-bloody vomiting with food content over five days, and three episodes of loose stools the day before admission, non-bloody and non-foul-smelling. He denied abdominal pain, distension, pedal edema, breathlessness, orthopnea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea, or burning micturition, and had no known hypertension, diabetes, stroke, coronary disease, tuberculosis, asthma, or epilepsy. His background included fifteen years of alcohol use (180 mL/day) and heavy tobacco chewing (two packets daily).

On examination his vitals were stable — pulse 76 bpm, blood pressure 110/80 mmHg — with a barrel-shaped chest noted, and petechiae present over both upper limbs, suggesting a bleeding tendency in keeping with thrombocytopenia. Cardiovascular and respiratory examination were otherwise unremarkable. Given the combination of high fever with chills, myalgia, gastrointestinal symptoms, petechial bleeding, and a background of heavy alcohol use, the differential diagnosis spanned alcoholic hepatitis and leptospirosis, both under evaluation in the context of thrombocytopenia.

He was managed with thiamine supplementation (reflecting his alcohol use), intravenous fluids, a proton pump inhibitor, antiemetics, and close monitoring for bleeding manifestations. From the second day, empirical broad-spectrum antibiotic cover (ceftriaxone) was added along with a hepatoprotective agent (ursodeoxycholic acid), continuing unchanged through the third documented day, with ongoing vigilance for bleeding and strict fluid balance monitoring. The documented record ends at this point in his evaluation.
