Case 61 — 55-Year-Old Man with Chronic Kidney Disease on Maintenance Hemodialysis

Source case log: https://102-crazymandadi.blogspot.com/2021/12/55-year-old-male-patient-with-ckd-on-mhd.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

A 55-year-old man, an agricultural labourer from a rural area, presented with shortness of breath and facial puffiness, each present for about a year. His history stretched back three years, when he first noticed bilateral pitting pedal edema that gradually progressed. He was found to be hypertensive at a local hospital and started on medication, though he took it irregularly.

About ten months prior to this presentation, the facial puffiness became more pronounced — worse in the mornings and easing over the course of the day, a pattern classically seen with nephrotic or renal fluid-retention states — along with breathlessness at rest. Evaluation at that time revealed a markedly elevated creatinine and shrunken kidneys on imaging, and he was started on dialysis.

He had no known diabetes, asthma, tuberculosis, coronary disease, or epilepsy, and no prior surgeries. He was a moderate, occasional alcohol user and had smoked for about twenty years, both of which he had stopped roughly ten months before this presentation — coinciding with the diagnosis of his kidney disease.

On examination, he was pale, with bilateral pitting pedal edema extending up to the knees, but no icterus, clubbing, cyanosis, or lymphadenopathy. Vitals were largely stable — blood pressure 140/90 mmHg, pulse 84/min, respiratory rate 20/min, saturating 98% on room air. Systemic examination of the respiratory, cardiovascular, abdominal, and neurological systems was unremarkable, without organomegaly or focal deficits.

Given the established diagnosis of chronic kidney disease on maintenance hemodialysis, management focused on supportive and dialysis-related care: he received a unit of packed red cell transfusion for his anemia, was placed on fluid and salt restriction, and was continued on antihypertensives, a loop diuretic, phosphate binder (nodosis), calcium supplementation, and iron replacement.

Outcome: He was managed as an established CKD patient on maintenance hemodialysis, with correction of anemia by transfusion and continuation of his dialysis and supportive medical regimen.
