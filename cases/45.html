50-Year-Old Woman with Polyarticular Pain and Stiffness — Rheumatoid Arthritis

Source case log: https://102-crazymandadi.blogspot.com/2021/04/1601006114-long-case.html

Case Narrative

A 50-year-old homemaker presented with a one-year history of pain and stiffness in multiple joints, on a background of symptoms that had actually begun a decade earlier. She had first developed a dull, aching pain and stiffness in the metacarpophalangeal joints of the right hand, with restricted movement. Within six months this had spread to involve the wrist and elbow joints of both hands, and within four years the feet and ankles were also affected. Over the three months prior to presentation, the pain had become severe enough to limit her daily activities.

The pain was insidious in onset and slowly progressive, dull and aching, non-radiating, and associated with swelling and stiffness in the affected joints. Characteristically, stiffness and pain were worst in the first hour after waking and improved gradually with movement — a pattern of prolonged morning stiffness. She had experienced occasional exacerbations associated with fever, but denied joint deformities, weight loss, involvement of the distal interphalangeal joints, a facial rash, or abnormal jerky movements.

She had no relevant past medical history and no history of thyroid disease, asthma, hypertension, or diabetes. Her menstrual history was notable for menarche at 13, previously regular 29-day cycles, and menopause at 47. On general examination she was moderately built and nourished, without edema, icterus, cyanosis, lymphadenopathy, or pallor, and vitally stable with a blood pressure of 120/80 mmHg.

Local examination of the joints showed soft-tissue swelling without deformity, warmth, mild capsular swelling, and tenderness on the squeeze test, with decreased range of active and passive movement at the proximal interphalangeal, metacarpophalangeal, wrist, elbow, and ankle joints — all painful on motion. There were no extra-articular manifestations affecting the eyes, ears, muscles, or salivary glands, and systemic cardiovascular, respiratory, neurological, and abdominal examinations were unremarkable. Radiographs of the affected joints showed decreased joint space, periarticular osteoporosis, and mild erosions. Serology showed a strongly positive rheumatoid factor and a positive C-reactive protein with an elevated ESR, while anti-CCP antibodies were negative. Taken together, this presentation — symmetric small- and large-joint polyarthritis with prolonged morning stiffness, radiographic erosive change, and a strongly positive rheumatoid factor — supported a diagnosis of rheumatoid arthritis over osteoarthritis, which had been considered as an alternative. She was started on corticosteroid therapy together with an analgesic for symptom control.
