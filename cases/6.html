42-Year-Old Man with Alcohol Dependence Syndrome and Alcoholic Liver Disease

Source case log: original article

Case Narrative

A 42-year-old man, a farmer by occupation, was brought to the casualty with irrelevant talk, giddiness, and epigastric pain that had all begun that same morning. He had a long history of heavy alcohol use — starting at roughly 90 mL of whisky a day two decades earlier and escalating to a full bottle (about 720 mL) daily over the last ten years. His most recent drink, 180 mL, had been consumed just an hour before he presented.

This was not his first such presentation. He had been admitted with similar episodes of confusion and giddiness before, and only a week prior to this admission he had been diagnosed with alcoholic liver disease with alcohol dependence syndrome after presenting with comparable symptoms and a documented severe hypoglycemia (capillary glucose of 20 mg/dL) at that time — a pattern consistent with alcohol-induced impairment of hepatic gluconeogenesis compounding chronic malnutrition.

On this admission he was confused with irrelevant talk. His vitals showed a mildly low blood pressure (90/60 mmHg) with a normal pulse, and his blood glucose on this occasion was within normal range (111 mg/dL), suggesting his sensorium changes were more likely related to acute intoxication and/or early withdrawal rather than hypoglycemia this time. Abdominal exam revealed epigastric tenderness, in keeping with alcohol-related gastritis, with normal bowel sounds and no peritonism.

Given his level of chronic dependence and the risk of withdrawal, a psychiatry referral was taken for alcohol withdrawal management. He was managed with intravenous fluids, thiamine (to pre-empt Wernicke's encephalopathy, a standard precaution in chronic heavy drinkers, especially before glucose-containing fluids are given), a proton pump inhibitor for gastritis, antiemetics, analgesia, and scheduled lorazepam for withdrawal prophylaxis, along with baclofen for anti-craving support. He was monitored closely with strict intake-output charting.

Outcome: The documented course ends with the patient established on supportive treatment for alcoholic liver disease and alcohol dependence syndrome, with withdrawal prophylaxis and psychiatric involvement in place; no further outcome beyond this point was recorded in the source.
