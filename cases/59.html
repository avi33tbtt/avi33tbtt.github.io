57-Year-Old Man with Bilateral Pedal Edema — Chronic Kidney Disease on Maintenance Hemodialysis

Source case log: https://102-crazymandadi.blogspot.com/2021/12/is-online-e-log-book-to-discuss-our.html

Case Narrative

A 57-year-old man from a rural area presented to the outpatient department with bilateral pedal edema of two months' duration. He had been asymptomatic before this, then developed gradually progressive, pitting bilateral pedal edema. He had gone to a hospital elsewhere, where high creatinine levels were found, and had four sessions of dialysis over one week before transferring to this hospital for continued dialysis care. He denied decreased urine output or shortness of breath.

He had no prior similar complaints, was a known hypertensive of two months' duration on medication (nicardia), and was not known to have diabetes, asthma, tuberculosis, coronary artery disease, or epilepsy, with no prior surgical history. He had a twenty-year history of both alcohol use and smoking, both of which he stopped two months earlier, coinciding with the onset of his current illness.

On examination he was conscious, coherent, and cooperative, moderately built and nourished, with bilateral pedal edema but no pallor, icterus, clubbing, cyanosis, or lymphadenopathy. Vitals showed he was afebrile with a pulse of 84/min, respiratory rate 20/min, blood pressure 140/90 mmHg, and SpO2 98% on room air. Respiratory examination showed normal vesicular breath sounds, cardiac examination was normal, the abdomen was soft and non-tender without organomegaly, and the neurological examination was unremarkable.

The clinical picture supported a diagnosis of chronic kidney disease on maintenance hemodialysis. He was managed with fluid and salt restriction, an antihypertensive (nicardia), a diuretic, iron supplementation, and a phosphate-binder-type agent alongside calcium supplementation, continuing the dialysis regimen begun at the outside hospital.
