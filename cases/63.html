Case 63 — 50-Year-Old Man with Uremic Encephalopathy Secondary to NSAID-Induced Nephropathy

Source case log: https://12avirnenivaishnavi.blogspot.com/2021/06/50y-male-with-uremic-encephalopathy.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

A 50-year-old man presented with decreased urine output for fifteen days, loss of appetite for a week, and shortness of breath with facial puffiness, nausea, and vomiting, also for about fifteen days. His story began two years earlier, with low back ache and pain in the knee, elbow, and wrist joints, for which he repeatedly self-medicated with over-the-counter analgesics from local pharmacies, obtaining only transient relief and continuing this pattern intermittently for roughly two years — a history strongly suggestive of chronic analgesic (NSAID) exposure.

Despite this, his symptoms never fully resolved, and he went on to develop reduced urine output together with non-bilious, non-projectile vomiting over the preceding two weeks. He was told at a local facility that his kidneys were affected and was referred for further evaluation. On admission, urine albumin was 3+, serum creatinine was markedly elevated at 16.6 mg/dL, blood urea was 214 mg/dL, and hemoglobin was low at 7.14 g/dL. Renal ultrasound the following day showed bilateral grade 2 chronic kidney disease changes, and he was diagnosed with CKD secondary to NSAID-induced nephropathy.

He underwent hemodialysis on the first day of admission and was started on antihypertensive, diuretic, iron, erythropoietin, and phosphate-binder therapy. By day two he developed generalized itching, attributed to uremic pruritus, and was started on an antihistamine. A second dialysis session was performed on day four.

On day five, he developed two episodes of involuntary clonic movements of the right upper and lower limbs, without associated incontinence, accompanied by tongue bite — a seizure. Following the episode he was irritable and disoriented to time, place, and person, with a blood pressure of 140/80 mmHg and pulse of 108/min. Neurological examination showed increased tone and power on the left side compared to the right, with reflexes that could not be elicited. This clinical picture — new-onset seizure with altered sensorium in the setting of severe uremia — was consistent with uremic encephalopathy, and he was started on intravenous and then oral levetiracetam.

His sensorium improved gradually: by day six he was oriented to person but not time or place; a third dialysis was performed on day eight; and by day nine he was fully conscious and oriented to place and person. He had one febrile spike on day nine that responded to paracetamol.

Outcome: The seizure, attributed to uremic encephalopathy, resolved with dialysis and anticonvulsant therapy, and his sensorium normalized by day nine, alongside ongoing management of CKD secondary to NSAID-induced nephropathy and uremic pruritus.
