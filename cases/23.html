60 Year Old Female with AKI on CKD

Source case log: http://hemachowdary.blogspot.com/2021/05/60-years-old-female-with-aki-on-ckd.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

A 60-year-old woman presented with left loin pain, decreased urine output, and burning micturition, each of 10 days' duration, along with one episode of low-grade fever and two episodes of vomiting. Her background was notable for a right nephrectomy performed 20 years earlier, after she was told her right kidney had failed and that removal was needed to protect the remaining left kidney. Seven years prior she had undergone a hysterectomy for suprapubic pain attributed to a cervical lesion. She had a further similar episode of pain six years ago that was managed conservatively. She had known hypertension for 5 years on treatment, no diabetes, tuberculosis, or asthma, and reported decreased appetite over the preceding 10 days.

On examination she was conscious, coherent, and cooperative, obese, with a fixed flexion deformity of the knee and flattened nails noted, but no cyanosis, clubbing, or lymphadenopathy. She was afebrile with a pulse of 86 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, blood pressure 150/90 mmHg, oxygen saturation 96% on room air, and a random blood glucose of 160 mg/dL. Abdominal examination was unremarkable — soft, non-tender, with no organomegaly or palpable masses, and normal bowel sounds. Given her solitary kidney and ten-day history of loin pain, reduced output, and urinary symptoms, the picture was consistent with an acute kidney injury superimposed on chronic kidney disease, most likely from a urinary tract source affecting her single functioning kidney.

She was managed with output-matched intravenous fluids and intravenous furosemide when systolic pressure permitted, alongside strict fluid balance monitoring and temperature charting. The documented record ends at this point in her admission.
