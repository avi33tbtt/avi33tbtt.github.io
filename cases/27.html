50 Year Old Woman with Backache and Quadriparesis Under Evaluation

Source case log: http://nikhilreddykooluru.blogspot.com/2021/03/this-is-online-e-log-book-to-discuss_15.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

A 50-year-old female laborer presented with a 20-day history of backache and a 10-day history of progressive inability to use all four limbs. Two months before admission she had developed sudden-onset, gradually progressive backache that initially responded to medication at a local hospital, but recurred 20 days before admission and became unresponsive to further treatment. Ten days before admission she developed proximal lower-limb weakness, sudden in onset, which progressed in an ascending-then-generalizing pattern — proximal lower limbs, then distal lower limbs, then proximal upper limbs, then distal limbs. She had difficulty combing her hair, feeding herself, buttoning clothes, squatting and rising from squatting, climbing stairs, wearing footwear, lifting her head off the pillow, and rolling over or getting up from bed. There was no breathing difficulty and no diurnal variation in weakness, though she noted temporary relief after tramadol injections. Her past history included a total abdominal hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy for dysfunctional uterine bleeding in 2009; she had no diabetes, hypertension, tuberculosis, asthma, epilepsy, coronary disease, or stroke.

On examination she was afebrile (99°F) with stable vitals, conscious and oriented, with intact higher mental functions and cranial nerves. Motor examination showed symmetric mild weakness (4-/5) in both upper and lower limbs with normal tone, and — notably — normal, symmetric deep tendon reflexes throughout, intact sensation, normal cerebellar function, and no spinal tenderness or meningeal signs. This combination of proximal-predominant, symmetric weakness with preserved reflexes and normal sensation raised concern for a myopathic process, while an underlying bony or vertebral lesion — including the possibility of multiple myeloma given the backache — was also considered given her age and pain pattern.

She underwent a broad workup including full blood counts, urine examination, arterial blood gas, liver and renal function tests, thyroid profile, serology, chest and sacroiliac joint X-rays, ECG, abdominal and neck ultrasound, and MRI of the brain and whole spine. She was treated with pain relief, muscle relaxants, and vitamin supplementation, and received a pulse of intravenous methylprednisolone partway through her stay. Over the following days her backache steadily improved with unchanged neurological findings, and she was discharged on Day 5 (calendar day 17/3) with continued oral analgesia, muscle relaxants, laxatives, and a physiotherapy plan for both lower limbs.

  3. Day 3: Similar findings; treatment escalated with a pulse of intravenous methylprednisolone.
  4. Day 4: Backache continuing to decrease; neurological exam unchanged; treatment simplified with oxazepam and analgesics.

Outcome
Discharged on Day 5 with backache substantially improved; neurological weakness (4-/5, symmetric) persisted at discharge without further deterioration, on a home physiotherapy and medication plan. Final diagnosis among the working differentials was not confirmed in the source.
