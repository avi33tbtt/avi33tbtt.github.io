Case 70 — 45-Year-Old Man with Pedal Edema and Breathlessness — Chronic Renal Failure

Source case log: https://12tejaswini.blogspot.com/2021/08/general-medicine-case-1.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

A 45-year-old man presented with two months of bilateral leg swelling and shortness of breath. He had been asymptomatic four months prior to that, when he first developed gradually progressive, pitting bilateral pedal edema below the knee, along with grade 2 breathlessness and leg pain. At that time he sought care and was diagnosed with a kidney problem and started on medication.

Six days before this admission, he presented to the casualty department with worsening leg swelling and breathlessness, at which point dialysis was advised; he underwent three dialysis sessions in the days leading up to this record. He had known hypertension for one year but no diabetes, thyroid disease, asthma, or epilepsy, and no history of prior surgery or blood transfusion. His family history included hypertension in his father and both diabetes and hypertension in his mother, without a family history of stroke, coronary disease, asthma, or thyroid disorder.

On examination he was conscious, coherent, and cooperative, with pedal edema but no pallor, clubbing, koilonychia, or lymphadenopathy. Vitals were stable: temperature 37.5°C, pulse 80/min, respiratory rate 16/min, blood pressure 110/80 mmHg. Cardiovascular examination showed a symmetric chest wall without precordial bulge, visible pulsations, or engorged veins, with the apex beat felt in the left fifth intercostal space in the mid-clavicular line and normal heart sounds. Respiratory examination showed a centrally placed trachea with bilateral air entry and normal vesicular breath sounds. The abdomen was distended but soft and non-tender, with normal bowel sounds and no palpable mass or free fluid. Neurological examination was normal, with intact sensory and motor function and no meningeal signs.

Given the low hemoglobin (6.6 g/dL) reflecting the anemia of chronic kidney disease, he underwent packed red cell transfusion. Renal function tests confirmed significantly elevated blood urea (97 mg/dL) and serum creatinine (7.5 mg/dL), consistent with advanced renal failure, alongside mildly elevated phosphorus (6.0 mg/dL) and alkaline phosphatase (283 IU/L) reflecting renal osteodystrophy-related mineral derangement; sodium, potassium, and chloride were within normal limits. He was provisionally diagnosed with chronic renal failure and managed with a standard CKD supportive regimen: antihypertensive therapy, phosphate binder, iron supplementation, erythropoietin, intravenous iron sucrose, and loop diuretic, along with fluid and salt restriction.

Outcome: Managed as chronic renal failure requiring maintenance dialysis (three sessions completed by the time of this record), with anemia corrected by transfusion and ongoing renal supportive therapy instituted.
