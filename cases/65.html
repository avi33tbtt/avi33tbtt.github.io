Case 65 — 52-Year-Old Man with Decompensated Chronic Liver Disease, Portal Hypertension, and Venous Thromboembolism

Source case log: https://12avirnenivaishnavi.blogspot.com/2022/05/52-year-old-male-with-portal-htn.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

A 52-year-old man, chronically alcoholic for thirty years and a chronic smoker for forty, presented with bilateral pitting pedal edema extending to the thighs over three months and exertional breathlessness over the preceding week. His illness had a long and complicated antecedent course. Four years earlier he first developed jaundice and was treated in Hyderabad; two years later he presented again with similar complaints but did not complete treatment, subsequently developing alcohol-related hallucinations that led to a three-month stay at a de-addiction center. He briefly remained abstinent and functional before resuming alcohol use.

In January of the presenting year, he developed bilateral lower-limb swelling with blistering and pain, along with recurrent jaundice, and was admitted elsewhere. Investigations at that time showed a prolonged INR (1.62–2.27) and low ascitic fluid albumin (<1 g/dL, consistent with a high serum-ascites albumin gradient picture of portal hypertension), with endoscopy revealing gastric erosions. He was discharged, but returned in March with breathlessness at rest, bilateral leg swelling with redness and pain, and productive cough. At that admission, D-dimer was markedly elevated (5405), and Doppler ultrasound revealed a floating thrombus in the right common femoral and left popliteal veins; CT confirmed mild pleural effusion, moderate ascites, and features of chronic liver disease, with a peripheral venogram showing left common iliac vein compression by the right common iliac artery and thrombosis of the distal superficial femoral and popliteal veins on the right. An IVC filter was placed for thromboembolic protection.

He again decompensated in early May, presenting with exertional dyspnea, vomiting, loss of appetite, worsening leg edema, discharge from a non-healing right lower-limb ulcer, and intermittent fever, with creatinine rising to 2 mg/dL and INR spiking to 7.33 — indicating an acute hepatorenal and coagulopathic decompensation. Over the following days his creatinine and INR trended down with supportive care (creatinine 1.6→0.62 mg/dL, INR 3.55→2.62), though a stool occult blood test returned positive. Repeat ultrasound showed hepatosplenomegaly with moderate ascites, and endoscopy again demonstrated reflux esophagitis with a lax lower esophageal sphincter, gastric erosions, and portal hypertensive changes. He was then transferred for de-addiction and further management.

On admission here, he was icteric with edema extending to the thighs but no pallor, cyanosis, clubbing, or lymphadenopathy. Vitals were stable (temperature 98.1°F, pulse 88/min, respiratory rate 19/min, blood pressure 120/70 mmHg, SpO₂ 98% on room air, capillary glucose 116 mg/dL). Abdominal examination showed a soft but distended abdomen consistent with ascites. Investigations confirmed anemia (hemoglobin 6.8 g/dL), a cholestatic and hepatocellular pattern on liver function tests (total bilirubin 3.25, direct 1.65, albumin 2.35 g/dL), mildly deranged coagulation (INR 1.4), and near-normal renal function at that point (creatinine 0.9 mg/dL). The working impression was decompensated chronic liver disease with portal hypertension, complicated by left lower-limb cellulitis with deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary thromboembolism, status post IVC filter placement.

He was managed with pentoxifylline, a proton pump inhibitor, ursodeoxycholic acid, a potassium-sparing diuretic combination (spironolactone/furosemide), thiamine and B-complex supplementation reflecting his alcohol-use history, and maintenance intravenous fluids, alongside a surgical referral for his lower-limb pathology.

Outcome: Managed as decompensated chronic liver disease with portal hypertension complicated by recurrent venous thromboembolism (status post IVC filter) and non-healing lower-limb ulceration, with supportive hepatic, anticoagulation-adjacent, and nutritional therapy alongside surgical referral.
