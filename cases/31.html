38 Year Old Man with Forgetfulness and Irrelevant Talk — Alcohol-Induced Dementia

Source case log: https://02shishirareddy.blogspot.com/2021/06/a-32-year-old-male-with-chief.html

Part 1 — Case Narrative

A 38-year-old man presented with three months of progressive forgetfulness and irrelevant speech, alongside two months of gait disturbance. Three years earlier he had been diagnosed with acute pancreatitis after presenting with abdominal pain, and was subsequently found to have type 3 (pancreatogenic) diabetes as a consequence. During COVID-19 lockdown he escalated his alcohol intake to more than a litre of whisky a day. Three months before this presentation he drank heavily for a week without eating, and one morning his family were unable to wake him; he was taken to another hospital where he was diagnosed with alcoholic ketosis and treated for two weeks. Since that illness he had been unable to speak, unable to follow commands, unable to remember things, and unable to perform tasks independently.

He had a documented history of type 1 diabetes for three years on treatment, and a long-standing history of heavy alcohol use (a litre of whisky daily for eight years) with recent light smoking. On examination he was conscious and oriented to people but not to place or time, with no response on formal speech testing. Vitals were stable with an elevated blood glucose of 269 mg/dL. Motor examination was essentially normal — full power and normal tone throughout, with only mildly reduced ankle jerks — but higher cortical testing showed clear frontal lobe dysfunction, with inability to problem-solve and loss of insight, while parietal lobe functions (right-left orientation, finger recognition) were preserved and occipital function (face recognition) was intact; visuospatial orientation was impaired. Renal and liver function tests were normal, and MRI brain showed a mildly diffusely thinned corpus callosum with otherwise normal grey-white differentiation and no structural lesion.

The clinical and imaging picture — chronic heavy alcohol use, an episode of severe ketosis/encephalopathy, and subsequent persistent cognitive decline with frontal-predominant deficits and callosal thinning — supported a diagnosis of alcohol-induced dementia in a patient with known type 1 diabetes and alcohol dependence syndrome. He was started on thiamine, insulin, an antipsychotic (haloperidol) for behavioral symptoms, an anticonvulsant/mood stabilizer (divalproate), pregabalin, and memantine for cognitive support. The documented record ends at this point of treatment initiation.
