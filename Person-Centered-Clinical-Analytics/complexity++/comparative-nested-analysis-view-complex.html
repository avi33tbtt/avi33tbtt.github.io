Comparative Nested Analysis — Patient vs Population

Overlay one patient's own checkpoint trajectory on top of a whole population's nested trajectory network, and read off where they sit relative to everyone else · runs entirely in-browser
What this is. Load a population cohort_graph.json (many patients) to build the background network — every patient's Diagnosis → Presentation → Initial plan → Escalation → Advanced/recurrent → Disposition sequence, nested onto shared checkpoint nodes. Then load a single-patient cohort_graph.json and their trajectory is drawn as a thick highlighted diamond-marker line on top of that network, in red. Where the patient's checkpoint value doesn't exist anywhere in the population, that step is placed off to the side with a dashed connector and flagged "not seen in population sample." Below the map, percentile cards show where the patient falls on age, event count, and comorbidities relative to the loaded population. Synthetic data only, not clinical decision support.

Comorbidity expansion. This view also accepts a raw multi-patient corpus file (one with all_patients, each carrying tags.comorbidities). When it's loaded, every recorded comorbidity is treated as its own diagnosis row for that patient — a patient with 3 comorbidities appears 3 extra times in the network, each time under a different diagnosis, but sharing that same patient's presentation → plan → escalation → advanced → disposition course. The primary condition still appears once too, so a 3-comorbidity patient contributes 4 diagnosis rows total to the graph.
