01

Case story

The admission told the way it unfolded at the bedside. The two moments marked in red are the decision points everything downstream hinges on.

Hour 0 — arrival

He'd been clearing brush behind the house when the viper struck his right forearm. By the time his brother got him to the district hospital, six hours had passed and the arm had begun to swell — not dramatically, but enough that the on-call resident didn't like the look of it. He was talking normally, no drooping eyelids, no trouble breathing. Nothing about him screamed emergency.

⬤ Decision point 1 — hour 0

Rather than starting a broad workup, the resident ran a single 20-minute whole blood clotting test at the bedside instead of a neurotoxicity screen. It came back non-clotting. That one call — test for coagulopathy, not neurotoxicity — decided everything that followed. It turned "possible snakebite" into "confirmed venom-induced coagulopathy" in one step.

Hour 0 — treatment begins

Ten vials of polyvalent antivenom went in — the same opening dose seen across the case-report corpus regardless of syndrome type. This is the well-trodden part of the story, with a protocol, a dose, and a timer already set for six hours out. For most patients on this corridor, that's the whole story.

⬤ Decision point 2 — hour 6

It didn't stay that way for him. At the re-check, the clotting test was still deranged — but two other numbers had moved in a way the standard corridor doesn't expect: falling urine output, climbing creatinine. The team chose to stop and ask "is this still the same story," rather than simply repeating the dose. That single choice — pausing to re-diagnose instead of escalating — is what caught the divergence.

Hour 6+ — confirmation

A peripheral smear came back with schistocytes — fragmented red cells, the signature of a microangiopathy rather than straightforward venom-driven clotting failure. The diagnosis shifted underneath the treatment plan: thrombotic microangiopathy, a different mechanism, one where more ASV would do nothing and might cost time he didn't have.

The tiered corpus every node above routes into — sourced from the real 78-paper PubMed "anti snake venom" funnel behind the reference implementation (Case Reports + Free Full Text → 78 results; 17 processed so far). The LLM's job is routing a patient to the right sub-graph — not generating the evidence itself.

structural maps of rare, non-linear divergence
Two different case reports that pass through the same clinical event — both patients receiving ASV, both developing the same complication — converge on a shared node. That convergence is what lets a clinician trace a multi-hop pathway across dozens of papers at once, instead of reading each report linearly. The illustrative case above mirrors the corpus's own recurring fork: an initial fixed-dose ASV bolus is the textbook path in 8 of 9 case-report papers with a named hub, but escalation without re-diagnosis is exactly where the fatal/refractory trajectories in the corpus (Cases 4, 5, 11, 12) diverge.

Corpus insights — what actually recurs

Pulled from the "Recurring checkpoint types" and "Unified case insights" analysis in the reference corpus — 12 case-report papers, 23 individual trajectories, not statistically pooled.

8 / 9
papers w/ named ASV hub

An initial fixed-dose ASV bolus (commonly 10 vials, regardless of bite severity grading) is the dominant intervention node across the corpus — escalation for a persisting finding recurs in 6 of those papers.

3×
delay → admission length

Case 8's matched pair (18h vs. 58h to first ASV, same species, same country) shows a roughly 3× longer admission for a 3× longer pre-ASV delay — both still converged on full recovery.

1 / 12
fatal single-patient report

Case 12 (Echis carinatus sochureki, 65-vial escalation) is the corpus's only fatal single-patient case report — reclassified at autopsy from DIC to VICC coexisting with TMA.

3
independent delayed-2nd-event papers

Cases 2, 4/5, and 5 (JFMPC) each independently flag a delayed secondary event — after an apparently clean discharge — as their most clinically informative fork.

OR 7.71
FFP for VICC resolution, shrinks on subgroup

The corpus's meta-analysis tier (Case 17, 4 studies, n=370) shows a large pooled benefit for adjunctive FFP that shrinks and loses significance once restricted to non-Australian snakes — certainty graded very low.

0
tiers that close the gap

Net corpus takeaway: no tier — case report, RCT, or meta-analysis — reliably predicts which patients will diverge into the rare-but-severe outcomes the case-report tier keeps surfacing.

Coded record — FHIR · SNOMED CT · LOINC

The same admission as an EMR would actually carry it: a FHIR resource stream, each entry coded with SNOMED CT (clinical concepts) or LOINC (labs/observations). The two red-ringed entries are the same decision points highlighted in the Patient Journey tab.

Bi-directional close-out

Because FHIR is bi-directional, the ServiceRequest for plasmapheresis above isn't just a suggestion — it can be written back into the record automatically, closing the loop between the reasoning layer and the chart itself, rather than leaving it for the clinician to re-enter by hand.
