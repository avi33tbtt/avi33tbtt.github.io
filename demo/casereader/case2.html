2. Decision Point Review

  1. ⚑ DP1 — Decompensated cirrhosis vs ACLF: ACLF requires an acute precipitant causing organ failure(s) beyond liver (renal, cerebral, circulatory, coagulation). Here there is no overt precipitant identified; decompensated cirrhosis is the working diagnosis. Evidence: EASL-CLIF ACLF criteria require organ failure score. Management implication: ACLF has higher short-term mortality and may require ICU-level care vs ward management.
  2. ⚑ DP2 — SpO₂ 91%: Order ABG. If PaO₂ <80 mmHg, proceed to bubble echocardiography. HPS confirmed by intrapulmonary shunting. Portopulmonary hypertension needs echo with estimated PASP >40 mmHg. HPS improves with liver transplant; portopulmonary hypertension may require pre-transplant pulmonary vasodilator therapy.
  3. ⚑ DP3 — Pre-paracentesis workup: Current evidence (EASL 2018) does not support routine FFP/platelets before paracentesis. Platelets >40,000 and INR <5 are generally acceptable. Perform diagnostic paracentesis for cell count, albumin, protein, culture to rule out SBP — this is urgent given decompensation.
  4. ⚑ DP4 — Coagulopathy correction before paracentesis: Do NOT give FFP. Paracentesis is safe at INR up to 5 without correction. Viscoelastic tests (TEG/ROTEM) better reflect true haemostatic status in cirrhosis than INR, as they capture both pro- and anticoagulant factor deficiencies.
  5. ⚑ DP5 — Rule out HCC: AFP + triphasic CT abdomen required. LI-RADS criteria on MRI/CT classify hepatic lesions >1 cm. If AFP >400 ng/mL with arterial enhancement + washout on CT = HCC without biopsy. This changes staging, MELD exception, and potentially eligibility for loco-regional therapy.

Evidence: both have ~97% sensitivity; echo preferred first-line for ease; MAA scan quantifies shunt fraction and predicts post-transplant oxygenation recovery.

PICO 2 — Albumin infusion in large-volume paracentesis: P: cirrhotic ascites ≥5L tapped; I: IV albumin 6–8 g/L removed; C: no albumin or gelatin; O: post-paracentesis circulatory dysfunction (PPCD), AKI, mortality. Evidence: EASL 2018 recommends albumin as standard of care; reduces PPCD from ~35% to <5%; gelatin/dextran are non-inferior for <5L taps.

Evidence: SALT trials showed tolvaptan increases serum sodium; FDA black box warning for hepatotoxicity in cirrhosis limits use; fluid restriction remains standard first-line in most guidelines.

6. What Would Make You Wrong Audit

Early commitment: "This is alcoholic cirrhosis."

  • Concurrent HBV/HCV: Co-infection accelerates fibrosis independently of alcohol — always check serology.
  • Wilson's disease: Rare at 45 with no Kayser-Fleischer rings mentioned, but a serum caeruloplasmin cost is low if atypical features emerge.
  • Haemochromatosis: Can present with cirrhosis in middle-aged males — ferritin and transferrin saturation warranted.
  • Portal vein thrombosis: Causes acute decompensation on top of cirrhosis; Doppler ultrasound is mandatory in all new decompensation episodes.
  • Hepatocellular carcinoma: Complicates 20% of cirrhotic patients annually; always check AFP and imaging before labelling "simple decompensation."
  • SBP as precipitant: Diagnostic paracentesis must be done; neutrophil count >250/mm³ = SBP requiring cefotaxime regardless of symptoms.
