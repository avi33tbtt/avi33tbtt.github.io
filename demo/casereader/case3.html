2. Decision Point Review

  1. ⚑ DP1 — Mandatory vascular imaging: In a non-alcoholic woman with progressive ascites, MR venogram or Doppler ultrasound of hepatic veins/IVC is mandatory before concluding "cryptogenic cirrhosis." This was done on Day 14 — arguably should have been done at admission. Early diagnosis could have accelerated anticoagulation and transplant evaluation.
  2. ⚑ DP2 — Periumbilical adenopathy: Immobile, adherent nodes mandate urgent ascitic fluid cytology + CECT abdomen for malignancy workup. Sarcoidosis, TB, and lymphoma must be excluded. The team correctly pursued bone biopsy but cytology of bloody ascitic fluid was not documented in the case.
  3. ⚑ DP3 — Vascular imaging timing: Ordering MR venogram on Day 14 was appropriate but the diagnosis should have been considered on Day 1 given the atypical profile. A Doppler ultrasound of hepatic veins (rapid, bedside) on Day 1 would have been the appropriate first-line investigation.
  4. ⚑ DP4 — Empirical warfarin + prednisolone simultaneously: Reasonable given BCS + probable sarcoidosis — but the bone biopsy result was pending. If the lesions had been lymphoma or myeloma, steroids alone could have been dangerous. Bone biopsy before steroids is the preferred sequence — the team correctly withheld steroids for biopsy, then restarted after.
  5. ⚑ DP5 — TIPS vs medical management: With a Rotterdam BCS score suggesting intermediate-to-high risk (ascites, prolonged PT, poor nutrition) and right lung collapse on Day 14, TIPS should have been discussed urgently as a bridge to transplant. Medical management alone in advanced BCS has poor outcomes.

3. Pearl Compendium

  1. 🪨 SAAG ≥1.1 = portal hypertension. SAAG ≥1.1 + ascitic protein >2.5 g/dL = sinusoidal congestion (Budd-Chiari, cardiac) rather than cirrhosis.
  2. 🪨 In South Asian non-alcoholic women with progressive ascites, Budd-Chiari syndrome (especially membranous IVC obstruction) must be considered early — order JAK2 mutation test and hepatic Doppler at admission.
  3. 🪨 Terry nails correlate with hypoalbuminaemia and chronic hepatic synthetic dysfunction — they can antedate clinical jaundice by months.
  4. 🪨 Immobile, adherent periumbilical nodes = malignancy until proven otherwise (Sister Mary Joseph nodule). Always send ascitic cytology from bloody taps.
  5. 🪨 Right-sided pleural effusion in portal hypertension = hepatic hydrothorax. Simultaneous ascitic and pleural fluid analysis showing parallel biochemistry confirms the communication.
  6. 🪨 Sarcoidosis can cause Budd-Chiari syndrome via granulomatous hepatic vein inflammation and simultaneously produce sclerotic bone lesions — a unifying diagnosis in this case.
  7. 🪨 TIPS reduces portal pressure directly and is the preferred bridging intervention in BCS not responding to anticoagulation + diuretics, with 5-year transplant-free survival exceeding 70% in selected patients.
  8. 🪨 Rotterdam BCS score (bilirubin, creatinine, PT, ascites) stratifies prognosis and guides escalation from medical to procedural management.

6. What Would Make You Wrong Audit

Early commitment: "This is portal hypertension from cirrhosis — manage with diuretics and paracentesis."

  • Budd-Chiari missed: Treating as cirrhosis without vascular imaging delays anticoagulation and TIPS evaluation — the single most important management difference.
  • Malignancy underweighted: Periumbilical adherent nodes + bloody ascites + bony lesions + progressive course = strong malignancy signal; ovarian cancer, peritoneal metastasis, lymphoma all cause similar presentations.
  • TB peritonitis: Endemic in West Bengal; TB can cause exudative ascites (low SAAG) but also granulomatous hepatic disease. ADA in ascitic fluid and AFB culture should have been sent.
  • Myeloproliferative neoplasm (polycythaemia vera): JAK2 mutation — the absence of erythrocytosis doesn't exclude it; "spent phase" PV can present with myelofibrosis and thrombotic complications.
  • Cardiac ascites: BP 110/90 with a high SAAG + high protein — constrictive pericarditis or right heart failure must be excluded with echocardiography.
