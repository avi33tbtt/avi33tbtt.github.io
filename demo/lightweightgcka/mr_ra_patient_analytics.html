Key drug–drug / drug–disease interactions
Olmesartan → secretory diarrhea (sprue-like enteropathy)
Olmesartan-associated enteropathy (OAE) is a well-documented but underrecognised cause of chronic secretory diarrhea, often nocturnal, with normal endoscopy. Resolution requires olmesartan withdrawal (trial switch to another ARB). This patient has been on olmesartan for 5 years; OAE typically manifests after prolonged use. The timing, secretory pattern, normal sigmoidoscopy, and failure of antibiotics all fit.
Levothyroxine over-replacement → diarrhea + fatigue + sleep disturbance
Without a recent TFT, we cannot rule out iatrogenic hyperthyroidism. In a 79-year-old, the dose of 100 mcg may now be supra-therapeutic (body weight and thyroid reserve both decline with age). Hyperthyroid state causes secretory diarrhea, weakness, heat intolerance, and paradoxical insomnia — all present here.
Desipramine + silodol-D → urinary retention risk in BPH
Desipramine is anticholinergic and increases urinary retention risk, especially in a 79-year-old with established BPH already on tamsulosin. Monitor for acute retention, worsened nocturia, or confusion.
Differential diagnoses — nocturnal watery diarrhea in this patient
1. Olmesartan-associated enteropathy (OAE)Priority 1
Fits perfectly: secretory diarrhea, nocturnal predominance, normal endoscopy, prolonged ARB use, antibiotic non-response. Diagnostic test: olmesartan withdrawal trial (switch to losartan or valsartan). Resolution in 4–8 weeks confirms diagnosis.
2. Levothyroxine-induced hyperthyroidismPriority 2
Diarrhea, fatigue, hypersomnia (post-exertional), heat intolerance (implicit), weight status unknown. Diagnostic test: TSH + free T4. If TSH suppressed, dose reduction resolves diarrhea quickly.
3. Microscopic colitis (lymphocytic / collagenous)Priority 3
Normal macroscopic sigmoidoscopy + secretory diarrhea in elderly male. Sigmoidoscopy alone is insufficient — requires biopsies. Collagenous colitis = watery, nocturnal, normal endoscopy. Associated with PPI, NSAID, SSRI use (not current meds, but still possible).
4. Colorectal malignancy (given FH)Must exclude
Elder brother had UC → colorectal cancer. Niece has breast cancer. Nocturnal diarrhea, age 79, significant family history = mandatory colonoscopy (not just sigmoidoscopy). No weight loss documented, no bleeding, but negative predictive value of these for CRC in elderly is limited.
5. IBS-D / functional diarrhea (current GI diagnosis)Diagnosis of exclusion
Current working diagnosis (hence despar + probiotic). However, functional diarrhea diagnosis in a 79-year-old with new-onset nocturnal diarrhea, multiple organic possibilities, and positive family history should only be made after full exclusion of organic causes.
6. Pruritus — separate differential axis
18-month history predates diarrhea. Consider: cholestatic pruritus (biliary disease, drug-induced cholestasis), uraemia (renal function unchecked), hypothyroidism (paradoxical — even in treated patients), atopic/contact dermatitis, polycythaemia vera (aquagenic itch), lymphoma-associated itch, or simply xerosis/asteatotic eczema (commonest in 79M). LFT, RFT, CBC, LDH needed. The itch and diarrhea may share a single systemic cause (cholestatic pruritus + steatorrhoea? lymphoma?) or be entirely independent.

Sigmoidoscopy only — proximal colon not visualised. FH of UC + CRC in first-degree relative. Colonoscopy is mandatory. Do not defer on grounds of age alone.

Generalised itch of this duration with no identified cause in an elderly man demands: LFT, RFT, TFT, CBC, LDH, serum ferritin, blood film. Haematological malignancy and cholestatic disease must be excluded.

3–4 watery stools nightly for 1 month in a 79-year-old. Despite ORS self-initiation, cumulative deficit may be significant. U&E, serum creatinine, sodium, potassium urgently needed.

Switch olmesartan to an alternative ARB (losartan 50 mg) or CCB. Monitor diarrhea at 2 and 4 weeks. If diarrhea resolves, OAE confirmed. Resolution validates olmesartan as the cause and avoids further investigation cascade.
