1Chief Complaint

Recurrent urinary tract infections, with a large painless abdominal mass found incidentally on examination by her general practitioner.

2History of Present Illness

A 70-year-old woman presented to her general practitioner with recurrent urinary tract infections. During evaluation she was incidentally found to have a large, painless abdominal mass on examination. She reported no systemic symptoms.

Cross-sectional imaging was pursued because of this incidental mass — CT showed a 160 × 123 × 144 mm fatty mass, favouring a differential diagnosis of uterine lipoleiomyoma. Because the mass was difficult to distinguish from the right ovary on CT, a follow-up pelvic ultrasound was performed, which showed a 124 × 123 × 111 mm mass favouring a dermoid arising from the right ovary. The two studies gave conflicting differentials, which drove the decision for surgical evaluation.

Clinical Progress Journey

The diagnostic and management course rewritten as a connected, reasoning-based sequence rather than a flat list of findings and orders.

This case's "journey" is primarily a diagnostic-to-surgical arc rather than a multi-day inpatient course: an incidental mass finding leads through conflicting imaging to definitive surgery, and the true diagnosis only emerges on histopathology. Tap each step to expand.
Presentation Incidental pelvic mass found during UTI work-up ▶
Clinical state

70-year-old woman evaluated by her GP for recurrent urinary tract infections; no systemic symptoms.

Decision / Action

Physical examination performed as part of the UTI evaluation, revealing a large painless abdominal mass.

Why inferred

A painless abdominal mass in a postmenopausal woman warrants further imaging to characterise its origin and rule out malignancy before it can be attributed to a benign cause.

Response / Outcome

Prompted referral for cross-sectional imaging.

Imaging — Step 1 CT favours a uterine lipoleiomyoma ▶
Clinical state

Abdominal mass identified on exam, cause still undetermined.

Decision / Action

Computed tomography performed, showing a 160 × 123 × 144 mm fatty mass.

Why

CT characterised the mass as fat-containing, leading radiologists to favour uterine lipoleiomyoma as the diagnosis.

Response / Outcome

Diagnosis remained provisional — the mass was difficult to differentiate from the right ovary on CT, prompting a second imaging modality.

Imaging — Step 2 Pelvic ultrasound instead favours an ovarian dermoid ▶
Clinical state

Origin of the mass (uterine vs. ovarian) still unresolved after CT.

Decision / Action

Pelvic ultrasound obtained specifically to differentiate the mass from the right ovary; it showed a 124 × 123 × 111 mm mass favouring a dermoid arising from the right ovary.

Why

The two imaging modalities now disagreed on the organ of origin (uterus vs. ovary), which is itself clinically important since it changes the differential and surgical approach.

Response / Outcome

Because of these conflicting radiological findings, definitive surgical management was pursued rather than further imaging.

Decision Point Elective hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy chosen ▶
Clinical state

Radiologically indeterminate pelvic mass with two discordant differentials (lipoleiomyoma vs. dermoid).

Decision / Action

Patient underwent elective hysterectomy and bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy, with the specimen sent for histopathological examination.

Why

Due to the conflicting radiological findings, definitive surgical excision with histopathology was chosen to resolve the diagnosis — consistent with the case discussion's point that radiological findings are often non-specific and diagnosis is frequently confirmed only at histopathology.

Response / Outcome

Specimen obtained for gross and microscopic examination.

Post-op — Gross Pathology Uterus weighed 1666 g with a discrete yellow fatty mass ▶
Clinical state

Surgical specimen available for gross examination.

Decision / Action

Gross cut surface examined: round yellow mass of likely adipose tissue in the posterior wall, 150 × 140 × 125 mm; no hemorrhage or necrosis seen macroscopically. Small paratubal cyst noted; tubes/ovaries otherwise unremarkable.

Why

Macroscopic appearance (yellow, fatty, well-circumscribed, no necrosis/hemorrhage) is consistent with a benign fat-containing lesion rather than a necrotic or hemorrhagic malignant mass, guiding the pathologist toward a lipomatous rather than sarcomatous process inferred.

Response / Outcome

Specimen sent for microscopic sectioning and immunohistochemistry.

Post-op — Microscopy & IHC Benign mature adipocytes confirmed; malignancy panel negative ▶
Clinical state

Gross findings suggestive of a benign lipomatous lesion; definitive diagnosis still pending microscopy.

Decision / Action

Microscopy showed benign mature adipocytes with septa containing thick-walled vessels and occasional perivascular smooth muscle, and no evidence of malignancy or atypia. Immunohistochemistry: focal desmin and SMA positivity around vessels; MDM2, HMB-45, and Melan-A negative; very low Ki-67 proliferation index.

Why

Desmin/SMA positivity localised to vessel walls (not extending into the adipocyte population) argues against lipoleiomyoma; negative HMB-45/Melan-A excludes angiomyolipoma; negative MDM2 with low Ki-67 excludes liposarcoma — together these narrow the diagnosis to a pure lipoma.

Response / Outcome

Findings supported a final diagnosis of benign intramural uterine lipoma with focal smooth muscle around vessels.

Diagnosis Final diagnosis: benign intramural uterine lipoma ▶
Clinical state

All gross, microscopic, and immunohistochemical findings reviewed together.

Decision / Action

Diagnosis rendered postoperatively as benign intramural uterine lipoma with focal smooth muscle around vessels.

Why

The combination of mature adipocyte morphology, absence of atypia, vessel-restricted smooth muscle, and a negative malignancy immunopanel fulfils the histological picture the literature describes for pure uterine lipoma.

Response / Outcome

No further oncologic work-up required based on these benign findings.

Follow-up Uncomplicated recovery, well at 3 years ▶
Clinical state

Post-surgical recovery period.

Decision / Action

Patient monitored clinically following surgery.

Why

Benign diagnosis meant surveillance needs were limited to routine post-surgical recovery rather than oncologic follow-up inferred.

Response / Outcome

Patient recovered well from her operations with no complications and remained well in the 3 years since her surgery.

Clinical Pearls

Case-specific takeaways, not generic textbook facts
1When CT and ultrasound disagree on whether a fat-containing pelvic mass is uterine or ovarian in origin, that discordance itself was the trigger for surgery in this case rather than further imaging.
2A fat-containing mass on CT does not equal a benign diagnosis by imaging alone — here it was still called a "lipoleiomyoma," not a pure lipoma, until histopathology proved otherwise.
3Desmin/SMA positivity confined to vessel walls (not extending into the adipocyte population) is the key discriminator that separates a pure lipoma from a lipoleiomyoma.
4A negative HMB-45/Melan-A panel excludes angiomyolipoma, and negative MDM2 with a low Ki-67 excludes liposarcoma — this three-marker logic is what converts "fatty pelvic mass" into "pure lipoma."
5Absence of macroscopic hemorrhage or necrosis on gross cut surface was an early clue toward a benign process, well before immunohistochemistry confirmed it.
6Pure uterine lipoma is a diagnosis of exclusion made only after thorough sampling — the case discussion notes this is needed to rule out atypical adipocytes or significant smooth muscle/connective tissue seen in other lipomatous tumours.
7Presenting symptoms here (recurrent UTIs, incidental mass) were nonspecific — uterine lipomas are often asymptomatic and, when symptomatic, mimic leiomyoma or malignancy clinically.
