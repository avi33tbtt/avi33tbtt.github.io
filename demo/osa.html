Illness Script Acquisition

Module 15 · Script Theory (enabling conditions → fault → consequences)

Enabling conditions only — Step 15.1

59F, BMI 40+, T2DM ×5y, rapid 8–9 kg weight gain in 20 days after starting diabetic medication, new loud snoring/sleep-talking over 10–15 days.

→ Scripts activated on enabling conditions alone: (a) medication-induced fluid retention/weight gain (e.g., insulin or a secretagogue), (b) worsening obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA), (c) an endocrine driver of rapid fat/fluid gain such as hypercortisolism.

Illness script anatomy — OSA script (Step 15.2)
Reconstructed from Step 15.2 (Full-Script Articulation). Dashed red box marks the component the case data least supports.

Script discrimination — near-miss pair — Step 15.3

OSA vs. Cushing's syndrome (exogenous/iatrogenic). Overlapping enabling conditions (obesity, weight gain, fatigue) and overlapping consequences (weight gain, possible sleep disturbance) but different fault (mechanical airway obstruction vs. cortisol excess). Discriminating feature: facial/skin changes (ulcers, facial swelling), medication history (undisclosed Ayurvedic use), and glycemic pattern (hypoglycemia despite insulin — atypical for steroid excess, which usually worsens hyperglycemia) — this mismatch is itself informative.

Atypical presentation / script mismatch — Step 15.4

The hypoglycemia-despite-treatment-and-food feature does not fit either the OSA script or a simple hypercortisolism script cleanly — hypercortisolism classically causes hyperglycemia, not hypoglycemia. This is the single most important "does not fit" signal in the case and should trigger fallback to stepwise reasoning rather than forcing closure on either script (e.g., consider reduced counter-regulation, autonomic neuropathy, adrenal insufficiency, or medication/dose mismatch as the fault instead).

Critical awareness — Step 15.8

Fast script-matching here risks anchoring on "obesity + T2DM + snoring = OSA" and missing the metabolically atypical hypoglycemia and the psychiatric turn. Best safeguard: treat any consequence that doesn't fit the activated script (as flagged in 15.4) as a mandatory trigger for slower, stepwise re-analysis rather than smoothing it into the existing script.

Differential Diagnosis Deepdive

Module 12 · Devil's Advocate Mode
Working diagnosis under test Obstructive sleep apnoea secondary to severe obesity, with T2DM and possible medication-related weight gain.

Three strongest attacks — Step 12.1

  1. No documented witnessed apnoeas, oxygen desaturation, or daytime hypersomnolence are recorded — only snoring and loud sleep-talking, which are consistent with but not diagnostic of OSA. Bias implicated: representativeness (matching "obese + snoring" to the OSA prototype).
  2. OSA does not explain the hypoglycemia occurring despite food and insulin, the new mouth ulcers, the facial swelling, or the eventual psychosis with normal SpO₂. Bias implicated: anchoring on the first plausible unifying diagnosis and discounting features that don't fit it.
  3. The acute 8–9 kg gain in 20 days is far too rapid for simple lifestyle-driven obesity progression and points toward a fluid-retentive or endocrine process rather than airway disease per se. Bias implicated: availability (obesity + diabetes is a very common combination, making it easy to stop looking further).

Zebra test — 24-hour window — Step 12.4

  1. Iatrogenic Cushing's from covert herbal/steroid use — confirm/exclude via serum cortisol + direct questioning/toxicology of the "Ayurveda" preparation.
  2. Insulin autoimmune syndrome or factitious insulin/sulfonylurea use — confirm/exclude via insulin, C-peptide, and sulfonylurea screen drawn during a hypoglycemic episode.

Semantic Qualifiers & Problem Representation

Module 17 · Compression before diagnosis

Qualifier-summary compression — Step 17.1

Raw narrative → "Chronic, progressive, disproportionate weight gain in a diabetic patient, with a superimposed acute, atypical (paradoxical) metabolic disturbance and subacute, diffuse mucocutaneous and neuropsychiatric features."

  • Qualifiers chosen well: chronic vs. acute (correctly separates the years-long obesity trajectory from the 20-day gain), disproportionate (the recent gain is out of proportion to stated intake reduction), paradoxical (hypoglycemia despite treatment/food, the case's central puzzle).
  • Detail correctly abstracted as noise: the exact kilogram figures and the precise sequence of family visits/reactions.
  • Detail nearly lost that should have stayed its own qualifier: "disproportionate to reported caloric intake" — folding this into a generic "weight gain" qualifier would obscure that the pattern is not simple dietary non-adherence, a key discriminator pointing toward fluid retention or an endocrine process.
Competing representations of the same findings (Step 17.3)
Re-checking the timeline: findings in Representation A cluster together within the same 10–15 day window, making it the more faithful summary and giving the endocrine/steroid-exposure hypothesis primary weight.

Critical awareness on abstraction — Step 17.8

  1. Risk of over-abstracting: qualifiers like "chronic obesity" could silently absorb and hide the specific, case-defining fact of a possible undisclosed steroid-containing herbal remedy — the single most abstraction-vulnerable detail in this case.
  2. Difference from premature closure: here the danger is losing a detail before a script is even activated, versus premature closure which happens after a script is chosen — an earlier and more silent failure point.
  3. Best safeguard: explicitly qualify any "chronic/progressive" label with a rate-of-change flag whenever the pace of a chronic process suddenly accelerates (as with the 20-day gain), so the acceleration itself is never abstracted away.

Revised reasoning simulation — Step 22.9

Re-running the case with corrections applied — holding the hypercortisolism branch open at Node 4d and pursuing cortisol testing to completion — would not necessarily change the immediate psychiatric management, but would change the endocrine/metabolic management plan materially: it could identify a reversible cause (steroid withdrawal/tapering, or treatment of endogenous Cushing's) for the weight gain, mucosal findings, and hypoglycemia, which the OSA-focused pathway alone does not address.

Forward commitment — Step 22.10

The bias most likely to recur is anchoring on a common, pattern-matching cluster (obesity/diabetes/snoring) in future complex multi-system cases. What held up well: the team did keep investigating (ENT referral, imaging, psychiatry involvement) rather than closing the case entirely — the differential stayed nominally open even if the highest-yield test was not completed.
