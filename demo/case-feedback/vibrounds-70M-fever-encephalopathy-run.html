13.8 — Personal learning summary (module output)

  • Three pharmacological principles this case illustrates: (1) a medication history is inseparable from a neurological differential in any rigidity/altered-sensorium presentation; (2) "not a known case of [chronic disease]" documents absence of chronic disease, not absence of drug exposure — OTC antipyretics, unregulated over-the-counter sedatives, or traditional/herbal remedies are common unrecorded exposures in this care setting; (3) a medication table with zero entries is not "nothing to audit" — it is itself the audit finding.
  • What would have been missed without this structured process: that the case, as documented, cannot exclude a drug-induced contributor to the neurological picture.
  • Bedside habit going forward: ask specifically and directly about any tablet, injection, traditional medicine, or over-the-counter drug taken in the preceding 2 weeks — framed as its own question, not folded into "past medical history."
  • One-sentence summary for the supervising clinician: "No medication history is documented for this patient despite a rigidity/altered-sensorium/fever presentation in which drug causation is a relevant differential — recommend an explicit medication history be taken from the wife or son before finalising the diagnosis."
