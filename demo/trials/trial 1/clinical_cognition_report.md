# Attending/Consultant Clinical Cognition Report

**Patient:** 23-year-old male, food supplier
**Date of Report:** October 2020
**Presenting Complaint:** Cough with expectoration × 1 week; Shortness of breath × 4 days
**Prepared by:** Attending Physician / Pulmonology Consultant

---

## 1. Case Summary

A previously healthy 23-year-old male presented with recurrent respiratory symptoms following a documented COVID-19 infection approximately one month prior. He had tested positive for SARS-CoV-2, received treatment, and subsequently tested negative after 20 days. He then re-presented with cough with whitish expectoration, pleuritic chest pain, and exertional dyspnea. On 11/10/2020 at 12:30 PM, he suffered an acute decompensation with rapidly falling oxygen saturations, hemoptysis (~50 mL), and tachycardia — prompting emergency evaluation revealing a left pneumothorax on CXR. An intercostal drain (ICD) was placed urgently. Subsequent HRCT and clinical course revealed bilateral loculated hydropneumothorax, subcutaneous emphysema, pneumomediastinum, and bilateral infective consolidations — consistent with severe post-COVID pulmonary sequelae.

---

## 2. Clinical Cognition: Problem Representation

**Core Problem Statement:**
> A young immunocompetent male with recent SARS-CoV-2 infection (CORADS 5 on prior HRCT) now presents with acute respiratory failure due to spontaneous left pneumothorax, hemoptysis, bilateral hydropneumothorax, and subcutaneous emphysema — most likely representing COVID-19-induced barotrauma/cystic lung disease with secondary infection.

**Key Anchors:**
- Young male (low baseline structural lung disease risk)
- Recent COVID-19 infection (high CORADS score — bilateral crazy paving pattern)
- Subpleural bulla noted on HRCT as early as 23/09/2020
- Acute onset at 12:30 PM on 11/10/2020 — classic sudden pneumothorax presentation
- Bilateral involvement (not a simple primary spontaneous pneumothorax)
- Hemoptysis accompanying hypoxia — raises concern for vascular injury or secondary infection/infarct

---

## 3. Differential Diagnosis and Clinical Reasoning

### Primary Diagnosis (Working)
**Post-COVID Sequelae with Spontaneous Pneumothorax secondary to Subpleural Bulla Rupture**

*Rationale:*
COVID-19 is known to cause alveolar damage, formation of pneumatoceles and subpleural bullae through direct cytopathic injury to type II pneumocytes, surfactant dysfunction, and inflammatory destruction of lung parenchyma. The HRCT from 23/09/2020 demonstrated a crazy paving pattern (CORADS 5) and explicitly noted a "possible spontaneous rupture of subpleural bulla." The subsequent HRCT (14/10/2020) confirmed a bulla in the apical segment of the right lower lobe and bilateral loculated hydropneumothorax. This is the most internally consistent diagnosis.

### Alternative / Concurrent Diagnoses Considered

| Differential | Arguments For | Arguments Against |
|---|---|---|
| Primary Spontaneous Pneumothorax (PSP) | Young male, thin build likely | Bilateral involvement; not classic unilateral apical; post-COVID context |
| Secondary Spontaneous Pneumothorax (SSP) due to underlying lung disease | COVID pneumonitis as precipitant; bullae on HRCT | No pre-existing chronic lung disease documented |
| Pulmonary Embolism | D-dimer elevated at 1940 µg/L; tachycardia; post-COVID hypercoagulable state | 2D Echo did not show RV strain; ECG normal; pneumothorax confirmed on CXR as primary event |
| Tuberculous empyema / Bronchopleural fistula | Cough with expectoration; loculated effusion | No AFB workup documented; CORADS pattern more consistent with viral; CRP negative |
| Iatrogenic pneumothorax (post-ICD) | Bilateral findings noted after ICD | Right-sided bulla and pneumothorax noted on HRCT before procedure; already bilateral |
| Bacterial superinfection (empyema) | Rising WBC initially; loculated hydropneumothorax; bilateral air-space opacities | CRP negative at one timepoint; antibiotic coverage already initiated |

```
                          |
                          ↓

                          |
                          ↓
              Bilateral lower lobe atelectasis + infective consolidation
```

**The D-Dimer Elevation (1940 µg/L):**
This is almost certainly a non-specific acute-phase reactant in the context of acute inflammatory/infectious disease and pleural pathology, rather than primary pulmonary embolism. However, PE cannot be fully excluded without CTPA and remains a concomitant risk given COVID-19's prothrombotic state. The normal ECG and Echo reduce — but do not eliminate — the probability of a clinically significant central PE.

**Hemoptysis:**
~50 mL hemoptysis in this context likely reflects:
1. Mucosal erosion from extensive endobronchial inflammation (post-COVID)
2. Traction injury on pulmonary vasculature from rapid lung collapse
3. Less likely: pulmonary infarction from in situ microvascular thrombosis (a recognized COVID complication)

**Cognition:**
- Initial leucocytosis with neutrophilia on 11/10 is consistent with acute stress response to pneumothorax and evolving pneumonia/infection.
- The fall in Hb from 14.6 to 11.6 by 13/10 is notable — likely dilutional (IV fluids + Fresh Frozen Plasma transfusion) or hemorrhagic (hemoptysis). This warrants monitoring for ongoing occult bleeding.
- Thrombocytopenia on 13/10 is concerning and could reflect: consumption coagulopathy, post-viral marrow suppression, or drug effect. FFP transfusion suggests coagulopathy was recognized clinically. Platelet recovery to 1.51 L by 16/10 is reassuring.
- WBC normalization by 13/10 may reflect response to antibiotics, but should not be interpreted as infection resolution given ongoing radiological abnormalities.

### Renal & Hepatic Function
- RFT is normal — no concern for sepsis-induced AKI at this point.
- SGPT mildly elevated at 74 IU/L and ALP at 157 IU/L — consistent with post-COVID hepatic involvement (a recognized extrapulmonary manifestation), or drug effect. Total proteins are mildly low at 5.3 g/dL — albumin 3.8 g/dL is borderline, suggesting nutritional/inflammatory catabolism.

### D-Dimer: 1940 µg/L (significantly elevated)
Non-specific in this acute inflammatory context, but CTPA should be considered when patient is stable, given post-COVID hypercoagulability.

### 2D Echo
- Good to fair LV function: myocardial involvement (COVID myocarditis) not prominent, though diastolic dysfunction is present — can be seen with tachycardia, acute illness, or early myocardial involvement.
- No LV clot: reduces embolic risk from cardiac source.
- Normal ECG: reduces probability of massive PE or arrhythmia.

### HRCT (23/09/2020 — during active COVID)
Bilateral crazy paving pattern, subcutaneous emphysema, pneumomediastinum, and subpleural bulla — CORADS 5. This is the critical background radiological context; the bulla was the structural vulnerability that led to the pneumothorax.

### HRCT (14/10/2020 — post-COVID negative)
Bilateral loculated hydropneumothorax, partial atelectasis of lower lobes, peribronchial infective opacities, ICD in situ, persistent bulla in right lower lobe apical segment — CORADS 2. The improvement in inflammatory pattern (CORADS 5 → 2) confirms viral clearance, but structural damage and secondary infection persist.

## 6. Management Critique and Recommendations

### Immediate Management (Appropriate)
- High-flow oxygen at 12 L/min — correct for SpO2 of 82%
- ICD insertion (24F, 5th ICS, mid-axillary line, left) — correctly prioritized as emergency life-saving intervention
- Broad-spectrum antibiotics: Piperacillin-Tazobactam 4.5g BD + Metronidazole 500mg TID + Azithromycin 500mg OD — appropriate empirical cover for community-acquired pneumonia with anaerobic cover (given hydropneumothorax and loculations)

### Concerns and Additional Recommendations

**1. The Right-sided Bulla — Prophylactic Consideration:**
HRCT confirmed a bulla in the right lower lobe apical segment. Given that the left pneumothorax arose from bulla rupture, the patient is at significant risk of a contralateral pneumothorax. Elective VATS (Video-Assisted Thoracoscopic Surgery) with bullectomy and pleurodesis of the right side should be planned once the acute illness resolves.

**2. Bilateral Loculated Hydropneumothorax — Thoracoscopy:**
The pulmonology team correctly recommended video-assisted thoracoscopy for loculations and decortication. A single ICD is unlikely to drain loculated collections adequately. Urokinase intrapleural instillation could be considered as a less invasive bridge if thoracoscopy is delayed.

**3. PE Work-up:**
Given elevated D-dimer and COVID-19 hypercoagulability, a formal CTPA or V/Q scan should be performed once the pneumothorax is resolved and the patient is stable enough for the procedure. Prophylactic anticoagulation (LMWH) should be continued.

**4. Microbiological Workup:**
- Sputum culture and sensitivity (including AFB smear/culture) should be sent, given the chronicity and loculated empyema.
- Pleural fluid obtained via ICD should be sent for: culture/sensitivity, AFB, cytology, LDH, protein, glucose, pH.
- Blood cultures (aerobic and anaerobic) should be drawn during febrile episodes.

**5. Coagulopathy / FFP Transfusion:**
The fact that FFP was transfused implies a coagulopathy was identified (likely PT/INR or aPTT derangement not recorded in the blog). This should be actively monitored and the underlying etiology (liver dysfunction, consumption, DIC) defined.

**6. Nutrition:**
Low total protein and albumin suggest catabolic state. Nutritional support (high-protein diet, consider nasogastric feeding if oral intake inadequate) is important for pleural healing and immune recovery.

**7. Monitoring the Acute-on-19th Deterioration:**
The new acute breathlessness on 19/10 with worsening CXR likely reflects ICD drain blockage, loculation, or progression of contralateral pneumothorax/hydrothorax. A new ICD or repositioning should be considered urgently.

## 7. Synthesis: What Makes This Case Instructive

This case illustrates a cascade of COVID-19-related pulmonary complications that unfolded in a stepwise, mechanistically linked fashion:

1. **Active COVID-19 with diffuse alveolar damage** → bilateral crazy paving, pneumomediastinum, subcutaneous emphysema, bulla formation (HRCT 23/09).
2. **Bulla rupture** (despite viral clearance) → acute left pneumothorax on 11/10 with hemoptysis and hypoxia.
3. **Pleural contamination + inflammatory exudate** → bilateral loculated hydropneumothorax.
4. **Ongoing structural lung disease** → partial atelectasis, infective consolidation, right-sided bulla at risk.
5. **Systemic inflammatory state** → elevated D-dimer, thrombocytopenia, mild hepatitis, diastolic dysfunction.

The temporal relationship between COVID-19 and this catastrophic pneumothorax is the central clinical lesson. In 2020, spontaneous pneumothorax as a complication of COVID-19 was being increasingly recognized and represents direct parenchymal injury rather than coincidence.

- Secondary bacterial superinfection (probable)

- Mild hepatitis (post-COVID extrapulmonary involvement)
- Diastolic dysfunction (post-COVID cardiac involvement)

---

## 9. Prognosis and Disposition

With appropriate ICD management, broad-spectrum antibiotics, and eventual thoracoscopy/decortication, prognosis for a 23-year-old with no prior comorbidities is cautiously optimistic. However, the bilateral nature of disease, loculation, and right-sided residual bulla represent ongoing mortality and morbidity risk. Close monitoring in HDU/ICU with daily CXR, clinical assessment, and multidisciplinary involvement (pulmonology, thoracic surgery, infectious disease) is essential.

Long-term pulmonary function testing (PFT) at 3 and 6 months post-discharge is recommended to assess for residual restrictive or obstructive defects from parenchymal scarring.
