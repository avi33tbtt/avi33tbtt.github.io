NODE 1 — Presenting Complaint
  Finding: 45M, pedal edema x2 months, SOB x2 months, facial puffiness
  Decision Point: Yes — Type: Bifurcating
  Insight: Bilateral pitting pedal edema with facial puffiness and SOB over a subacute (2-month) timeframe should activate a triad of pattern candidates — renal (nephrotic/nephritic or CKD-related fluid overload), cardiac (heart failure), and hepatic (hypoalbuminemia from liver disease) — and the co-occurrence of facial puffiness specifically favors a renal/nephrotic-range process over isolated cardiac edema, which more typically spares the face.

NODE 2 — History
  2a. Gradual, progressive, pitting pedal edema up to knee: Decision Point: Bifurcating — Insight: Progressive bilateral pitting edema over months is consistent with a chronic fluid-retentive state (renal or cardiac) rather than an acute unilateral process (e.g., DVT), narrowing the branch but not yet discriminating renal vs. cardiac.
  2b. SOB grade 2 dyspnea, onset 1 month (after edema): Decision Point: Bifurcating — Insight: SOB following edema onset by roughly a month is compatible with fluid overload from either a cardiac or renal source; the sequence (edema first, then dyspnea) mildly favors a primary fluid-retention process over primary cardiac pump failure, where dyspnea often precedes or coincides with edema.
  2c. Known hypertension x1 year: Decision Point: Pivotal — Insight: A 1-year history of hypertension is a major risk factor for both chronic kidney disease and hypertensive heart disease, and is the single most important piece of history steering the differential toward a hypertension-related renal or cardiac process.
  2d. Decreased appetite, stopped eating non-veg food: Decision Point: Missed (as a discriminator) — Insight: Anorexia and specific avoidance of protein-rich food are classic uremic symptoms and should be flagged as supporting a renal etiology, but the case does not explicitly connect this symptom to the provisional diagnosis reasoning.
  2e. Occasional alcohol, no tobacco: Decision Point: Closing — Insight: Absence of significant alcohol/tobacco use somewhat de-weights an alcohol-related cardiomyopathy or hepatic cause, keeping the primary renal-hypertensive branch more prominent.
  Missing history: No documented duration/control of hypertension (medications, compliance), no history of diabetes screening beyond "not a k/c/o," no prior renal function tests or baseline creatinine, no urine output history (oliguria/anuria), no orthopnea/PND explicitly asked about despite SOB being a chief complaint, no family history of renal disease despite "no significant family history" being a blanket statement.

NODE 3 — Examination
  3a. Pitting edema to knees, pallor present: Decision Point: Pivotal — Insight: The combination of pitting edema and pallor (anemia) together is a strong pattern for chronic kidney disease — anemia of CKD from reduced erythropoietin — more than it would be for isolated cardiac failure, and this pairing is the examination finding that most concretely supports the stated provisional diagnosis.
  3b. No icterus, cyanosis, lymphadenopathy: Decision Point: Closing — Insight: Absence of icterus reduces likelihood of a hepatic/hemolytic contributor to the edema and pallor, and absence of cyanosis argues against significant hypoxemia at rest.
  3c. BP 140/90, pulse 82 regular, SpO2 98%, GRBS 109: Decision Point: Closing — Insight: Mildly elevated BP consistent with known hypertension, normal oxygenation, and normal random glucose together argue against acute decompensated hypoxic cardiac failure or undiagnosed diabetes as the dominant driver at this moment.
  3d. CVS — S1S2 heard, no thrills/murmurs; RS — vesicular, trachea central; abdomen scaphoid, non-tender: Decision Point: Closing — Insight: Absence of murmurs argues against a primary valvular cause of heart failure, and a scaphoid (non-distended) abdomen with no palpable mass argues against ascites or an intra-abdominal mass as a major contributor, further supporting a renal rather than hepatic or structural-cardiac process.
