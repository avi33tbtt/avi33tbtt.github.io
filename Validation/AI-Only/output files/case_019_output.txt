DIAGNOSTIC REASONING MAP — 14-year-old boy with pain abdomen, poor urinary stream, and fever (anemia under evaluation)
Module: DRM — Diagnostic Reasoning Map

NODE 1 — Presenting Complaint
  Finding: Pain abdomen x1 month, loss of appetite x1 month, poor stream of urine x10 days (with post-micturition dribbling), fever x6 days.
  Decision Point: Yes — Type: Bifurcating
  Insight: The combination of a subacute constitutional/GI complaint (1 month) with a distinct, more recent urinary obstructive-type complaint (10 days) and fever (6 days) should split early reasoning into at least two candidate tracks — a chronic/subacute systemic-inflammatory or hematologic process, and a genitourinary process (obstructive uropathy, UTI, or a pelvic/retroperitoneal mass compressing the urinary tract) — rather than being read as one continuous story.

NODE 2 — History
  2a. Abdominal pain insidious onset with 2 episodes vomiting/loose stools, self-limited: Decision Point: Type Closing (partial) — Insight: A brief self-limited vomiting/diarrhoeal illness makes a primary GI infective cause of the ongoing 1-month symptom complex less likely as the sole explanation, since that episode resolved while the pain and appetite loss persisted.
  2b. Poor stream of urine with dribbling, no burning micturition, x10 days: Decision Point: Type Pivotal — Insight: Obstructive urinary symptoms without dysuria point away from simple cystitis and toward a mechanical or infiltrative process (e.g., mass, lymphadenopathy, or structural lesion) narrowing or compressing the urinary outflow tract — this should have been a strong early trigger for abdominal/pelvic imaging.
  2c. Fever pattern — low-grade continuous fever x6 days, then evening-rise fever with chills/rigors from 2 days: Decision Point: Type Bifurcating — Insight: A fever pattern that intensifies and develops rigors partway through raises probability of an intermittent parasitic (e.g., malarial) or an evolving bacterial/systemic infective process, and should prompt peripheral smear and blood culture in addition to malaria testing.
  2d. Absence of cough, headache, ear symptoms, burning micturition: Decision Point: Type Closing — Insight: Negative associated symptoms reasonably lower the pre-test probability of a primary respiratory, ENT, or straightforward lower-UTI source for the fever.
  2e. No past history of DM, HTN, asthma, TB: Decision Point: Type Closing — Insight: Absence of chronic comorbidity lowers prior probability of a comorbidity-driven chronic process but does not by itself exclude a new hematologic or infiltrative diagnosis in an adolescent.
  Single most important history feature: The poor urinary stream with dribbling (2b) — it is the feature most likely to redirect the diagnostic pathway away from a purely hematologic/infective frame toward investigation of a mass or infiltrative lesion causing outflow compression, and the source text does not show it being revisited after admission.
  Missing history: No documented family history, no growth/nutritional history, no dietary history relevant to iron intake, no history of pica or blood loss (melena/haematuria/haemoptysis) explicitly asked about apart from the later "dark coloured stools" noted on Day 1 — and no clarification of whether the urinary symptom was ever specifically re-examined once anemia became the working diagnosis.

NODE 3 — Examination
  3a. Pallor present: Decision Point: Type Pivotal — Insight: Pallor supports a clinically significant anemia and anchors the team toward a hematologic work-up, consistent with the eventual working diagnosis.
  3b. No icterus, cyanosis, clubbing, lymphadenopathy, pedal edema: Decision Point: Type Closing — Insight: Absence of icterus lowers probability of overt haemolysis as the anemia's dominant mechanism; absence of lymphadenopathy somewhat lowers (but does not exclude) probability of a lymphoproliferative or infiltrative cause for both the anemia and the urinary symptom.
  3c. Vitals essentially stable on admission (afebrile at the time, PR 92, BP 100/60, RR 22, SpO2 98%): Decision Point: Type Missed — Insight: Reassuring initial vitals despite a 6-day fever history and 10-day obstructive urinary symptom may have contributed to the pace of work-up; vitals alone should not have closed the question of an underlying structural or infiltrative cause.
  3d. Abdomen soft, non-tender on admission; later noted liver and spleen not palpable (Day 1): Decision Point: Type Closing — Insight: Absence of hepatosplenomegaly reduces (without excluding) probability of a hemolytic or infiltrative hepatosplenic process, but the urinary-stream complaint is not addressed by an abdominal exam that reports the abdomen as unremarkable.
  Finding listed but not visibly followed up in the text: The poor urinary stream/dribbling is not mentioned again in any of the subsequent daily notes (Day 1–4) — no renal, bladder, or genital examination or urology-specific comment is recorded in the fetched case text.
  System 1 vs System 2: Pallor recognition and the pivot to "anemia under evaluation" reads as a System 1 pattern match (pallor → anemia work-up). The urinary complaint, by contrast, needed a deliberate System 2 step (specific GU examination/imaging) that is not evidenced in the available text.

NODE 4 — Investigations
  4a. Laboratory investigations on admission and Day 1/2/3/4: Results are presented only as embedded images in the source page; the fetched text does not contain OCR'd values, so specific CBC indices, renal function, urine, or culture-sensitivity results cannot be verified against the primary text from this fetch. This is flagged as a data-availability gap, not a fabricated finding.
  4b. Serum LDH 154, peripheral smear — microcytic hypochromic anemia (Day 1, text-confirmed): Decision Point: Type Pivotal — Insight: A microcytic hypochromic picture with a non-elevated LDH points toward iron deficiency rather than a haemolytic process, and directly supports the shift from "anemia under evaluation" to "iron deficiency anemia" made on Day 2.
  4c. Culture-sensitivity (c/s) report referenced on Day 3 ("Norfloxacin started night after getting c/s reports"): Decision Point: Type Pivotal (implied) — Insight: The text confirms a culture was sent and returned, and that an antibiotic was chosen based on it, but does not state the source of the culture (urine vs blood vs other) or the organism — this is a specific point where the DRM cannot confirm the reasoning chain from the available text and should not be inferred.
  4d. Empirical antimalarial therapy (Inj. Artesunate) started same day as admission, before any malaria-specific result is described in the fetched text: Decision Point: Type Missed/Bifurcating — Insight: Starting artesunate on admission suggests the treating team suspected malaria (consistent with the rigor-associated fever), but the text does not show a peripheral smear for malarial parasites or a rapid antigen test result justifying this — a gap between action and documented evidence in the fetched page.
  Unused/unclear results: The urinary complaint has no corresponding investigation (urine routine/microscopy, renal ultrasound) documented anywhere in the fetched text, despite being one of the four presenting complaints.
  Missing investigations (per available text): No renal/abdominal ultrasound, no urine routine/microscopy or urine culture, and no explicit malaria smear/antigen result are documented in the fetched case text, despite clinical indications for each.

NODE 5 — Diagnostic Synthesis
  Working diagnosis reached: Iron deficiency anemia (revised from "anemia under evaluation" on Day 2/03-03-2021).
  Point of closure: After the Day 1 peripheral smear (microcytic hypochromic) and LDH result.
  Closure type: Analytical — the smear morphology plus normal LDH is a reasonable, evidence-based basis for favouring iron deficiency over haemolysis.
  However, this is also a Premature closure with respect to the urinary complaint: the working diagnosis explains the anemia and fits the fatigue/appetite-loss picture, but it does not address or formally exclude the poor urinary stream that was one of the four presenting complaints — that branch appears abandoned rather than explicitly ruled out in the fetched text.
  Open branches at closure: (1) cause of the underlying iron deficiency in a 14-year-old boy — dietary vs occult GI blood loss (the "dark coloured stools" on Day 1 is a red flag pointing toward possible melena that is not explicitly reconciled with "iron deficiency anemia" as a closed diagnosis); (2) the cause of the urinary obstructive symptom; (3) the source of the culture-proven infection treated with norfloxacin.
  Synthesis insight: The team correctly used smear morphology to move from a broad "anemia under evaluation" to a specific mechanistic diagnosis (iron deficiency), which is sound reasoning for the hematologic branch — but the map shows the urinary and possible GI-bleeding branches being left open without documented closure, which is the more clinically important gap given a "dark stools" symptom in a newly diagnosed anemic adolescent.

BIAS AUDIT OVERLAY
  Anchoring: Node 3a/5 — Once pallor and a compatible smear pattern were found, the team appears to have anchored on "iron deficiency anemia" as sufficient explanation for the whole presentation, with the urinary complaint not revisited in subsequent notes.
  Availability: Not clearly identifiable from the fetched text — insufficient information to flag with confidence (no evidence of a specific recent/memorable case biasing this team is present in the source).
  Premature closure: Node 5 — The working diagnosis was finalized without the dark-coloured-stool finding or the urinary-stream complaint being explicitly reconciled or excluded in the documented notes.
  Framing: Node 1 — The provisional diagnosis was framed as "? Anemia under evaluation" from the outset (before urinary or GI-bleed causes were explored), which may have channelled subsequent documentation toward the hematologic branch preferentially.
  Omission bias: Node 4 — No urine studies or renal imaging are documented despite an obstructive urinary complaint being one of the four presenting complaints; this is a plausible under-investigation gap, though it is possible imaging/urine tests were done but not captured in the fetched text (images not parsed).

HIGHEST-YIELD DECISION POINT
  Node: 2b / Node 4 (poor urinary stream with dribbling → absence of any documented urine or renal-imaging work-up)
  Why: This complaint was present for 10 days, is a genuinely distinct symptom from the anemia/fever picture, and is never revisited in any of the four subsequent daily notes in the fetched text. Earlier recognition — with a directed genitourinary exam, urine microscopy, and renal/bladder imaging — would either have identified a separate diagnosis (e.g., obstructive uropathy, mass lesion) or definitively closed that branch, rather than leaving it as an unresolved thread beside the anemia diagnosis.
