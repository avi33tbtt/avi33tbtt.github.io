DIAGNOSTIC REASONING MAP — case_045 / 50F, symmetric polyarthritis x10 years (worsening x1 year), Rheumatoid Arthritis
Date: 2026-08-18
Module: DRM — Diagnostic Reasoning Map
Source: https://102-crazymandadi.blogspot.com/2021/04/1601006114-long-case.html

NODE 1 — Presenting Complaint
  Finding: 50F homemaker, pain and stiffness in several joints x1 year (on a background of a 10-year insidious course)
  Decision Point: Yes — Type: Bifurcating
  Insight: Chronic, insidious-onset small-joint polyarthritis with morning stiffness in a middle-aged woman should immediately activate an inflammatory arthropathy pattern (rheumatoid arthritis being the prototype) versus a degenerative pattern (osteoarthritis) — the semantic qualifiers "MCP joint onset" and "morning stiffness improving with movement" are the two most decision-relevant descriptors present from the very first sentence, since DIP-sparing small-joint involvement with prolonged morning stiffness classically favors inflammatory over degenerative arthritis.

NODE 2 — History
  2a. Onset in right-hand MCP joints 10 years ago, spreading to both hands (wrist, elbow) within 6 months, then feet/ankle within 4 years: Decision Point: Pivotal — Insight: A symmetric, additive spreading pattern from small hand joints outward to larger joints over years is a classic rheumatoid arthritis trajectory, and this progression pattern alone substantially narrows the differential before any exam or lab data — this is the single history feature most responsible for privileging RA over OA from the outset.
  2b. Morning stiffness >1 hour, improving with movement: Decision Point: Pivotal — Insight: Prolonged morning stiffness that improves with activity is a core discriminator between inflammatory arthritis (RA) and osteoarthritis, where stiffness is typically brief (<30 min) and pain classically worsens with use — this single history feature does much of the discriminatory work in this case.
  2c. Occasional exacerbations with fever, no weight loss, no DIP joint involvement, no butterfly rash, no chorea: Decision Point: Closing — Insight: Explicitly documenting the absence of a malar rash and chorea closes off systemic lupus erythematosus as a leading alternative, and explicit DIP sparing closes off a pattern more typical of osteoarthritis or psoriatic arthritis, which together is efficient, well-targeted history-taking for narrowing between the two stated differentials.
  2d. Menopause at 47 (3 years before symptom escalation), no thyroid/DM/HTN history: Decision Point: Missed (as a discriminator) — Insight: The temporal relationship between perimenopause/menopause and inflammatory arthritis onset/flare is a recognized association (estrogen's immunomodulatory role) but is not explicitly discussed or connected to the case's reasoning, despite menstrual history being documented.
  Missing history: No smoking history (a well-established RA risk factor and severity modifier), no family history of autoimmune disease beyond a blanket denial, no explicit dry eyes/dry mouth screening despite an extra-articular exam section later checking for sicca symptoms, no functional/disability assessment (e.g., HAQ-type functional impact) despite 1 year of "unbearable" pain limiting activity.

NODE 3 — Examination
  3a. Symmetric swelling and decreased range of motion at PIP, MCP, wrist, elbow, ankle joints; joint capsule swelling; positive squeeze test: Decision Point: Pivotal — Insight: Symmetric small-and-large joint synovitis with a positive squeeze test (MCP/MTP compression tenderness) is a classical RA examination finding and objectively confirms the pattern suggested by history, closing off a purely mechanical/degenerative process which would not typically produce this symmetric inflammatory pattern.
  3b. No deformities (e.g., no swan-neck, boutonnière, ulnar deviation): Decision Point: Closing — Insight: Absence of the classic late RA deformities is consistent with earlier/moderate-stage disease despite the long symptom duration, or reflects reasonably effective symptom control over the years — this finding appropriately does not rule out RA but calibrates disease-stage expectations.
  3c. Extra-articular exam: no episcleritis/scleritis/sicca, no hearing loss, no muscle atrophy, no xerostomia/parotid enlargement/dysphagia, no lymphadenopathy: Decision Point: Closing — Insight: A systematic and appropriately thorough extra-articular screen closes off several RA-associated or overlapping conditions (Sjögren's overlap, vasculitic complications) at this visit, which is good practice for a case this chronic.
  3d. CVS/RS/CNS/abdomen unremarkable, no pedal edema: Decision Point: Closing — Insight: Absence of pericarditis signs, normal JVP, and no pedal edema argue against a cardiac extra-articular RA complication or an alternative systemic process at this time.
  System 1 nodes: Symmetric small-joint swelling + morning stiffness pattern → RA pattern recognition (immediate, before labs)
  System 2 nodes: Deliberately screening for lupus features (butterfly rash), chorea, and DIP involvement to differentiate RA from its key mimics — this appears to be active System 2 reasoning built into the history-taking itself

NODE 4 — Investigations
  4a. Rheumatoid factor: strongly positive: Decision Point: Pivotal — Insight: A strongly positive RF substantially raises post-test probability of RA, though RF alone is not fully specific (also positive in other connective tissue diseases and a minority of the general population), so it supports but does not singlehandedly confirm the diagnosis.
  4b. Anti-CCP antibodies: negative: Decision Point: Bifurcating — Insight: This is the most clinically interesting result in the case — anti-CCP is more specific for RA than RF, and a negative anti-CCP alongside a strongly positive RF creates a genuine branch point (seropositive-RF/seronegative-CCP RA is a recognized subgroup, but this combination should also keep alternative RF-positive conditions, such as Sjögren's syndrome or other connective tissue disease, in view rather than closing the differential purely on RF).
  4c. ESR elevated, CRP positive: Decision Point: Closing — Insight: Elevated inflammatory markers confirm an active inflammatory process is underway, supporting the RA diagnosis over a purely mechanical/degenerative process, though these markers are nonspecific to RA itself.
  4d. X-ray: decreased joint space, osteoporosis (juxta-articular), mild erosions: Decision Point: Pivotal — Insight: Periarticular osteoporosis with early erosions and joint space narrowing is a classic radiographic triad for RA and is the most objective, disease-specific finding in the whole workup — erosive change in particular argues strongly against osteoarthritis (which shows osteophytes and asymmetric joint space loss rather than erosions).
  Unused results: The anti-CCP negative result (Node 4b) is not explicitly discussed or reconciled with the strongly positive RF in the source's own documentation — it is reported but its interpretive tension with the RF result is not addressed.
  Missing investigations: No documented CBC, LFTs, or RFTs results despite being listed as ordered (relevant both for baseline disease-activity assessment and for pre-treatment screening before starting steroids), no synovial fluid analysis, no formal disease activity score (e.g., DAS28) despite ESR/CRP/joint counts being available to calculate one.

NODE 5 — Diagnostic Synthesis
  Working diagnosis reached: Rheumatoid Arthritis
  Closure type: Analytical closure (System 2) — the diagnosis is reached through a logical convergence of a classical history pattern, confirmatory symmetric small-joint synovitis on exam, supportive serology (RF+, elevated inflammatory markers), and confirmatory radiographic erosive changes, representing a well-reasoned, criterion-consistent synthesis rather than a snap pattern-match alone.
  Alternatives open at closure: Osteoarthritis, the only other differential explicitly listed, is reasonably closed by the erosive/osteoporotic X-ray pattern (OA would show osteophytes, not erosions) and by the strongly positive RF and elevated inflammatory markers, which OA would not produce — this branch is adequately ruled out. However, the anti-CCP-negative result was never explicitly reconciled, leaving a minor open question about whether a broader connective-tissue-disease workup (e.g., ANA, Sjögren's-specific antibodies) might have been warranted given the RF-positive/anti-CCP-negative combination.
  Synthesis insight: This case is a good example of well-supported, multi-modality diagnostic convergence (history + exam + serology + imaging all aligning) rather than a diagnosis resting on a single finding, which is reassuring from a reasoning-quality standpoint — the main gap is not in reaching the correct diagnosis but in not explicitly addressing the anti-CCP-negative result's implications.

HIGHEST-YIELD DECISION POINT
  Node: 4b — anti-CCP negative despite strongly positive RF, not reconciled
  Why: This is the single point in the case where the data itself generated a discrepancy (RF+/anti-CCP−) that could have prompted either a broader autoimmune workup or an explicit statement of why RA remains the most likely diagnosis despite this combination — recognizing and explicitly addressing serological discordance, rather than letting a strong result (RF) silently outweigh a discordant one (anti-CCP), is the most transferable reasoning lesson from this otherwise well-reasoned case.
