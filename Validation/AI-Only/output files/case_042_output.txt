  Decision Point: Yes — Type: Pivotal
  Insight: A year-long palpitation history that precedes and outlasts a progressive SOB/chest-pain picture strongly activates an arrhythmia-driven cardiac pattern (e.g., atrial fibrillation leading to tachycardia-mediated or decompensated heart failure) rather than a purely ischemic-onset presentation, and the progressive functional decline (grade 2→4 dyspnea) is itself decision-relevant as a marker of worsening cardiac reserve.

  2b.   2c. Functional decline severe enough that patient stopped farming x1 year due to chest pain and SOB: Decision Point: Pivotal — Insight: A full year of activity limitation from cardiopulmonary symptoms indicates this is a chronic, progressive process rather than an acute event, and is the single history detail that most reframes the case from "acute cardiac event" to "decompensation of a long-standing, previously under-investigated cardiac condition."
  2d. Hospitalization 2 months ago for abdominal pain (Khammam): Decision Point: Missed — Insight: A prior hospitalization for abdominal pain shortly before this presentation is a potentially decision-relevant prior encounter (possibly an earlier presentation of the same epigastric/cardiac overlap), but no discharge diagnosis or workup from that admission is documented here, leaving this branch unexplored.

Not a known case of DM, HTN, TB, Asthma, Epilepsy: Decision Point: Closing — Insight: Absence of previously diagnosed hypertension or diabetes is notable given the eventual CAD/HFrEF diagnosis — it suggests either undiagnosed risk factors or a presentation not primarily driven by classic metabolic risk factors, which is itself worth flagging rather than assuming.
