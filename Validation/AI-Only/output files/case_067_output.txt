NODE 2 — History
  2a. Left hemiplegia 4 years ago (prior CVA), now diagnosed with hypertension and diabetes on regular medication: Decision Point: Pivotal — Insight: A prior stroke with concurrent hypertension and diabetes establishes this patient as having significant, long-standing cardiovascular risk, making a cardiac cause of the current presentation (rather than a purely pulmonary or renal one) the leading hypothesis from the outset.
  2b. SOB grade 2-3, associated with orthopnea and PND: Decision Point: Pivotal — Insight: Orthopnea and paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea are classically specific symptoms for left-sided heart failure (pulmonary venous congestion), and their presence here strongly collapses the differential toward a cardiac decompensation process rather than a primary pulmonary or renal cause.
  2c. Pedal edema on and off for 1 year, worse in evenings: Decision Point: Closing — Insight: Edema that fluctuates with a clear evening/dependent pattern over a full year is more consistent with a chronic, intermittently-compensated cardiac process than with a purely renal etiology, which typically produces more constant edema; this supports (though does not alone confirm) the heart-failure framework.
  2d. No history of fever; complaints of nocturnal micturition: Decision Point: Closing — Insight: Absence of fever argues against an infectious precipitant for the acute presentation, and nocturnal micturition (nocturia) is consistent with recumbency-related redistribution of fluid seen in heart failure, reinforcing rather than contradicting the cardiac hypothesis.
  Most important history feature: The combination of orthopnea and PND with a prior stroke and known hypertension/diabetes — this trio most directly and specifically points toward cardiac decompensation as the driver of the current presentation, more so than any single other feature.
  Missing history: No documentation of prior echocardiography or known baseline ejection fraction from the time of the stroke 4 years ago; no explicit description of the chest pain's character (exertional, pleuritic, positional) to help distinguish ischemic from non-ischemic causes — these gaps should not be filled in from assumption.
