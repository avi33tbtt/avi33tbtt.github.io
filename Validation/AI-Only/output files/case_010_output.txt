DIAGNOSTIC REASONING MAP — Case_010 (33F, recurrent oral erosions + truncal lesions x10mo)
Module: DRM — Diagnostic Reasoning Map

NODE 1 — Presenting Complaint
  Finding: 33F housewife, recurrent oral erosions x10mo + lesions over abdomen/back x10mo,
  history relayed by daughter (patient unable to speak at presentation)
  Decision Point: Yes — Type: Bifurcating
  Insight: Recurrent, cyclical mucocutaneous lesions (oral + truncal skin) over 10 months
  immediately splits the differential into an autoimmune/immunobullous process (e.g., pemphigus
  vulgaris, erythema multiforme) vs. an infectious/recurrent process (e.g., recurrent HSV) —
  and the fact that the patient herself cannot currently speak is itself a red flag that should
  be triaged as urgent (possible airway/oral involvement severity or a separate acute process)
  rather than only a history-taking inconvenience.

NODE 2 — History
  2a. Temporal/psychological trigger sequence (fall from auto with ear injury treated by RMP →
      family dispute/violence → one day of not eating → first oral lesion the day after):
      Decision Point: Missed
      Insight: The narrative explicitly links psychological stress and a period of not eating to
      the first lesion, and this causal thread is documented in detail but never explicitly
      evaluated against a specific mechanism (e.g., stress-triggered HSV reactivation, or a
      drug reaction to the RMP-prescribed analgesic) — the case as written leaves this link
      dangling rather than testing it.
  2b. Cyclical pattern — lesions appear in a line, heal in 7-10 days, followed by symptom-free
      periods, then recur: Decision Point: Pivotal
      Insight: A relapsing-remitting course with lesions appearing "in a line" (dermatomal-
      suggestive language) followed by healing cycles is classically suggestive of recurrent
      HSV or erythema multiforme triggered by HSV — this pattern description is the single
      strongest clue in the entire history toward the eventual "erythema multiforme 2° to HSV"
      lead diagnosis.
  2c. Occasional painful red eyes, attributed by family to lack of sleep: Decision Point: Missed
      Insight: Ocular involvement alongside oral/genital-region mucosal disease is a decision
      point for the classic mucocutaneous triad (oral-ocular-genital), directly relevant to
      Behçet's disease (explicitly on the provisional differential) — the family's attribution
      to poor sleep appears to have been accepted rather than independently assessed by the
      treating team, which the text does not show being challenged.
  2d. No lesions on vaginal/anal regions, no joint pain, no chest pain/palpitations/SOB, no
      focal neuro deficit/seizures: Decision Point: Closing
      Insight: Directly reduces support for Behçet's (typically has genital ulcers) and for a
      systemic vasculitic/connective-tissue process with joint or cardiopulmonary involvement.
  2e. Aggravating factors per family: eating chicken and brinjal: Decision Point: Missed
      Insight: A reported food-trigger association is a soft clue toward either a
      food-hypersensitivity/allergic contribution or simply parallel timing coincidentally
      reported by family — the case doesn't interrogate whether this reflects a true
      dietary-trigger pattern (relevant to some erythema multiforme and aphthous ulcer variants)
      or anecdotal correlation.
  Single most important history: 2b (the cyclical, line-forming, self-healing lesion pattern) —
  most directly supports the eventual leading diagnosis and should have been weighted earliest.
  Missing history: No explicit question about prior cold sores/genital herpes episodes to
  support the "secondary to HSV" qualifier; no medication history beyond the single RMP
  analgesic course; history obtained secondhand via daughter due to patient's inability to
  speak, and the reason for that inability to speak is never explained in the text — a gap that
  itself constitutes a missing decision point.

NODE 3 — Examination
  3a. Multiple erythematous papules over abdomen and trunk (posteriorly): Decision Point:
      Bifurcating
      Insight: Truncal distribution alongside oral erosions supports a systemic mucocutaneous
      process rather than a purely localized oral condition, keeping both erythema multiforme
      and an immunobullous disorder (pemphigus) live.
  3b. Vaginal mucosa with curdy white discharge: Decision Point: Closing (partial) / New branch
      Insight: This finding is not explained by any of the four listed provisional diagnoses and
      instead suggests a co-existing candidal vaginitis — potentially relevant as a marker of an
      underlying immunocompromised or diabetic state, or simply an incidental finding, but it is
      documented in exam without being folded into the differential or explained anywhere in the
      write-up.
  3c. Multiple erosions over buccal mucosa and hard palate, white coated tongue, white adherent
      plaque on dorsal tongue surface: Decision Point: Pivotal
      Insight: Oral candidiasis (white adherent plaque, "coated tongue") appearing alongside
      erosions is directly relevant to the "chronic candidiasis" branch of the provisional
      differential, but could equally represent secondary oral candidiasis complicating
      pre-existing mucosal erosions (opportunistic overgrowth on damaged mucosa) rather than the
      primary driver — this distinction is not resolved in the text.
  System 1 nodes: Oral erosions + truncal lesions pattern-matched quickly to a mucocutaneous
  syndrome category.
  System 2 nodes: Weighing the four-way provisional differential (erythema multiforme/HSV vs.
  aphthous ulcers vs. Behçet's vs. chronic candidiasis) against the negative findings (no
  genital ulcers, no joint pain) — this is explicit analytical work reflected in the differential
  list itself.
  Unfollowed finding: The curdy white vaginal discharge (3b) is examined and documented but never
  connected to any of the four listed differentials or investigated further (e.g., no blood
  glucose or HbA1c mentioned to assess for a diabetes-driven candidiasis pattern).

  4b. CUE — Albumin: +, sugars: nil, pus cells 3-4, epithelial casts 2-3: Decision Point:

  4c. HIV — non-reactive: Decision Point: Closing
      Insight: Rules out HIV-associated immunosuppression as the driver of recurrent

  4d. ~13 additional image-only investigation/photo files (skin/oral lesion photos, other labs):

  Unused/unverifiable results: The image-only investigations are referenced by position in the
  post but not described in text.
  Missing investigations (as documented in text): No stated HSV PCR/serology, no Tzanck smear, no
  skin/mucosal biopsy result described in text, no autoimmune/vasculitis workup (ANA, etc.)

NODE 5 — Diagnostic Synthesis
  Working diagnosis reached: Recurrent erythema multiforme secondary to HSV (lead diagnosis);
  differentials — chronic aphthous ulcers, Behçet's disease, chronic candidiasis
  Point of closure: Not reached — this remains an open, four-item provisional differential
  rather than a single closed diagnosis; the map's "closure" is a ranked hypothesis list, not a
  final synthesis.
  Classification of leap: This node is explicitly NOT closed (appropriately) — the case is
  presented as an open provisional differential. Where a leap was made, it is a
  pattern-recognition closure (System 1) ranking erythema multiforme/HSV highest based on the
  cyclical lesion pattern (2b), but the text does not show confirmatory testing (HSV PCR,
  Tzanck, biopsy) that would convert this from a leading hypothesis to a demonstrated diagnosis.
  Open branches at closure: All three alternative differentials (aphthous ulcers, Behçet's,
  chronic candidiasis) remain genuinely open per the text — none is explicitly ruled in or out
  by a confirmatory test described in prose. The curdy vaginal discharge (3b) is also an
  unresolved thread not integrated into any of the four differentials.
  Synthesis insight: This case is a good example of appropriately NOT collapsing to premature
  closure — the differential stays open and ranked rather than falsely resolved — but the
  reasoning chain would benefit from an explicit statement of what specific test result would be
  needed to separate the top two differentials (HSV-driven erythema multiforme vs. Behçet's),
  since as written that decisive test is referenced only as an unlabeled image, not a stated plan.

HIGHEST-YIELD DECISION POINT
  Node: 4d / Node 5 (the undocumented confirmatory test)
  Why: With four genuinely plausible differentials still open at the point this record ends, the
  single highest-yield move is identifying and obtaining the discriminating test — for this
  presentation, that would most likely be an HSV PCR/serology or Tzanck smear (to confirm/refute
