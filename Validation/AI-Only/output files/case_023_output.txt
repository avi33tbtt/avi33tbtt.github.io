DIAGNOSTIC REASONING MAP — 60-year-old woman with left loin pain, decreased urine output, and burning micturition (AKI on CKD, solitary kidney)

NODE 1 — Presenting Complaint
  Finding: Pain abdomen in the left loin region x10 days, decreased urine output x10 days, burning micturition x10 days.
  Decision Point: Yes — Type: Pivotal
  Insight: Left loin pain with concurrent oliguria and dysuria over the same 10-day window, in a patient who (as later revealed) has only one functioning kidney, should immediately be treated as a potential single-kidney urinary tract obstruction/infection until proven otherwise — this is a scenario where delay carries a disproportionately high risk of irreversible renal loss.

  2c. Suprapubic pain with burning sensation 7 years back, told she had a "growth on cervix," underwent hysterectomy: Decision Point: Type Bifurcating — Insight: A history of pelvic surgery raises the possibility of ureteric injury, stricture, or retroperitoneal fibrosis as a chronic contributor to obstructive uropathy in the remaining kidney — this is a plausible mechanistic link to the current presentation that is not explicitly explored in the text.

Known hypertensive x5 years on medication; not diabetic, no TB, no asthma: Decision Point: Type Closing — Insight: Long-standing hypertension is a plausible chronic contributor to background renal impairment (hypertensive nephrosclerosis) layered onto the solitary-kidney status, consistent with a CKD baseline onto which an acute insult (AKI) is now superimposed.
