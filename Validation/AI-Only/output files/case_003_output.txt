DIAGNOSTIC REASONING MAP — Case 003: 42F, fever + altered sensorium + irrelevant talking + focal involuntary movements
Date: 2021-03-02 (admission)
Module: DRM — Diagnostic Reasoning Map

NODE 1 — Presenting Complaint
  Finding: Fever (5d, high-grade, intermittent, chills/rigors) + altered sensorium (3d) + irrelevant talking (4d) + today's involuntary RT UL movements
  Decision Point: Yes — Type: Bifurcating
  Insight: Fever + evolving encephalopathy over days (not hours) with a new focal motor event should immediately split the differential into CNS infection (meningoencephalitis, viral vs. tubercular vs. bacterial) vs. non-infectious encephalopathy (metabolic, toxic, autoimmune), rather than anchoring on either branch this early.

NODE 2 — History
  2a. Subacute febrile prodrome (5d) with headache and vomiting on day 1: Decision Point — Bifurcating — Insight: Headache + vomiting preceding sensorium change supports raised ICP/meningeal irritation but is nonspecific; keeps infectious and non-infectious causes both live.
  2b. Progression fever → headache → irrelevant talking → confusion → focal involuntary movements over ~5 days: Decision Point — Pivotal — Insight: The tempo (days, not hours/weeks) narrows toward acute/subacute infectious meningoencephalitis over both hyperacute (stroke/status) and chronic (neurodegenerative) processes.
  2c. Farm labourer occupation, no documented travel/exposure history: Decision Point — Missed — Insight: Occupational exposure (rural/agricultural) raises pretest probability for endemic causes (e.g., scrub typhus, leptospirosis, Japanese encephalitis, TB) that were never explicitly elicited or excluded in the note as presented.
  2d. No prior comorbidities (no DM/HTN/asthma/epilepsy/CAD/CVA), no prior hospital visits: Decision Point — Closing — Insight: Absence of a prior seizure disorder or vascular risk factors lowers prior probability of a primary epileptic or cerebrovascular explanation for the involuntary movements, favoring a new acute insult (infectious/metabolic).
  Most important history: The 5-day tempo from fever to progressive encephalopathy to focal motor signs — this is the feature that most shapes the differential toward meningoencephalitis.
  Missing history: Exact character/duration of involuntary movements (focal seizure vs. myoclonus vs. dystonia — not described), animal/water exposure, mosquito exposure, immunization status, any rash, sick contacts — none of these are documented in the case as presented.

NODE 3 — Examination
  3a. GCS E4V1M4, drowsy but arousable: Decision Point — Pivotal — Insight: A verbal score of 1 (no verbalization) with intact eye opening indicates a significant global encephalopathy rather than a focal cortical deficit alone.
  3b. Neck stiffness "+?" (documented with uncertainty): Decision Point — Missed — Insight: An equivocally documented meningeal sign in a febrile encephalopathic patient is exactly the kind of finding that should trigger explicit re-examination rather than being left ambiguous; as recorded, it neither confirms nor excludes meningitis.
  3c. Hypertonia in all four limbs with exaggerated reflexes: Decision Point — Bifurcating — Insight: Diffuse upper motor neuron signs without lateralization support a diffuse encephalitic/encephalopathic process over a focal structural lesion, but bilateral pyramidal signs can occur in both meningoencephalitis and severe metabolic derangement.
  3d. Higher mental functions "intact" documented alongside disorientation to time/place/person: Decision Point — Missed — Insight: This is an internal inconsistency in the exam as recorded (higher mental function cannot be intact if the patient is disoriented in all three spheres) — a charting/reasoning gap that was not flagged or reconciled in the source note.
  3e. Vitals essentially normal (BP 130/90, HR 94, RR 21, SpO2 97%), GRBS 112: Decision Point — Closing — Insight: A normal random glucose and stable vitals lower (but do not eliminate) probability of hypoglycemic or acute hemodynamic causes of the encephalopathy at this point.
  System 1 nodes: Recognizing fever + meningismus + altered sensorium as a "meningoencephalitis" pattern (rapid pattern match, appropriate here).
  System 2 nodes: Reconciling the intact-vs-disoriented HMF discrepancy; interpreting the equivocal neck stiffness; weighing viral vs. tubercular vs. metabolic etiology — none of these were worked through analytically in the note as presented.

NODE 4 — Investigations
  4a. Lumbar puncture — CSF: neutrophils 10%, lymphocytes 90%, normal sugar/protein/chloride: Decision Point — Bifurcating — Insight: Lymphocyte-predominant CSF with normal glucose/protein favors a viral process over bacterial or classic TB meningitis (which more typically shows low glucose/high protein), but does not exclude early/partially-treated TB or atypical presentations.
  4b. Fundoscopy — no papilledema: Decision Point — Closing — Insight: Absence of papilledema makes clinically significant raised ICP from a mass lesion less likely, supporting proceeding with LP (which was done) rather than delaying for imaging-only reassurance.
  4c. ATT started (Day 7) despite CSF picture more consistent with viral than TB: Decision Point — Bifurcating/Unused — Insight: The shift in working diagnosis toward "?tubercular" on Day 7 is not clearly justified by any new CSF or imaging data presented in the case (no repeat CSF, no CSF ADA, no imaging findings described) — this is a decision point where the reasoning behind escalation to anti-tubercular therapy is not traceable from the data given.
  4d. Falcigo (antimalarial) given empirically without a documented positive peripheral smear/malaria antigen result in the case text: Decision Point — Unused/Missed — Insight: Empiric antimalarial coverage was added without the causative lab result being shown, so the decision point (positive test result) that should justify it is not visible in the source data — a fabrication-risk-adjacent gap for any output that assumes a positive malaria test occurred.
  Unused results: MRI brain and CSF C/S, blood C/S, urine C/S results were ordered but their findings are not reported in the text provided — the decision points they should have generated cannot be evaluated from this source.
  Missing investigations: No CSF ADA, no CSF PCR (viral panel/TB PCR), no explicit MRI findings reported — all of which would be required to adjudicate viral vs. tubercular with more confidence than the treatment course implies.

NODE 5 — Diagnostic Synthesis
  Closure type: Forced/Pattern-driven — the diagnosis evolved reactively (viral encephalitis → viral/metabolic encephalopathy → meningoencephalitis ?viral ?tubercular) alongside clinical deterioration rather than a single, evidence-anchored synthesis point.
  Open branches at closure: Viral encephalitis, tubercular meningoencephalitis, and (briefly) malaria and metabolic encephalopathy were all treated empirically in parallel (acyclovir + ATT + antimalarial + steroids) without the case text showing the confirmatory result that closed any one branch definitively.
  Synthesis insight: This case reflects real-world diagnostic uncertainty management (empiric multi-cover treatment while awaiting/without confirmatory results) rather than a clean diagnostic leap — the final working label "?viral ?tubercular" is explicitly left open in the source, which the DRM should preserve rather than force into a false closure.

BIAS AUDIT OVERLAY
  Anchoring: Node 1 — Early framing as "meningoencephalitis" in the case title may have anchored subsequent documentation, though the differential was kept broad in practice (multiple empiric covers given).
  Availability: Node 4c — Addition of ATT on Day 7 without new confirmatory data is consistent with availability/practice-pattern bias (TB is locally prevalent) rather than a data-driven update.
  Premature closure: Not clearly present — the team notably avoided premature closure by maintaining broad empiric cover; if anything the risk here is the opposite (diffuse, non-targeted escalation).
  Framing: Node 1 — The blog post's own title ("?Meningoencephalitis? Viral/Tubercular") frames the case before any findings are presented, which could bias a reader's (or an AI's) interpretation of ambiguous findings like the "+?" neck stiffness toward confirming that frame.
  Commission bias: Node 4 — Sequential addition of acyclovir, antimalarial, and ATT without documented confirmatory results for each represents commission-leaning empiric escalation as the patient worsened.

HIGHEST-YIELD DECISION POINT
  Node: 4c/4d — Escalating antimicrobial/antitubercular/antimalarial coverage without documented confirmatory lab results driving each addition.
  Why: In a deteriorating encephalopathic patient, empiric broad coverage is often justifiable, but the reasoning map loses auditability when each addition isn't explicitly tied to a result. Earlier, explicit documentation of why ATT and antimalarial therapy were added (which specific result triggered each) would make this map — and any future comparable case — far more teachable and defensible.
