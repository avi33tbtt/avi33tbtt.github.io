NODE 1 — Presenting Complaint
  Finding: 47M, pedal edema + facial puffiness x6 days, SOB x6 days (worsening x3 days), decreased urine output x3 days
  Decision Point: Yes — Type: Bifurcating
  Insight: Acute-on-chronic anasarca (pedal edema, facial puffiness) with progressive SOB and oliguria in a patient with an 8-year hypertension and 6-year diabetes history should immediately activate the two leading candidates named in the provisional diagnosis itself — decompensated heart failure and diabetic nephropathy/CKD — and the combination of both symptom sets (facial puffiness typically renal-weighted, orthopnea/PND typically cardiac-weighted) means this case genuinely opens with two live, overlapping branches rather than one dominant pattern.

NODE 2 — History
  2a. Hypertension diagnosed 8 years ago, diabetes diagnosed 6 years ago (with antecedent weakness/weight loss): Decision Point: Pivotal — Insight: Nearly a decade of hypertension plus 6 years of diabetes is the single most important piece of history in the case — this duration is long enough for both hypertensive/diabetic cardiomyopathy and diabetic nephropathy to have developed, and is why both provisional diagnoses are simultaneously plausible rather than mutually exclusive (cardiorenal syndrome is a real third possibility not explicitly named).
  2b. Acute pancreatitis 5 years ago: Decision Point: Missed — Insight: A history of acute pancreatitis is not clearly connected to the current presentation in the note — it could be relevant if related to alcohol use (see 2c) or if it reflects an underlying metabolic syndrome/hypertriglyceridemia predisposition, but this branch is left unexplored.
  2c. Alcohol and smoking both stopped 5 years ago (around the time of the pancreatitis episode): Decision Point: Missed — Insight: The temporal coincidence between stopping alcohol/smoking and the pancreatitis episode is suggestive (possibly alcohol-related pancreatitis prompting cessation) but is not explicitly stated or confirmed, and past alcohol use is also relevant background for assessing cardiomyopathy risk (alcoholic cardiomyopathy), which is not discussed.
  2d. SOB progressing Grade II-III to Grade IV over 3 days, with orthopnea and PND: Decision Point: Pivotal — Insight: Orthopnea and PND are relatively specific for a cardiac (left heart failure) contribution to the dyspnea, rather than being purely explained by renal fluid overload alone, and this detail meaningfully weights the differential toward a real cardiac component being present, supporting the "?Heart Failure" arm rather than treating it as a pure renal question.
  2e. No chest pain, palpitations, giddiness, fever, cold, cough, burning micturition: Decision Point: Closing — Insight: Absence of chest pain reduces (but does not eliminate, especially in a diabetic where ischemia can be silent) likelihood of an acute coronary event as the proximate trigger; absence of fever/cough reduces likelihood of a superimposed infective/pneumonic contributor to the SOB.
  Missing history: No diabetes control history (HbA1c trend, prior complications screening — retinopathy, neuropathy), no prior echocardiogram or renal function baseline despite 6-8 years of relevant risk factors, no medication adherence history for the Olmesartan/Glimy M2, no family history of renal or cardiac disease, no explicit alcohol quantity/duration before cessation (relevant to both pancreatitis and cardiomyopathy risk).

NODE 3 — Examination
  3a. BP 180/100, PR 94: Decision Point: Pivotal — Insight: Markedly elevated BP despite being on Olmesartan for known hypertension indicates poor control or a hypertensive component actively contributing to acute decompensation (afterload-driven heart failure, or hypertensive nephrosclerosis progression) — this is a significant, actionable finding that directly informs both differential branches.
  3b. GRBS 166 mg/dl: Decision Point: Closing — Insight: Mild hyperglycemia (not markedly elevated) suggests glycemic control, while imperfect, is not the dominant acute driver of this presentation, keeping focus on the cardiorenal axis rather than a hyperglycemic-crisis-related process.
  3c. RS — bilateral basal and infra-axillary crepitations: Decision Point: Pivotal — Insight: Bilateral basal crepitations are a classical sign of pulmonary edema from left heart failure/fluid overload, and combined with the orthopnea/PND history, this is the examination finding that most concretely supports a genuine cardiac (not purely renal) contribution to the current decompensation.
  3d. Feet edema, no pallor/icterus/cyanosis/clubbing/lymphadenopathy: Decision Point: Closing — Insight: Absence of pallor is notable given that CKD (if present and longstanding) often produces anemia — its absence doesn't rule out renal involvement but suggests it may be earlier-stage or the anemia hasn't developed yet, which is a mild point in favor of the process being more acute/subacute or less advanced than end-stage.
  System 1 nodes: Anasarca + orthopnea + crepitations → cardiac decompensation pattern recognition
  System 2 nodes: Weighing which of the two provisional diagnoses (heart failure vs. CKD) is primary vs. secondary — not explicitly resolved in the note, appropriately left as "?" for both
