DIAGNOSTIC REASONING MAP — Case 061: 55-year-old male agricultural labourer with shortness of breath, facial puffiness, CKD on MHD
Date: 2026-08-18

Module: DRM — Diagnostic Reasoning Map

NODE 1 — Presenting Complaint
  Finding: Shortness of breath since 1 year; facial puffiness since 1 year.
  Decision Point: Yes — Type: Bifurcating
  Insight: Facial puffiness paired with dyspnea, especially if periorbital and worse in the morning, is a classic semantic qualifier set pointing toward either a renal (nephrotic-range proteinuria) or less commonly a cardiac/venous cause; this pairing should activate the renal pattern more strongly than isolated pedal edema alone would, since facial/periorbital edema is more specific for renal fluid retention than dependent edema is.

NODE 2 — History
  2a. Bilateral pedal edema 3 years ago, gradually progressive, preceding the facial puffiness/dyspnea by roughly 2 years: Decision Point: Missed — Insight: This earlier pedal edema episode is a decision point that appears to have been under-recognized in real time — it represents an earlier opportunity to investigate renal function that was not evidently acted upon until symptoms escalated 2 years later.
  2b. Diagnosed with hypertension at a local hospital on irregular, unspecified medication (at the time of the earlier pedal edema): Decision Point: Pivotal — Insight: Poorly controlled/irregularly treated hypertension over roughly 3 years is a strong candidate driver for progressive hypertensive nephrosclerosis, and its irregular treatment is itself a modifiable factor that likely contributed to progression.
  2c. Facial puffiness more pronounced in the morning, gradually decreasing over the day; shortness of breath at rest: Decision Point: Pivotal — Insight: Morning-predominant periorbital puffiness that improves through the day is a semantically specific pattern for renal (nephrotic-type) fluid shifts related to recumbency overnight, and is a stronger discriminating feature than the pedal edema alone.
  2d. Investigations at that time showed high creatinine and shrunken kidney size, dialysis started: Decision Point: Pivotal — Insight: Shrunken kidney size on imaging is a key finding supporting a chronic (rather than acute) renal process, and materially strengthens the CKD diagnosis over an acute kidney injury differential — this is one of the few investigation findings stated explicitly in the text rather than only in an image.
  2e. Known hypertension for 3 years on irregular Clonidine: Decision Point: Closing — Insight: Confirms hypertension as a long-standing, likely under-treated condition consistent with a hypertensive CKD etiology, though true causality (hypertension causing CKD vs. CKD causing secondary hypertension) is still not established with certainty from history alone.
  2f. No DM, asthma, TB, CAD, epilepsy: Decision Point: Closing — Insight: Excludes diabetic nephropathy as the presumed driver, leaving hypertension as the leading candidate etiology by exclusion.
  2g. Alcohol occasionally (weekly, ≤180ml) and smoking for 20 years, both stopped 10 months ago: Decision Point: Missed — Insight: As in similar cases, this substantial substance-use history is not explicitly connected to the renal or cardiovascular picture in the source text and is a potential contributing decision point left unexplored.
  Most important history feature: The combination of long-standing, irregularly treated hypertension with an initial pedal-edema episode 3 years before presentation and shrunken kidneys on imaging — together these support a genuinely chronic, likely hypertensive-nephrosclerosis process rather than a recent or acute one, distinguishing this case from the shorter-duration cases seen previously.
  Missing history: No family history of renal disease documented; no explicit mention of proteinuria symptoms (e.g., frothy urine) despite the nephrotic-pattern facial puffiness, which would have been a decision-relevant detail to seek.

NODE 3 — Examination
  3a. Pallor present: Decision Point: Closing — Insight: Consistent with anemia of chronic kidney disease from reduced erythropoietin production, fitting the chronic (3-year) disease course.
  3b. Bilateral pedal edema up to the knee: Decision Point: Pivotal — Insight: More extensive (up to knee) than in prior cases, suggesting a more significant degree of fluid overload consistent with more advanced/longstanding CKD.
  3c. BP 140/90, SpO2 98%, RR 20/min: Decision Point: Closing — Insight: Normal oxygen saturation despite the stated 1-year history of shortness of breath argues against acute severe pulmonary compromise at the time of this examination, though it does not rule out an intermittent or exertional component not captured in a single reading.
  3d. CVS — S1/S2 heard, no murmurs; RS — normal vesicular breath sounds; per abdomen soft, non-tender, no organomegaly: Decision Point: Closing (negative findings) — Insight: These negatives argue against a primary structural cardiac or hepatic cause for the facial puffiness and dyspnea, again supporting a renal origin by exclusion, though a formal echocardiogram is not documented in the text to confirm this.
  System 1 nodes: Recognition of facial puffiness + pedal edema + long-standing hypertension + shrunken kidneys as a classic CKD/hypertensive-nephrosclerosis pattern.
  System 2 nodes: Reconciling the 1-year duration of dyspnea/facial puffiness with normal SpO2 and clear lungs on a single exam — this discrepancy invites deliberate reasoning about symptom fluctuation over time rather than a single-timepoint pattern match.

NODE 5 — Diagnostic Synthesis
  Working diagnosis reached: Chronic Kidney Disease on Maintenance Hemodialysis.
  At what point reached: Approximately 10 months prior to this presentation, when facial puffiness and shortness of breath developed and investigations showed high creatinine with shrunken kidneys.
  Closure type: Pattern-recognition closure (System 1), reasonably well-supported here by the shrunken-kidney imaging finding, which adds an analytical (System 2) element not present in the other two cases reviewed.
  Open branches at closure: Whether hypertension caused the CKD or resulted from it is not formally resolved; a nephrotic-range proteinuria contribution to the facial puffiness pattern is not confirmed or excluded in the text; a cardiac contribution to the dyspnea is not formally excluded via echocardiography.
  Synthesis insight: This is the most etiologically well-supported of the three cases reviewed so far, because the shrunken-kidney finding provides objective evidence of chronicity, materially strengthening the "hypertensive CKD" conclusion beyond history and exam pattern-matching alone.
