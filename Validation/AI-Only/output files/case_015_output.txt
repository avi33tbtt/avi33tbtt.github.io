DIAGNOSTIC REASONING MAP — Case_015 (46M, SOB since morning, alcohol binge + no food x1-2d,
found to have severe metabolic acidosis + right heart failure)
Module: DRM — Diagnostic Reasoning Map

NODE 1 — Presenting Complaint
  Finding: 46M, SOB since morning (10/5/21), preceded by an episode of profuse sweating and
  rapid deterioration; conflicting histories of a possible insect/scorpion/snake bite the day
  before (patient-reported) vs. attenders stating patient was home the whole time; alcohol binge
  and no food intake since the day before
  Decision Point: Yes — Type: Bifurcating
  Insight: A conflicting envenomation history (patient says bitten, attenders say he never left
  home) alongside a clear alcohol-binge-plus-starvation history creates two competing acute
  narratives from the very first node — envenomation-related autonomic/cardiotoxic crisis vs. a
  metabolic crisis from combined alcohol/starvation — and this conflict itself is a decision
  point that should be explicitly flagged and actively resolved (e.g., examining for bite marks,
  fang marks, local tissue changes) rather than silently defaulted to one narrative, since the
  two etiologies would call for very different urgent management pathways.

NODE 2 — History
  2a. Chronic alcoholic per son, continuous drinking x2 days with no food intake: Decision Point:
      Pivotal
      Insight: Combined alcohol excess with prolonged fasting is the classic dual trigger for
      alcoholic ketoacidosis, and separately, thiamine depletion from chronic alcoholism combined
      with acute glucose/carbohydrate loading (or even ongoing catabolic stress) is the classic
      setup for acute wet beriberi (thiamine-deficiency high-output/right heart failure) — this
      single history line plausibly explains both major problems that emerge later (metabolic
      acidosis AND right heart failure) via a shared root cause (thiamine deficiency +
      starvation/alcohol), which is a strong unifying insight the case eventually converges on.
  2b. No known DM/HTN/CA/CVA/epilepsy: Decision Point: Closing
      Insight: Reduces likelihood of a pre-existing diabetic ketoacidosis as the primary driver,
      supporting the "starvation/alcoholic" (rather than diabetic) ketoacidosis branch, though
      new-onset diabetes cannot be excluded by history alone.
  2c. Conflicting bite history (2b under Node 1): Decision Point: Missed
      Insight: This conflict is never explicitly resolved in the visible text — no examination
      finding (presence/absence of bite marks, local swelling, fang marks) is documented to
      settle it one way or the other, despite envenomation being seriously considered enough to
      be part of the initial narrative.
  Single most important history: 2a (alcohol + starvation, son's account) — this single history
  item is the thread that ties together the eventual severe metabolic acidosis and right heart
  failure diagnoses via a shared thiamine-deficiency/starvation mechanism.
  Missing history: No stated duration/quantity of the "chronic alcoholic" pattern prior to this
  acute binge; no dietary history beyond the acute 1-2 day fast; no examination for bite marks
  documented in text despite the envenomation question being explicitly raised.

NODE 3 — Examination
  3a. Initial O/E (at first, non-crisis documentation): BP 110/70, PR 82, CVS S1S2+, RS BAE+,
      CNS NAD, conscious/coherent/cooperative, no cyanosis/clubbing/lymphadenopathy:
      Decision Point: Closing
      Insight: This initial exam appears deceptively reassuring/near-normal, which is notable
      given how rapidly and severely the patient deteriorated shortly afterward (per the hospital
      course) — this is a useful teaching point about how early vitals in evolving metabolic/
      cardiac crises can look falsely stable before rapid decompensation.
  3b. Hospital course vitals at crisis point: tachypneic (RR 40), hypotensive (SBP 60, required
      fluid bolus + inotropes), improved to 110/70, SpO2 ~88% on room air improving to 96% on
      oxygen, GRBS 61 mg/dL (treated with 25% dextrose): Decision Point: Pivotal
      Insight: This constellation — severe tachypnea (likely compensatory for metabolic acidosis,
      i.e., Kussmaul-type breathing), profound hypotension, hypoxia, and hypoglycemia together —
      represents a multi-system crisis requiring simultaneous resuscitation on several fronts
      (glucose, fluids/pressors, oxygen) before the underlying cause could even be fully
      characterized; the hypoglycemia specifically is consistent with depleted glycogen stores
      from prolonged starvation, reinforcing 2a.
  System 1 nodes: Alcohol/starvation history + tachypnea rapidly pattern-matched toward a
  metabolic acidosis process once the ABG became available.
  System 2 nodes: Distinguishing right heart failure due to a primary cardiac/pulmonary cause
  (e.g., pulmonary embolism, given elevated D-dimer) from a nutritional/thiamine-deficiency
  mechanism (wet beriberi) required deliberate analytical work — reflected in the decision to
  pursue CTPA specifically to rule out PE before settling on the beriberi explanation.
  Unfollowed finding: The initial "near-normal" exam (3a) versus the rapid crisis that followed
  is never explicitly discussed as a teaching point in the text — the transition between these
  two states is not narrated (i.e., what exactly happened between the initial exam and the
  "3 hrs ago" sweating/deterioration).

NODE 4 — Investigations
  4a. 2D echo (first, timing implied pre-CTPA): showed right heart failure, D-shaped LV, dilated
      IVC (stated in prose): Decision Point: Pivotal
      Insight: D-shaped LV (septal flattening) with right heart dilation is a classic
      echocardiographic sign of acute right ventricular pressure/volume overload, and is exactly
      the finding that appropriately triggered the next decision point — ruling out pulmonary
      embolism as the cause of acute right heart strain, given this could also represent
      thiamine-deficient wet beriberi (high-output failure) rather than an obstructive/embolic
      process. This is well-reasoned sequential testing.
  4b. D-dimer: elevated (stated in prose, no numeric value given): Decision Point: Bifurcating
      Insight: Elevated D-dimer is nonspecific (can be raised in many acute illness states,
      including sepsis-like presentations and severe metabolic derangement) but was appropriately
      treated as insufficient on its own to confirm PE, prompting definitive CTPA rather than
      empiric anticoagulation — good practice, avoiding premature closure on a PE diagnosis from
      a nonspecific marker alone.
  4c. CTPA: no pulmonary embolism, lung parenchyma normal (stated in prose): Decision Point:
      Closing
      Insight: This decisively closes the pulmonary embolism branch, redirecting the right heart
      failure explanation toward the beriberi/thiamine-deficiency hypothesis — appropriately
      sequenced after the echo raised concern and before committing to a nutritional-deficiency
      explanation.
  4d. ABG: severe metabolic acidosis (stated in prose, no numeric pH/bicarbonate given); urine
      ketones positive (stated in prose): Decision Point: Pivotal
      Insight: Positive urine ketones with severe metabolic acidosis, in the context of alcohol
      binge + starvation (2a) and normoglycemia-to-hypoglycemia (not hyperglycemia, per 3b's GRBS
      61), appropriately supports starvation/alcoholic ketoacidosis over diabetic ketoacidosis —
      the low (not high) glucose is itself a discriminating data point that argues against DKA and

  4e. Review 2D echo (12/5, after treatment): dilated RA, decreased RV dilatation, no D-shaped LV,

      Insight: This follow-up echo showing resolution of the D-shaped LV and improving RV
      dilation after thiamine/fluid treatment is itself a form of therapeutic/diagnostic
      confirmation — the reversal supports the wet beriberi hypothesis retrospectively, since
      thiamine-responsive improvement is a recognized way this diagnosis is confirmed when direct
