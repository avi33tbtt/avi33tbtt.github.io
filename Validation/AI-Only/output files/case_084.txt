CASE 084a — LONG CASE: 38yo male, 8-year history of progressive limb-girdle weakness (suspected muscular dystrophy)
==================================================================

Informant: patient

A 38 year old male, resident of Chandanapally, Nalgonda district, came to the hospital with complaints of difficulty in walking since 8 years.

CHIEF COMPLAINTS:
- Difficulty in walking since 8 years (2014 March)
- Feeling weak during walking since 7 years (2015 January)

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:

Difficulty walking, difficulty getting up from chair without support (can get up with support), difficulty squatting, difficulty sitting on floor, difficulty getting up without support from floor.

Initially difficulty going uphill; since 3 years, also difficulty walking on level ground. Uphill steps difficult; downhill comparatively easier. Difficulty running. No difficulty wearing/holding/removing chappals; in fact finds it easier to walk without chappals due to less weight.

After 8-9 months of symptoms, went to hospital for checkup, given medication (no record available); not on regular medication; medication didn't improve symptoms per patient.

Over time: heaviness of upper limb lifting hand over head, progressing to difficulty lifting arm to shake hands, eat, take a brush from cupboard — needs an increased initial effort to lift the hand. No difficulty combing hair once initiated, but difficulty moving the brush to mouth for tooth brushing. Difficulty lifting food to mouth (not associated with dropping food, no difficulty chewing once food is in mouth). Difficulty bathing with mug — washes more on the right side, difficulty washing left side. Difficulty getting up from bed without support since 1 year; no difficulty turning to sides on bed.

No difficulty eating, chewing, closing eyes, swallowing, whistling, shouting, winking.

Complaints of intermittent muscle spasm after prolonged sitting; muscle cramps.

No difficulty feeling things touched; no difficulty feeling chappal sensation (walks without chappals — reports feet more prone to injury but able to feel pain from injury). No ulcerations or abnormal sensations anywhere. Able to feel water temperature while bathing.

No blurring of vision, smell difficulty, double vision, visual field loss, swallowing/tasting difficulty, facial expression abnormality, hearing difficulty (low or high intensity). No difficulty turning head, eating, drinking.

No loss of consciousness, no light/sound irritability, no memory loss, no hallucinations. Does not use spectacles.

Patient reports feeling bad about losing his job as a watchman due to difficulty working after onset of weakness, but no history of acting out; feels bad about not earning money but has adjusted to complaints over time.

No headaches, nausea, vomiting, involuntary movements.

No loss of balance or falls, but sometimes lacks power to get up from a chair and sits back down.

No urgency, hesitancy, increased nighttime frequency, difficulty initiating urination, burning micturition.

Intermittent difficulty passing stools, relieved with more water intake or a tablet; did not seek medical care for this as it was intermittent and improved with water/bananas.

No fever, sleep disturbances, or history of foot injury.

BIRTH HISTORY:
Second-degree consanguinity in parents. Born at home with a dai (traditional birth attendant), apparently uncomplicated per patient. Walked without support at 3 years, started talking in sentences at 7 years. Stutters when talking (worse with lack of sleep) but no problem with sentence formation, language, or word pronunciation otherwise.

FAMILY HISTORY:
No similar complaints in family. Mother and father died in an accident. Not married (attributed by patient to stuttering initially, then weakness later).

PERSONAL HISTORY:
Smoker for 6 months in 2012, stopped since. Occasional alcoholic — one glass of toddy during festivals. No sleep or appetite abnormalities. No bowel/bladder abnormalities (aside from the intermittent constipation noted above).

PAST HISTORY:
Fall from cycle in 2012, left wrist fracture, did not seek hospital care, took Ayurvedic treatment instead. Now has deformity in left wrist with reduced range of movement and difficulty using the hand.
No known DM, HTN, bronchial asthma, allergies, TB, jaundice, or prolonged hospital stay.

DRUG HISTORY:
No known drug use for more than 1 week; no history of injections in hospital; no known drug allergies.

SUMMARY (as stated in source): Slowly progressive weakness of lower limbs (more proximal than distal), progressing over time to upper limbs (more proximal than distal), with trunk weakness developing over time. No spasticity or rigidity. No sensory complaints. Weakness more in lower limbs than upper limbs. No cerebellar, autonomic, cranial nerve, or higher mental function abnormalities. History of consanguinity, delayed milestones, and malunited left wrist fracture.

GENERAL PHYSICAL EXAMINATION:
- Conscious, coherent, comfortable, cooperative; no distress or pain features; does not appear pale
- No icterus, clubbing, cyanosis, pedal edema, generalized lymphadenopathy
- Weight: 54 kg
- Height: 162 cm
- BMI: 20.57 kg/m²
- BP: 110/70 mmHg
- Hair, nails, skin, spine: normal

SYSTEMIC EXAMINATION — NEUROLOGICAL:

Higher mental functions: conscious, comfortable, normal language/behaviour; normal orientation; normal mood; memory (immediate/recent/remote) normal.
MMSE: orientation 5/5, registration 3/3, attention and calculation 2/5, recall 3/3, total score 25/30.
No illusions/hallucinations. Speech: normal verbal output, fluency, repetition, naming, reading, writing.
Appearance: no tics, tremors, myoclonus, involuntary/voluntary movements.

Motor examination:
- Bulk: UL right 24.5cm above elbow/22cm below elbow; UL left 23.5cm above elbow/22cm below elbow. LL right 43cm above knee/32cm below knee; LL left 43cm above knee/32cm below knee.

Cerebellar examination: hypotonia present; no rebound phenomenon; finger-nose normal; finger-finger normal; heel-shin normal; no past-pointing, intentional tremor, or gait abnormality.

Gait: normal stride, normal width, normal turning; unable to walk on toes.

CASE 084b — SHORT CASE-1: 58yo female, suspected mitral stenosis with heart failure exacerbations
==================================================================

Informant: patient's daughter

A 58 year old woman presented with:
- Shortness of breath with exertion since 1 year, at rest since 15 days
- Cough intermittently since 4 months
- Swelling of both lower limbs on and off since 2 months
- Swelling of right lower limb since 10 days

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:

SOB since 1 year, initially with farm work, insidious onset, progressive, exertional, non-seasonal, now at rest. Worse lying down, relieved sitting/standing.

Cough with expectoration intermittently, associated with worsening chest pain, no fever, no diurnal variation. Expectorant whitish to slightly pinkish, not foul smelling, no plugs, no frank blood.

Bilateral pedal edema on and off since 2 months, pitting, extending to ankles, equal both sides.

Not associated with chest pain, dizziness, loss of consciousness, palpitations.
Not associated with fever, weight loss.
Associated with increased urinary frequency since 4 months.

PAST HISTORY:
No similar complaints before 1 year. Hospitalized 3 times in the past year, each time with worsening SOB, pedal edema, cough. Attenders reported on-and-off medication adherence each time.
No DM, HTN, bronchial asthma, TB, jaundice. No known drug allergies.

FAMILY HISTORY:
No similar complaints in family; no history of sudden cardiac death in family.

GENERAL PHYSICAL EXAMINATION:
- Conscious, cooperative, dyspnoeic at rest
- Pulse: 86 bpm, regular, low volume, equal on both sides and all peripheral areas, no radio-radial or radio-femoral delay
- BP: 120/60 mmHg
- JVP: engorged vein with pulsation; hepatojugular reflex present
- RR: 24 cpm
- SpO2: 96% on room air
- Pallor present; no icterus, cyanosis, clubbing, lymphadenopathy
- Pedal edema present, bilateral pitting, extending to ankles

CARDIOVASCULAR EXAMINATION:

Inspection: no precordial deformity/bulge; apical impulse seen in 6th ICS 1cm lateral to mid-clavicular line; no diffuse precordial pulsations; no superficial engorged veins; no scars/sinuses. Pulsations seen in right parasternal region and epigastrium. No prominent aortic/suprasternal/supraclavicular pulsations; no visible carotid or back pulsations. No kyphosis, scoliosis, shoulder drooping, scapular winging.

Palpation: apex beat in 6th ICS, left side, 1cm lateral to mid-clavicular line, spread over 2 ICS. Parasternal heave present right parasternal region, obliterated on pressure. Palpable P2 in pulmonary area, no palpable thrill there. No other palpable heart sounds, no carotid thrill, no superficial veins.

Percussion: right heart border — dullness to 2.5cm lateral to sternal border; other borders not well localized.

Auscultation:
- Cardiac rate ~87 bpm, regular rhythm
- Mitral area: soft S1, diastolic murmur (mid-to-late, low-pitched), no presystolic accentuation, better heard in left lateral position, no radiation. Difficult to appreciate on initial presentation, more audible after initial management.
- Pulmonary area: loud P2, no murmur, no added sounds

Moderate to severe mitral stenosis with frequent acute exacerbations of heart failure.

CASE 084c — SHORT CASE-2: 54yo male, suspected left-sided pleural effusion secondary to infection
==================================================================

Informant: patient

A 54 year old male patient came with pain in the left side of the chest radiating to the back since 8 days, difficulty in breathing since two days.

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:

Patient apparently asymptomatic 8 days back, then developed pain in the left side of chest, stabbing type, increased on inspiration, radiating to left upper back. Associated with difficulty breathing (pain on inspiration), progressing over time, worse with intermittent cough.

Not associated with high grade fever, chills.
No SOB before 8 days.
No palpitations, orthopnoea, PND, headache, burning micturition, vomiting, loose stools, cough, fever (beyond above).
No headache, tingling, numbness.
No decreased urine output.

History of burning sensation of both feet since 1 year, associated with tingling of both lower limbs extending to ankle, equal both sides, difficulty feeling chappals while walking.

PAST HISTORY:
Reduced vision in right eye post-trauma; also sustained a leg injury during that trauma, after which started using a stick to walk.
Operated 10 years back for a scrotal mass (? inguinal hernia).
No HTN, DM, epilepsy, bronchial asthma, CAD.

FAMILY HISTORY: no similar complaints in family.

PERSONAL HISTORY:
Chronic alcoholic, ~180ml/day, 4-5 days/week. Not a smoker. Married, 2 kids.

GENERAL PHYSICAL EXAMINATION:
- Sitting position, conscious, coherent, cooperative
- Febrile to touch: 99.3°F
- Pulse: 98 bpm, regular, normal volume, no radio-radial or radio-femoral delay
- BP: 120/100 mmHg
- No pallor, icterus, clubbing, lymphadenopathy, pedal edema
- Spine normal

RESPIRATORY EXAMINATION:

Upper respiratory tract: nose, septum, sinuses, oral cavity, pharyngeal cavity normal; no mouth breathing.

Lower respiratory tract:

Inspection: elliptical shape; chest movements slightly reduced on left; apical impulse not visible; abdominal-type respiration; ~19 cpm; no visible tracheal deviation; no visible veins/pulsations; no scars/marks/sinuses; intercostal fullness present on left, localized to lower half; no accessory muscle usage; normal nipples/muscles; no audible sounds; no shoulder/spine abnormality.

Palpation: normal surface temperature; no local tenderness; no tracheal deviation; reduced chest movement on left; no spinal/shoulder deformity; asymmetrical chest expansion, reduced on left; reduced vocal fremitus in left infrascapular and left infra-axillary regions.

Percussion: stony dull note in left infrascapular and left infra-axillary regions; normal resonant elsewhere.

Auscultation: bilateral air entry present, equal sounds both sides; normal vesicular breath sounds in right infraclavicular, clavicular, supraclavicular, mammary, scapular, suprascapular, infrascapular, interscapular regions. Reduced breath sounds in left infrascapular and left infra-axillary regions. Reduced vocal resonance in left infra-axillary and infrascapular regions.

PROVISIONAL DIAGNOSIS:
Left-sided pleural effusion, probably secondary to infection.

INVESTIGATIONS:
- Total leukocyte count: 18,000/cumm
- Hemoglobin: 14 g/dl
- Platelets: 2.89 lakh/cumm
