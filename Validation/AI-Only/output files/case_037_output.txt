DIAGNOSTIC REASONING MAP — Case 37: 48F with known DM2, DKA secondary to pneumonia (?PCP/?viral), severe metabolic acidosis with shock requiring vasopressors
Date: 2026-08-17
Learner: N/A
Module: DRM — Diagnostic Reasoning Map

NODE 1 — Presenting Complaint
  Finding: SOB grade 4 since 3:30pm same day, uneasiness since 3:30pm, on-and-off suprapubic pain x1 week, low-grade fever with chills since morning of admission day.
  Decision Point: Yes — Type: Bifurcating
  Insight: Acute-onset severe dyspnea in a known diabetic, preceded by a week of low-grade suprapubic pain and same-day fever, should immediately activate two intertwined hypotheses — a metabolic decompensation (DKA, given known DM2) and an infective trigger for that decompensation (urinary vs. respiratory source) — the presenting complaint alone should prevent premature closure on either "DKA" or "pneumonia" in isolation, since the two are very likely causally linked here.

NODE 2 — History
  2a. Suprapubic tenderness on and off x1 week, radiating to right and left lumbar areas, without burning micturition or fever at that time: Decision Point: Bifurcating — Insight: Bilateral lumbar radiation from suprapubic pain without classic lower-urinary-tract irritative symptoms (burning micturition) makes a straightforward cystitis less certain and should keep pyelonephritis or another intra-abdominal/gynecological source in the differential, though the later urinalysis findings and clinical course ultimately point toward a pulmonary rather than urinary infective source.
  2b. No oral intake since 11:00am on the day of the acute deterioration, developing SOB grade 4 by 3:30pm, diagnosed as DKA at an outside facility (Nalgonda) and started on IV fluids and an insulin infusion before referral: Decision Point: Pivotal — Insight: The temporal sequence — several hours of poor oral intake (plausibly due to underlying illness/anorexia) culminating in a metabolic crisis — is classic for DKA precipitated by an intercurrent illness in a person with known diabetes; the outside facility's prompt initiation of fluids and insulin represents an appropriately early intervention on the metabolic derangement, even before the underlying trigger was identified.
  2c. History of RTA-related lumbar vertebral fracture with spine surgery in 2016, at which time DM2 was diagnosed and started on metformin: Decision Point: Missed — Insight: DM2 diagnosed incidentally during a trauma admission 5 years prior is a notable detail — it is unclear from the case how well-controlled her diabetes has been in the interim (no HbA1c trend, no mention of insulin use prior to this admission), and this gap in the chronic disease history limits the ability to judge whether this DKA episode reflects a first major decompensation or a recurring pattern.
  2d. Not a known case of HTN, CAD, CVA: Decision Point: Closing — Insight: Absence of these comorbidities somewhat lowers (though does not eliminate) the pretest probability of a primary cardiac event as the cause of her SOB, supporting a metabolic/respiratory explanation, though this is later actively investigated rather than assumed (troponin and echo were both obtained).
  Missing history: No explicit travel, sick-contact, or immunosuppression history is documented despite the eventual differential explicitly including PCP (Pneumocystis pneumonia), which classically occurs in immunocompromised hosts — no HIV status or other immunosuppression risk factor is recorded anywhere in the case; no documented smoking history; no clarification of how many days the suprapubic pain preceded the current fever by exactly, versus how many days of true systemic illness preceded the DKA crisis.

NODE 3 — Examination
  3a. Tachypneic, afebrile at initial presentation, RR 32, PR 123, BP 110/70; RS showing coarse end-inspiratory crepitations in the left infra-axillary/infra-scapular areas with decreased breath sounds in the left inter-mammary area: Decision Point: Pivotal — Insight: Focal left-sided crepitations with regional decreased breath sounds strongly localizes a pulmonary process to the left lung, appropriately directing the subsequent imaging (HRCT) toward confirming and characterizing a pneumonic process as the likely DKA trigger, rather than defaulting to a purely metabolic or urinary explanation.
  3b. CVS S1S2 normal, abdomen soft/non-tender with bowel sounds present, CNS HMF intact with no focal deficit: Decision Point: Closing — Insight: A normal cardiac and neurological exam at presentation argues against a primary cardiac event or CNS catastrophe as the driver of her tachypnea/tachycardia, and a benign abdominal exam somewhat de-emphasizes (without excluding) the suprapubic pain as a major ongoing acute process, redirecting attention appropriately toward the respiratory and metabolic axes.
  System 1 nodes: known diabetic + severe dyspnea + prior DKA diagnosis at outside facility → DKA (rapid, appropriately confirmed pattern match).
  System 2 nodes: localizing focal left-sided lung findings on exam, then deliberately pursuing HRCT to characterize a "non-resolving tachypnea and opacities" (as explicitly documented) reflects genuine analytical escalation rather than passive acceptance of an outside diagnosis — the clinical team is shown actively working to identify why the DKA occurred, not just treating the DKA itself.

NODE 4 — Investigations
  4a. HbA1c 6.8%: Decision Point: Bifurcating — Insight: An HbA1c in this range suggests reasonably controlled glycemia over the preceding ~3 months, which is somewhat inconsistent with a picture of severe, longstanding poor control — this supports the interpretation that this DKA episode was precipitated by an acute intercurrent illness (infection) superimposed on previously reasonable glycemic control, rather than reflecting chronic, severe diabetic mismanagement.
  4b. Troponin I negative; LDH 506 IU/L (elevated): Decision Point: Closing/Bifurcating — Insight: A negative troponin reasonably excludes acute myocardial injury as a primary explanation for the tachycardia/dyspnea; the elevated LDH is nonspecific but is compatible with tissue stress/hypoxia from severe illness, and in the context of the eventual PCP-related differential, an elevated LDH is a recognized (though nonspecific) supportive marker sometimes associated with PCP.
  4c. HRCT chest: peribronchial, predominantly centrally-placed ground-glass opacities in both lungs, with differentials of infective etiology (PCP/COVID-19 pneumonia) or acute interstitial pneumonia; CORADS III; CT severity score 24/25: Decision Point: Pivotal — Insight: Bilateral, central, ground-glass-predominant opacities with this specific differential (PCP vs. COVID vs. acute interstitial pneumonia) and a very high severity score is the single most important imaging-driven decision point in the case — it correctly reframes the working problem from "isolated left-sided pneumonia" (suggested by the focal exam finding) to a severe, bilateral, atypical pneumonic process, which is a more diagnostically and prognostically significant finding than the initial exam alone suggested.
  4d. Hemogram: Hb 14.5, TLC 27,400 (markedly elevated) with neutrophilic predominance (N87, Lym10), platelets 5.8 lakh (elevated): Decision Point: Pivotal — Insight: A markedly elevated, neutrophil-predominant white cell count with reactive thrombocytosis is strongly consistent with a significant bacterial or severe systemic infective/inflammatory process, supporting the working "DKA secondary to sepsis/pneumonia" framework, though a neutrophilic leukocytosis of this magnitude is somewhat atypical for a purely viral or PCP-type pneumonia and could suggest a bacterial co-infection or a bacterial process being the actual/additional driver.
  4e. Urine ketones positive; urine albumin+++, sugars+++, pus cells 2-3, epithelial cells 2-3, RBCs 2-3; spot urine protein/creatinine ratio 1.19 (protein 31.5, creatinine 26.5): Decision Point: Bifurcating — Insight: Positive urine ketones confirm the DKA state biochemically; the modest pyuria (2-3 pus cells) is not striking enough to strongly confirm a urinary source for the systemic infection despite the suprapubic pain history, while the significant proteinuria (protein/creatinine ratio 1.19, i.e., roughly nephrotic-range by some conventions) is a notable and under-discussed finding that could reflect acute kidney stress from severe DKA/shock, an underlying diabetic nephropathy, or a separate renal process — the case does not explicitly address this proteinuria finding.
  4f. Serial ABGs from 21/02 9:30pm (pH 6.77, HCO3 3.8, anion gap 38.3) through 23/02 2:00am (pH 7.42, HCO3 11.7): Decision Point: Pivotal — Insight: The initial ABG (pH 6.77) represents an extremely severe, life-threatening metabolic acidosis with a markedly elevated anion gap, and the serial trend shows a slow, staged correction over roughly 30 hours with an intervening bicarbonate correction — this trajectory is the clearest quantitative evidence of both the severity of the initial presentation and the appropriateness/effectiveness of the aggressive, closely-monitored management that followed.
  4g. Cardiology opinion and screening echo showing mild LV dysfunction and LV dilatation: Decision Point: Bifurcating — Insight: New/incidental mild LV dysfunction and dilatation found on screening echo is a notable finding whose relationship to the primary presentation (new stress cardiomyopathy from severe illness vs. a pre-existing but previously undiagnosed cardiac process) is not explicitly resolved in the case, despite prompting specific management changes (Lasix, head-end elevation).
  4h. Sputum, urine, and blood cultures sent; sputum CBNAAT/AFB sent; results not documented in the extracted text: Decision Point: Missed — Insight: These are the investigations most likely to definitively identify the causative organism (confirming or refuting PCP, bacterial pneumonia, or tuberculosis), and their absence from the visible record leaves the final etiology formally unconfirmed by microbiology within this entry, despite the diagnosis eventually being updated to name PCP specifically by Day 5.
  Unused/unclear results: The significant proteinuria (protein/creatinine ratio 1.19) is documented but never discussed or acted upon in the treatment plan; the LV dysfunction/dilatation finding is acted upon (diuretic, head-end elevation) but its underlying cause is never resolved in the text.
  Missing investigations: Explicit culture/CBNAAT results; HIV status (highly relevant given PCP was ultimately named as the causative diagnosis, since PCP is classically an AIDS-defining or otherwise immunocompromise-associated infection); a follow-up echo to determine whether the LV dysfunction was transient (stress-related) or persistent.

NODE 5 — Diagnostic Synthesis
  Working diagnosis reached: Progressed from "DKA secondary to ?sepsis" (admission) → "DKA secondary to pneumonia ?viral ?PSP [sic, presumably PCP] with K/C/O DM2 with K/C/O spine surgery 5 years back" (Days 1-4) → "DKA (resolved) secondary to PCP with K/C/O DM2" (Day 5).
  Closure type: Analytical closure achieved iteratively over 5 days — the diagnosis was appropriately left as a qualified differential ("?viral ?PSP") for most of the admission while imaging, labs, and specialist input (cardiology, pulmonology) were gathered, and only committed to a specific organism (PCP) by Day 5, once the patient's clinical trajectory (resolving SOP, weaning vasopressors) supported it.
  Open branches at closure: The specific microbiological confirmation of PCP (or exclusion of bacterial pneumonia/COVID/other viral causes) is never shown in the visible text — the Day 5 diagnosis names PCP definitively, but the reasoning bridge from "differential includes PCP" (Day 1, from HRCT) to "PCP" (Day 5, as the settled diagnosis) is not documented, meaning this DRM cannot confirm whether this reflects a positive confirmatory test (e.g., PCR, immunofluorescence, or beta-D-glucan) or a presumptive clinical diagnosis based on radiologic pattern and treatment response; the significant proteinuria and LV dysfunction findings remain unexplained branches throughout.
  Synthesis insight: This is a well-managed, high-acuity case with an appropriately staged diagnostic and therapeutic response (aggressive DKA correction, escalating respiratory support, empiric broad coverage including antibacterial, antiviral (Tamiflu), and anti-PCP (Bactrim-DS) therapy, and vasopressor support for the associated shock) — the main critique is that the case never explicitly documents the confirmatory evidence that finally settled on PCP as the causative organism, despite empirically treating for multiple possible pathogens simultaneously throughout (a broad-coverage strategy that is clinically defensible in a critically ill patient but makes retrospective etiologic attribution difficult from the documentation alone).

BIAS AUDIT OVERLAY
  Anchoring: Node 1 — the outside facility's initial "DKA" diagnosis (without an identified trigger) could have anchored the receiving team to treat DKA as the primary problem; to their credit, the team explicitly and promptly pursued the underlying trigger (HRCT, cardiology and pulmonology consults) rather than stopping at the DKA label alone.
  Availability: Node 4c — given the case's 2021 timeframe (February 2021, during the COVID-19 pandemic), COVID-19 pneumonia would have been a highly "available" diagnosis for bilateral ground-glass opacities; the case appropriately lists COVID-19 as a differential (with a CORADS III severity designation, which itself reflects COVID-specific reporting conventions) but does not appear to have let this availability bias override consideration of PCP and other differentials.
  Premature closure: Node 5 — the transition from a qualified "?viral ?PSP" differential to a definitive "PCP" diagnosis by Day 5 is not accompanied by explicit confirmatory evidence in the visible text, which is a soft form of closure without full documentation of its justification (though this could simply reflect an editing/transcription gap in the blog rather than an actual clinical reasoning failure).
  Framing: Node 1 — the blog's own header framing ("46 years old female with C/O SOB" in the blog title bar, versus "48year old female" in the post title itself) contains an internal age inconsistency, a documentation-level issue rather than a clinical reasoning bias, but worth flagging.
  Search satisficing/Commission bias: Node 4h — extremely broad empiric coverage (antibacterial, antiviral, and anti-PCP therapy simultaneously) was maintained for the duration of the admission without the case showing culture/PCR results being used to narrow (de-escalate) therapy — this is a recognized and often necessary strategy in critically ill patients, but it also means the case does not demonstrate an explicit narrowing/de-escalation step, which would be the expected next stage of antimicrobial stewardship-oriented reasoning.

HIGHEST-YIELD DECISION POINT
  Node: 4c/4h — the HRCT differential (PCP/COVID/acute interstitial pneumonia) and the subsequent absence of documented confirmatory microbiology before the diagnosis was finalized as PCP.
  Why: This is the single point in the case with the greatest transferable teaching value: severe bilateral ground-glass pneumonia in a diabetic patient with an unclear immune status should trigger an explicit, urgent question — "why does this patient have an opportunistic-pattern pneumonia, and should her immune status be formally investigated (e.g., HIV testing)?" — before settling on PCP as a final diagnosis. The broader principle: when treatment empirically covers multiple possible pathogens simultaneously (as was clinically appropriate here given severity), the reasoning pathway should still explicitly plan for and document the confirmatory test that will allow diagnosis-specific narrowing, rather than allowing the initial working differential to silently become the final diagnosis by default.
