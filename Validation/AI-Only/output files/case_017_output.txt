DIAGNOSTIC REASONING MAP — Case_017 (55M, k/c/o HTN+DM, acute Rt UL/LL weakness + numbness +
facial deviation)
Module: DRM — Diagnostic Reasoning Map

NODE 1 — Presenting Complaint
  Finding: 55M, sudden-onset (on getting up from bed) right upper and lower limb weakness with
  tingling/numbness x1 day, acute onset non-progressive, with deviation of mouth to left; known
  HTN x8 years (on Telma-AM 40/5) and DM x3 months (on Glimi-M1)
  Decision Point: Yes — Type: Pivotal
  Insight: Sudden-onset unilateral (right-sided) weakness with facial deviation, occurring on
  waking, in a patient with established vascular risk factors (long-standing HTN, newly
  diagnosed DM), is a textbook acute ischemic stroke presentation — the semantic qualifier set
  (acute, non-progressive, unilateral, with facial involvement) combined with known risk factors
  should activate "acute stroke, likely ischemic, wake-up stroke" in an experienced clinician's
  pattern library immediately, with "wake-up stroke" specifically raising a time-of-onset
  ambiguity relevant to thrombolysis eligibility.

NODE 2 — History
  2a. "Wake-up" timing (symptoms noticed after getting up from bed): Decision Point: Missed
      Insight: Wake-up strokes carry an inherently uncertain true onset time (last-known-well may
      be hours before symptom recognition), which is a critical decision point for acute stroke
      management (affecting eligibility for time-sensitive interventions) — the case narrative
      does not show this ambiguity being explicitly addressed or discussed despite its clinical
      importance.
  2b. Preserved complex motor tasks (combing hair, buttoning/unbuttoning, climbing stairs, rolling
      over in bed) but slippage of chappal (sandal) present: Decision Point: Bifurcating
      Insight: This is an unusually granular and useful functional history — the pattern of
      preserved fine proximal/bilateral coordination tasks but lost distal foot control
      (chappal slippage) suggests the weakness, while present, may be relatively mild-to-moderate
      rather than a dense hemiplegia, which is consistent with the exam grading (4/5) documented
      later, and this functional detail usefully calibrates severity before formal exam.
  2c. No LOC, no seizures, no dysphagia, no bowel/bladder abnormality, no fever/trauma/vomiting/
      diplopia: Decision Point: Closing
      Insight: Absence of these features reduces concern for a more extensive or brainstem-
      involving process, a seizure-related (Todd's paresis) etiology, or a systemic infectious/
      traumatic cause, supporting a discrete vascular event as the leading explanation.
  2d. HTN x8 years on treatment; DM x3 months, recently diagnosed and on treatment:
      Decision Point: Pivotal
      Insight: Long-standing hypertension is the single strongest chronic risk factor present,
      and the very recent DM diagnosis (3 months) raises the question of how well-controlled the
      diabetes is and whether it has already contributed measurably to vascular risk — both
      history items appropriately anchor the differential toward an atherothrombotic/small-vessel
      ischemic mechanism.
  Single most important history: 2a (the wake-up timing) — this is the piece of history most
  likely to have changed real-time management (thrombolysis window calculation) and is not shown
  being explicitly reasoned through in the case as written.
  Missing history: No documented exact time patient went to bed vs. time symptoms were noticed
  (needed to estimate last-known-well); no lipid history or prior stroke/TIA history; no
  medication adherence detail for the 8-year HTN treatment (relevant to whether "known,
  treated HTN" was actually well-controlled).

NODE 3 — Examination
  3a. BP 140/80, PR 78, pedal edema+: Decision Point: Missed
      Insight: Pedal edema in this patient is documented but never explained or connected to any
      diagnosis — it doesn't fit cleanly with an isolated acute stroke picture and could reflect

  3b. Motor exam: Right UL power 5/5, Right LL power 4/5 (left side 5/5 throughout); tone normal
      bilaterally: Decision Point: Pivotal
      Insight: The exam reveals a more limited/subtle deficit than the presenting complaint's
      "weakness of right upper limb and lower limb" would suggest — the right upper limb power is
      actually documented as full (5/5), with only the right lower limb showing reduction (4/5).
      This is an important internal inconsistency between the stated chief complaint (bilateral
      limb weakness) and the formal exam findings (weakness essentially confined to the right
      leg) that the DRM should flag explicitly rather than let pass silently — it may reflect
      resolving symptoms, exam timing, or a milder deficit than initially perceived by the
      patient/history-taker.
  3c. Reflexes: Biceps 3+/2+ (Rt/Lt), Triceps 2+/2+, Supinator 2+/2+, Knee 3+/3+, Ankle 1+/1+,
      Plantar "?E"/F (right plantar response uncertain/equivocal-extensor, left flexor):
      Decision Point: Pivotal
      Insight: An exaggerated right biceps reflex (3+ vs. 2+ on the left) combined with an
      equivocal/possibly extensor right plantar response is classic evidence of an upper motor
      neuron lesion on the right side (corresponding to the left cerebral hemisphere, given
      crossed motor pathways) — this is the single most specific localizing finding in the exam
      and correctly supports a left-hemisphere ischemic process, consistent with the described
      right-sided weakness and left-deviating mouth.
  3d. Sensory system intact; no cerebellar or meningeal signs: Decision Point: Closing
      Insight: A pure or predominantly motor deficit with intact sensation and no cerebellar
      involvement further supports a discrete small-vessel/lacunar-type ischemic lesion (e.g.,
      pure motor hemiparesis syndrome) rather than a larger territory or hemorrhagic process with
      broader deficits.

  pattern-matched to acute ischemic stroke.
  System 2 nodes: Reconciling the mismatch between the presenting complaint (bilateral UL+LL
