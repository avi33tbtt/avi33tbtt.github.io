DIAGNOSTIC REASONING MAP — 85-year-old man with shortness of breath, pedal edema, and decreased urine output
Module: DRM — Diagnostic Reasoning Map

NODE 1 — Presenting Complaint
  Finding: Pedal edema x7 days, shortness of breath x7 days, abdominal distension x7 days, decreased urine output x7 days — on a background of on-and-off pedal edema for 1 year.
  Decision Point: Yes — Type: Bifurcating
  Insight: A tetrad of edema, dyspnoea, abdominal distension, and oliguria appearing together over the same 7-day window strongly suggests a single fluid-overload/congestive process, but the differential must still split early between cardiac (heart failure), renal (AKI/CKD with volume overload), and hepatic (cirrhosis with ascites) causes of that congestive picture before converging.

NODE 2 — History
  2a. On-and-off pedal edema for 1 year, now progressed to thigh level (grade III) over 7 days: Decision Point: Type Pivotal — Insight: A chronic, relapsing edema pattern that has now acutely worsened suggests an underlying chronic cardiac, renal, or hepatic condition that has recently decompensated, rather than a new acute process; this should have prompted specific inquiry into a prior diagnosis or workup for the 1-year history, which is not documented.
  2b. SOB since 7 days, insidious, progressed grade I→II, worse on exertion, relieved by rest, associated with wheeze; no orthopnoea, no PND, no postural variation: Decision Point: Type Bifurcating — Insight: Exertional dyspnoea with wheeze but without orthopnoea or PND is a somewhat atypical pattern for classic left heart failure (which usually favours orthopnoea/PND), keeping a pulmonary or mixed cardiopulmonary contributor live in the differential alongside heart failure.
  2c. Decreased urine output x7 days, abdominal distension x7 days: Decision Point: Type Bifurcating — Insight: Oliguria appearing simultaneously with edema and distension could represent either a cardiorenal mechanism (reduced renal perfusion from a failing heart) or a primary renal process; the text does not document renal function trend or fluid balance data that would distinguish these.
  2d. No fever, cough, burning micturition, chest pain, palpitations, loose stools/vomiting, no loss of appetite or weight: Decision Point: Type Closing — Insight: Absence of chest pain lowers (but does not exclude, especially in an 85-year-old, where silent ischaemia is common) the probability of an acute coronary event as the direct trigger; absence of fever and urinary symptoms lowers probability of an infective precipitant.
  2e. Not a known case of DM, HTN, CVA, CAD, TB, asthma, epilepsy: Decision Point: Type Bifurcating — Insight: The absence of a documented prior CAD/HTN diagnosis is notable given the provisional diagnosis ultimately reached ("heart failure with preserved ejection fraction secondary to CAD") — this is a point where the map shows a diagnostic leap not fully supported by the stated past history, and is flagged for the auditor rather than assumed to be an omission in the source.
  Single most important history feature: 2a (the 1-year history of relapsing pedal edema) — it reframes the current 7-day acute presentation as a decompensation of a pre-existing, undiagnosed chronic process rather than a de novo illness, and the source text does not show this being pursued (e.g., no reported prior diagnostic evaluation for the 1-year symptom).
  Missing history: No documented alcohol history (relevant given a differential of ascites/hepatic causes for abdominal distension), no smoking history, no medication history (e.g., prior diuretic use, NSAID use), and no clarification of why CAD is invoked in the provisional diagnosis when no past history of CAD is stated.

  3b. Grade III pedal edema, no cyanosis/clubbing/generalised lymphadenopathy: Decision Point: Type Closing — Insight: Absence of clubbing and lymphadenopathy lowers probability of chronic hypoxic lung disease or lymphoproliferative/infiltrative disease as primary drivers of the presentation.
  3c.
