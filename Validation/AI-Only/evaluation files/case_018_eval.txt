HEADLINE FINDING — VITALS OMITTED FROM THE EXAMINATION NODE (not a
fabrication, but a real completeness gap with a missed clinical tension)
================================================================
The source's O/E section states full vitals: **"temp-afebrile, pulse-82bpm,
BP-130/80mmHg, Resp rate-16 cpm, spo2-97%."** None of these five values
appear anywhere in the DRM output's Node 3 (Examination) — the node jumps
directly from the negative general-exam findings (pallor/icterus/etc.) to
the respiratory-system findings (fremitus, percussion, breath sounds)
without ever stating a single vital sign.

This matters for two reasons:
  1. The patient's fever is a major presenting complaint (x10 days, high-
     grade, intermittent, with chills/rigors) — the fact that he was
     **afebrile at the time of this exam** is a real, source-stated data
     point directly relevant to Node 1's own characterization of the fever
     as "intermittent, relieved on medication." This would have been
     genuinely corroborating evidence worth citing, and it's missing.
  2. More significantly: the patient's chief complaint includes **SOB Grade
     3** (a substantial subjective dyspnea severity), yet SpO2 was **97% on
     room air** and respiratory rate was **16** (normal) — this is a real
     tension between the severity of the subjective complaint and the
     objective vitals that the DRM output does not surface at all. This is
     the same category of "complaint vs. objective finding" mismatch that
     this same DRM generation process caught explicitly and well in
     case_017 (bilateral-complaint vs. unilateral-exam-finding). Here, the
     analogous mismatch exists in the source but was missed entirely,
     apparently because the vitals were never brought into the node.
This is not a fabrication — nothing invented — but it's a more consequential
omission than most flagged so far, because it caused a real, source-
supported clinical inconsistency to go unnoticed rather than just leaving a
background detail unmentioned.
