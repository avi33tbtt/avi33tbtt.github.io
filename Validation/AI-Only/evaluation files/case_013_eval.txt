NODE-BY-NODE SCORING (rubric, Section 4)

NODE 1 — Presenting Complaint
  Decision-point classification accuracy: 2/2 — "Pivotal" is well-justified;
    a sudden focal deficit with transient speech involvement in a young adult
    is a genuine stroke-alarm presentation.
  Insight correctness: 1/2 — mostly correct, but contains a timing misread.
    The DRM states numbness (7pm) was "followed within 5 minutes by left
    lower limb weakness progressing to left upper limb weakness." The source
    sentence — "he developed numbness of left upper limb and lower limb at
    7pm and then he developed weakness of left lower limb which gradually
    progressed to left upper limb within 5min" — more naturally reads as the
    5-minute window describing the LL-to-UL weakness progression, not the
    gap between numbness onset and weakness onset. The DRM's phrasing
    conflates these two intervals. The underlying source sentence is itself
    a little ambiguous, so this isn't a clean fabrication, but it's a
    specific, confidently-stated temporal claim that overstates what the
    text actually pins down — relevant here because stroke timelines
    (thrombolysis window) are exactly the kind of detail that should be
    read conservatively, not smoothed over.
  Fabrication check: No — not an invented event, a reasonable-but-imprecise
    reading of a genuinely ambiguous source sentence.

NODE 2 — History
  Decision-point classification accuracy: 2/2 — negative ROS (2a), family
    history of DM/HTN with patient not a known case himself (2b), and the
    alcohol/smoking history (2c) all trace to real source lines.
  Insight correctness: 1/2 — the "single most important history" line states
    the patient has been drinking and smoking "since age 13, i.e., 18 years
    by age 31" as if this were a directly stated fact. The source only says
    "since 18yrs" / "since 18 years," which — consistent with this blog's own
    phrasing convention elsewhere in the corpus (e.g., case_011's "alcoholic
    since 7 years") — most plausibly means an 18-year duration, making the
    age-13 start a reasonable derived inference. But it is presented flatly
    ("alcohol + smoking since age 13") rather than flagged as a calculated
    inference from an ambiguous duration phrase, which risks reading as a
    more precise fact than the source actually supports. This is the kind of
    unflagged inference the fabrication check is meant to catch even when
    the underlying arithmetic is reasonable.
  Fabrication check: No — the source does state an 18-year duration; the
    age-13 figure is derived, not invented, but should have been hedged.
  Missed-data flag accuracy: 2/2 — the stated "Missing history" list (no
    lipid/glucose screening history, no clear pre-hospital symptom timeline
    for thrombolysis-window purposes) is accurate and clinically relevant.

NODE 3 — Examination

    truncal obesity, acanthosis nigricans, and "hesitation cuts" all match

  Fabrication check: No.
  Missed-data flag / unfollowed finding: 2/2 — correctly identifies

================================================================
FABRICATION CHECK (case-level)
================================================================
Fabrication flag: No.
Every specific value checked (vitals, BMI/height/weight, full motor and
reflex table, final diagnosis wording) matches the source exactly, and the
image-only investigations are correctly withheld rather than inferred. No
node introduces a finding, value, or event absent from the source.

Two accuracy-adjacent issues are worth flagging (neither rises to
fabrication, but both are the kind of imprecision the rubric's fabrication
check is meant to surface):
  1. Node 1 conflates two different time intervals in the HOPI sentence
     (numbness→weakness gap vs. LL→UL weakness progression), stating a more
     precise timeline than the source actually supports — notable because
     stroke timelines carry real clinical weight (thrombolysis window).
  2. Node 2 presents a derived figure ("since age 13") as if directly stated,
     when the source only gives a duration ("since 18 years") that requires
     an inference to convert to an age. The inference is reasonable and
     consistent with this blog's own phrasing conventions elsewhere in the
     corpus, but it is not the same epistemic status as a source-stated fact
     and should have been hedged accordingly.
Both are precision/hedging issues rather than invented content, but both
also represent a case where the DRM output was less careful about flagging
its own inferences than it was in flagging the alcohol-duration ambiguity in
case_011 — a pattern worth tracking across further cases.
